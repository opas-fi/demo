Sura 2: Al-Baqara, The Heifer

Verse 1

بِسْمِ اللَّهِ الرَّحْمَـٰنِ الرَّحِيمِ الم
Translation: Alif-Lãm-Mĩm.
Reflection: This verse consists of three Arabic letters. In Islamic belief, these letters, which begin several chapters of the Qur’an, are part of the divine revelation. While their exact meaning is known only to Allah, one perspective is that they highlight that the Qur’an is composed of the same letters used in the Arabic language known to its first audience. Despite this, the Qur’an is considered a miraculous and unparalleled work that humans cannot replicate, challenging anyone to produce something similar.

Verse 2

ذَٰلِكَ الْكِتَابُ لَا رَيْبَ ۛ فِيهِ ۛ هُدًى لِّلْمُتَّقِينَ
Translation: This is the Book in which there is no doubt—a guidance for those who are mindful (of Allah),
Reflection: Following the opening letters, this verse declares that the Qur’an is a Book of certain guidance. It is not for everyone indiscriminately, but specifically for those who are mindful of Allah (often translated as "fear Allah"). Being mindful of Allah means having a sensitive conscience, being alert to life's challenges and temptations, and striving to stay on the right path, much like carefully navigating a thorny road to avoid harm. This state of mind makes one receptive to the Qur’an's light and wisdom.

Verse 3

الَّذِينَ يُؤْمِنُونَ بِالْغَيْبِ وَيُقِيمُونَ الصَّلَاةَ وَمِمَّا رَزَقْنَاهُمْ يُنفِقُونَ
Translation: who believe in the unseen, establish prayer, and give (in charity) from what We have provided for them,
Reflection: This verse begins a description of the qualities of those who benefit from the Qur’an's guidance mentioned in the previous verse. The first quality is belief in the Unseen – matters beyond normal human perception, such as Allah Himself, angels, and the Hereafter. This belief elevates human consciousness beyond the purely physical world. The verse also mentions establishing prayer, which connects the believer directly to Allah, and spending charitably from what Allah has provided. This spending is seen as acknowledging that wealth is a trust from Allah and fosters compassion and community.

Verse 4

وَالَّذِينَ يُؤْمِنُونَ بِمَا أُنزِلَ إِلَيْكَ وَمَا أُنزِلَ مِن قَبْلِكَ وَبِالْآخِرَةِ هُمْ يُوقِنُونَ
Translation: and who believe in what has been revealed to you (O Muhammad) and what was revealed before you, and who are certain (in faith) about the Hereafter.
Reflection: Continuing the description from Verse 3, these verses describe the believers as those who accept the revelation given to Prophet Muhammad and all previous divine revelations sent to prophets before him. This highlights the Islamic belief in the unity of Allah's message throughout history. Furthermore, they have certainty in the Hereafter (the life to come), which provides a sense of purpose beyond this world and assurance of ultimate justice.

Verse 5

أُولَـٰئِكَ عَلَىٰ هُدًى مِّن رَّبِّهِمْ ۖ وَأُولَـٰئِكَ هُمُ الْمُفْلِحُونَ
Translation: It is they who are truly guided by their Lord, and it is they who will be successful.
Reflection: This verse concludes the description of the believers, affirming that because they possess the qualities mentioned in the preceding verses (3 and 4), they are indeed on true guidance from Allah. It states that it is these people who will ultimately be successful and prosperous. This verse serves as a direct consequence of the description of the true believers.

Verse 6

إِنَّ الَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا سَوَاءٌ عَلَيْهِمْ أَأَنذَرْتَهُمْ أَمْ لَمْ تُنذِرْهُمْ لَا يُؤْمِنُونَ
Translation: Indeed, those who are bent on rejecting belief—it is all the same whether you warn them or do not warn them—they will not believe.
Reflection: This verse introduces a contrasting group: those who reject faith. Unlike the receptive believers described earlier, the verse states that for these individuals, warnings are ineffective. It signifies their complete unwillingness or inability to accept the truth, no matter how clearly it is presented.

Verse 7

خَتَمَ اللَّهُ عَلَىٰ قُلُوبِهِمْ وَعَلَىٰ سَمْعِهِمْ ۖ وَعَلَىٰ أَبْصَارِهِمْ غِشَاوَةٌ ۖ وَلَهُمْ عَذَابٌ عَظِيمٌ
Translation: Allah has sealed their hearts and their hearing, and over their eyes is a veil. And they will suffer a tremendous punishment.
Reflection: Following the description of the rejecters of faith in Verse 6, this verse explains their condition as a consequence of their obstinacy. It states that Allah has set a seal on their hearts and hearing and placed a veil on their eyes, meaning they are unable to comprehend or respond to guidance. This state is described as leading to a great penalty, highlighting the severe consequences of persistent rejection of truth.

Verse 8

وَمِنَ النَّاسِ مَن يَقُولُ آمَنَّا بِاللَّهِ وَبِالْيَوْمِ الْآخِرِ وَمَا هُم بِمُؤْمِنِينَ
Translation: And among people are those who say, “We believe in Allah and the Last Day,” but they are not (true) believers.
Reflection: Moving beyond the clear-cut categories of believers and outright rejecters, this verse introduces a third group: those who outwardly claim to believe but lack sincere faith inwardly. This group describes the hypocrites, whose words do not align with their true state. While these hypocrites appeared during the time the Qur'an was being revealed in Medina, they represent a type found in every generation. This verse paves the way for a more detailed examination of their behavior in the verses that follow.

Verse 9

يُخَادِعُونَ اللَّهَ وَالَّذِينَ آمَنُوا وَمَا يَخْدَعُونَ إِلَّا أَنفُسَهُمْ وَمَا يَشْعُرُونَ
Translation: They (seek to) deceive Allah and those who believe, but they deceive only themselves—though they do not realize it.
Reflection: Expanding on the nature of the hypocrites introduced in Verse 8, this verse describes their attempt to deceive Allah and the believers. However, the verse immediately points out the futility of their efforts, stating that they are only deceiving themselves, even if they are unaware of it. It also subtly implies that Allah is aware of their intentions.

Verse 10

فِي قُلُوبِهِم مَّرَضٌ فَزَادَهُمُ اللَّهُ مَرَضًا ۖ وَلَهُمْ عَذَابٌ أَلِيمٌ بِمَا كَانُوا يَكْذِبُونَ
Translation: In their hearts is a disease, so Allah has increased their disease. And they will suffer a painful punishment because they (persistently) lie.
Reflection: This verse explains the underlying reason for the hypocrites' behavior described in verses 8 and 9: they have a spiritual or moral "disease" in their hearts. The verse states that Allah increases this disease, meaning their insincerity and deceptive actions lead to a worsening of their spiritual condition. Because of their falsehood, they face a painful punishment, connecting their character flaws directly to their fate.

Verse 11

وَإِذَا قِيلَ لَهُمْ لَا تُفْسِدُوا فِي الْأَرْضِ قَالُوا إِنَّمَا نَحْنُ مُصْلِحُونَ
Translation: And when they are told, “Do not spread corruption on the earth,” they say, “We are only reformers.”
Reflection: This verse describes a characteristic of the hypocrites: when asked to cease causing trouble or disruption on Earth, they falsely claim they are working towards peace. This highlights their deceptive nature and their distorted perception of their own actions. The verse introduces the theme of their self-deception and denial, which is further explored in the verses that follow.

Verse 12

أَلَا إِنَّهُمْ هُمُ الْمُفْسِدُونَ وَلَـٰكِن لَّا يَشْعُرُونَ
Translation: Truly, it is they who are the corruptors—but they do not perceive it.
Reflection: This verse directly refutes the claim made in the previous verse. It states clearly that these individuals are, in fact, the ones who are causing disruption, even though they do not understand or admit this reality about themselves. Their sickness of heart and lack of sincerity towards Allah distorts their view of right and wrong, leading them to believe their harmful actions are beneficial. This continues the depiction of their self-deception and adds the dimension of their being unaware of the true nature and consequences of their deeds.

Verse 13

وَإِذَا قِيلَ لَهُمْ آمِنُوا كَمَا آمَنَ النَّاسُ قَالُوا أَنُؤْمِنُ كَمَا آمَنَ السُّفَهَاءُ ۗ أَلَا إِنَّهُمْ هُمُ السُّفَهَاءُ وَلَـٰكِن لَّا يَعْلَمُونَ
Translation: And when they are told, “Believe as the people have believed,” they say, “Should we believe as the foolish have believed?” Truly, it is they who are the foolish—but they do not know it.
Reflection: This verse reveals another trait of these individuals: their arrogance and contempt for sincere believers. When asked to have faith in the way others have sincerely believed, they dismiss the believers as fools. The Qur'an directly responds by stating that it is *they* who are truly the fools, precisely because they lack the awareness or insight to recognize their own misguided state. This links to the previous verses by showing how their distorted perception extends to judging others and their own understanding.

Verse 14

وَإِذَا لَقُوا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا قَالُوا آمَنَّا وَإِذَا خَلَوْا إِلَىٰ شَيَاطِينِهِمْ قَالُوا إِنَّا مَعَكُمْ إِنَّمَا نَحْنُ مُسْتَهْزِئُونَ
Translation: And when they meet the believers, they say, “We believe.” But when they are alone with their evil ones, they say, “We are definitely with you—we were only mocking.”
Reflection: This verse exposes the double-faced behavior of these hypocritical individuals. When they encounter the sincere believers, they falsely claim to have faith, seeking acceptance and perhaps wanting to cause trouble from within the community. However, when they are in private with their close companions, described in the verse as "evil ones," they reveal their true stance, affirming their loyalty to their companions and admitting their outward profession of faith was merely mockery or pretense. This illustrates their lack of sincerity and their cunning nature. The description of their companions as "evil ones" reflects the perspective that these allies encouraged or shared their negative behavior.

Verse 15

اللَّهُ يَسْتَهْزِئُ بِهِمْ وَيَمُدُّهُمْ فِي طُغْيَانِهِمْ يَعْمَهُونَ
Translation: (But) Allah will respond to their mockery, and will let them continue blindly in their transgression.
Reflection: This verse describes Allah's response to the deceptive and mocking behavior of the hypocrites. It states that Allah will turn their mockery back upon them, essentially allowing their own schemes and insincerity to become their undoing. He gives them temporary leeway in their wrongdoing, but this only leads them further into misguidance, causing them to stumble blindly and lose direction. This verse serves as a stern warning about the consequences of hypocrisy and highlights Allah's power over their affairs, contrasting with their belief in their own cleverness.

Verse 16

أُولَـٰئِكَ الَّذِينَ اشْتَرَوُا الضَّلَالَةَ بِالْهُدَىٰ فَمَا رَبِحَت تِّجَارَتُهُمْ وَمَا كَانُوا مُهْتَدِينَ
Translation: They are the ones who have exchanged guidance for misguidance. But their trade has brought no gain, and they are not rightly guided.
Reflection: This verse provides a summary of the state of the hypocrites described in the preceding verses. It uses the metaphor of a transaction, saying they have exchanged true guidance for misguidance or error. However, this trade has brought them no benefit; their endeavor is described as "profitless," and they have completely lost their way. This verse underscores the ultimate failure of their path, linking their deceptive actions and arrogance to a fundamental loss of direction in life.

Verse 17

مَثَلُهُمْ كَمَثَلِ الَّذِي اسْتَوْقَدَ نَارًا فَلَمَّا أَضَاءَتْ مَا حَوْلَهُ ذَهَبَ اللَّهُ بِنُورِهِمْ وَتَرَكَهُمْ فِي ظُلُمَاتٍ لَّا يُبْصِرُونَ
Translation: Their example is like that of someone who kindled a fire, but when it lit up what was around him, Allah took away their light and left them in complete darkness—unable to see.
Reflection: This verse presents the first of two parables used to illustrate the condition of the hypocrites. It compares them to someone who lights a fire: for a moment, the fire provides light, enabling them to see—but then Allah takes the light away, leaving them in total darkness, unable to perceive anything. This reflects the situation of the hypocrites: they may have had some exposure to the truth or guidance (kindling the fire), and perhaps even understood it briefly (when it lit up around them). However, because they rejected or turned away from it, Allah removed that light, leaving them lost in darkness. This parable underscores the consequences of rejecting guidance after having had the opportunity to grasp it.

Verse 18

صُمٌّ بُكْمٌ عُمْيٌ فَهُمْ لَا يَرْجِعُونَ
Translation: (They are portrayed as) deaf, dumb, and blind—so they will not return (to the right path).
Reflection: Following the first parable, this verse describes the state of the hypocrites left in darkness. Having rejected the light, they are portrayed as deaf to the truth, dumb (unable or unwilling to speak it), and blind to guidance. This suggests that their rejection has led them to a condition in which they are no longer capable of finding their way back to the right path. Their senses—once potential channels for receiving guidance—are effectively shut off. This verse reinforces the hopelessness of their condition after having turned away from the light.

Verse 19

أَوْ كَصَيِّبٍ مِّنَ السَّمَاءِ فِيهِ ظُلُمَاتٌ وَرَعْدٌ وَبَرْقٌ يَجْعَلُونَ أَصَابِعَهُمْ فِي آذَانِهِم مِّنَ الصَّوَاعِقِ حَذَرَ الْمَوْتِ ۚ وَاللَّهُ مُحِيطٌ بِالْكَافِرِينَ
Translation: Or (it is) like a rainstorm from the sky, full of darkness, thunder, and lightning. They press their fingers into their ears at every thunderclap, fearing death. But Allah fully encompasses the disbelievers.
Reflection: This verse presents a second parable to illustrate the condition of the hypocrites. It compares their situation to being caught in a severe storm: a dark cloud with heavy rain, thunder, and lightning. The storm represents the trials, challenges, and perhaps moments of truth or warnings that come their way. The darkness, thunder, and lightning evoke fear and confusion. They react by trying to block out the terrifying sounds of thunder, fearful for their lives. This depicts their panic and desperate attempts to avoid the overwhelming reality of their situation and the consequences of their disbelief. The statement "But Allah is ever round the rejecters of Faith!" serves as a reminder that they cannot escape Allah's power and ultimate judgment, emphasizing that He is fully aware of and in control of their situation, even amidst their turmoil.

Verse 20

يَكَادُ الْبَرْقُ يَخْطَفُ أَبْصَارَهُمْ ۖ كُلَّمَا أَضَاءَ لَهُم مَّشَوْا فِيهِ وَإِذَا أَظْلَمَ عَلَيْهِمْ قَامُوا ۚ وَلَوْ شَاءَ اللَّهُ لَذَهَبَ بِسَمْعِهِمْ وَأَبْصَارِهِمْ ۚ إِنَّ اللَّهَ عَلَىٰ كُلِّ شَيْءٍ قَدِيرٌ
Translation: The lightning nearly takes away their sight. Whenever it flashes, they walk in its light; but when darkness covers them, they stand still. And if Allah had willed, He could have taken away their hearing and sight. Truly, Allah is Most Capable of everything.
Reflection: This verse continues the second parable, detailing the hypocrites' erratic behavior during the storm. The lightning flashes intensely, almost blinding them, yet provides fleeting moments of light. During these brief moments, they manage to move forward tentatively, but when the darkness returns between flashes, they stop, paralyzed by fear and uncertainty. This illustrates their unstable and inconsistent approach; they only react or move towards guidance when it's immediately apparent and seems advantageous, but freeze or turn away when faced with difficulty or uncertainty. The verse concludes by stating Allah's absolute power, noting that if He willed, He could permanently remove their ability to hear or see, reinforcing the idea that their faculties and their fate are entirely within His control.

Verse 21

يَا أَيُّهَا النَّاسُ اعْبُدُوا رَبَّكُمُ الَّذِي خَلَقَكُمْ وَالَّذِينَ مِن قَبْلِكُمْ لَعَلَّكُمْ تَتَّقُونَ
Translation: O mankind! Worship your Lord, who created you and those before you, so that you may become mindful (of Him).
Reflection: This verse begins a universal call to all people. It asks everyone to worship Allah, their Creator and Sustainer. We are reminded that Allah created not only us but also all the generations before us. The purpose of this worship, according to this verse, is so that we may develop mindfulness of Allah and live righteously. It sets the stage by establishing Allah's fundamental relationship with humanity as the Creator.

Verse 22

الَّذِي جَعَلَ لَكُمُ الْأَرْضَ فِرَاشًا وَالسَّمَاءَ بِنَاءً وَأَنزَلَ مِنَ السَّمَاءِ مَاءً فَأَخْرَجَ بِهِ مِنَ الثَّمَرَاتِ رِزْقًا لَّكُمْ ۖ فَلَا تَجْعَلُوا لِلَّهِ أَندَادًا وَأَنتُمْ تَعْلَمُونَ
Translation: (He is the One) who made the earth a stable ground for you, and the sky a canopy, and sent down rain from it, producing by it fruits as provision for you. So do not set up equals to Allah when you know (that He alone created and provides for all your needs).
Reflection: Following the call to worship the Creator, this verse provides reasons why Allah alone deserves worship. It highlights some of Allah's immense blessings: making the earth a comfortable and habitable place like a resting-place for us, and making the sky above us like a protective and orderly roof. It also mentions the sending down of rain from the sky, which brings forth fruits and sustenance for us. These are presented as clear signs of Allah's power and provision. Because we know these facts – that Allah is the Creator and Provider of all these things – the verse concludes with a directive not to associate partners or equals with Him in worship. This verse emphasizes recognizing Allah's unique role in creation and provision as the basis for exclusive worship.

Verse 23

وَإِن كُنتُمْ فِي رَيْبٍ مِّمَّا نَزَّلْنَا عَلَىٰ عَبْدِنَا فَأْتُوا بِسُورَةٍ مِّن مِّثْلِهِ وَادْعُوا شُهَدَاءَكُم مِّن دُونِ اللَّهِ إِن كُنتُمْ صَادِقِينَ
Translation: And if you are in doubt about what We have revealed to Our servant (Muhammad), then produce a surah like it, and call your witnesses (supporters and helpers) besides Allah—if you are truthful.
Reflection: Transitioning from the call to worship based on creation, this verse addresses those who have doubts about the divine origin of the Qur’an, which was revealed to Prophet Muhammad. It presents a challenge: if you doubt that this is from Allah, then produce a single chapter (Sura) that is comparable to it in quality and nature. You are invited to seek help from anyone or anything you wish, other than Allah, to meet this challenge. This is presented as a proof of the Qur’an's unique nature and source.

Verse 24

فَإِن لَّمْ تَفْعَلُوا وَلَن تَفْعَلُوا فَاتَّقُوا النَّارَ الَّتِي وَقُودُهَا النَّاسُ وَالْحِجَارَةُ ۖ أُعِدَّتْ لِلْكَافِرِينَ
Translation: But if you cannot do so—and you will never be able to—then fear the Fire, whose fuel is people and stones, prepared for the disbelievers.
Reflection: This verse directly follows the challenge in the previous one. It asserts with certainty that the challenge (to produce a chapter like the Qur'an) cannot be met. It then presents a warning of the consequences for those who reject the truth after this evidence and challenge have been presented. It describes a fearsome punishment in the Fire, whose fuel includes people and stones, indicating its intensity. This fire is stated to be prepared for those who reject faith. This verse serves as a stern reminder of accountability for rejecting the divine message.

Verse 25

وَبَشِّرِ الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا وَعَمِلُوا الصَّالِحَاتِ أَنَّ لَهُمْ جَنَّاتٍ تَجْرِي مِن تَحْتِهَا الْأَنْهَارُ ۖ كُلَّمَا رُزِقُوا مِنْهَا مِن ثَمَرَةٍ رِّزْقًا ۙ قَالُوا هَـٰذَا الَّذِي رُزِقْنَا مِن قَبْلُ ۖ وَأُتُوا بِهِ مُتَشَابِهًا ۖ وَلَهُمْ فِيهَا أَزْوَاجٌ مُّطَهَّرَةٌ ۖ وَهُمْ فِيهَا خَالِدُونَ
Translation: And give good news (O Muhammad) to those who believe and do righteous deeds: they will have gardens (in Paradise) beneath which rivers flow. Every time they are provided with fruit, they will say, “This is what we were given before,”—for they will be served fruit that appears similar. And they will have pure spouses therein, and they will remain there forever.
Reflection: In sharp contrast to the warning of punishment for rejecters in the previous verse, this verse offers joyful news for those who believe and live righteously. It describes their reward as Gardens in Paradise, flowing with rivers. When they are given fruits there, they recognize a familiarity, saying it is like what they received before, though the reality is different. This hints at a delightful experience where things appear similar yet are uniquely pleasurable. The verse also mentions having pure companions and dwelling in this state eternally. It paints a picture of ultimate peace, joy, and permanence for the believers.

Verse 26

إِنَّ اللَّهَ لَا يَسْتَحْيِي أَن يَضْرِبَ مَثَلًا مَّا بَعُوضَةً فَمَا فَوْقَهَا ۚ فَأَمَّا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا فَيَعْلَمُونَ أَنَّهُ الْحَقُّ مِن رَّبِّهِمْ ۖ وَأَمَّا الَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا فَيَقُولُونَ مَاذَا أَرَادَ اللَّهُ بِهَـٰذَا مَثَلًا ۘ يُضِلُّ بِهِ كَثِيرًا وَيَهْدِي بِهِ كَثِيرًا ۚ وَمَا يُضِلُّ بِهِ إِلَّا الْفَاسِقِينَ
Translation: Indeed, Allah is not shy to set forth an example—even of a mosquito or anything greater (or smaller). As for those who believe, they know it is the truth from their Lord. But those who disbelieve say, “What does Allah mean by such an example?” Through it, He causes many to stray and many to be guided. But He causes none to stray except the defiantly disobedient.
Reflection: This verse addresses potential questions or objections raised about the use of parables or comparisons in the Qur'an, particularly those involving seemingly insignificant things (like a gnat, as later examples in the Qur'an show, although not specifically in this verse). It explains that Allah does not hesitate to use comparisons involving anything, from the smallest to the greatest, because all creation reflects His power and wisdom. It contrasts the reactions of believers and those who reject faith. Believers accept these parables as truth from Allah, understanding that there is wisdom in them. Those who reject faith, however, question the purpose of such parables, showing a lack of reverence and understanding. The verse explains that these parables serve as a test: they guide those who are receptive to truth but cause those who are already inclined to stray to drift further away.

Verse 27

الَّذِينَ يَنقُضُونَ عَهْدَ اللَّهِ مِن بَعْدِ مِيثَاقِهِ وَيَقْطَعُونَ مَا أَمَرَ اللَّهُ بِهِ أَن يُوصَلَ وَيُفْسِدُونَ فِي الْأَرْضِ ۚ أُولَـٰئِكَ هُمُ الْخَاسِرُونَ
Translation: (Those) who break Allah’s covenant after it has been confirmed, and sever what Allah has commanded to be joined, and spread corruption on the earth. It is they who are the true losers.
Reflection: This verse further describes the characteristics of those who "forsake the path" mentioned at the end of the previous verse. These are identified as evildoers who suffer loss. Their defining traits are listed: they break the commitments they made to Allah after affirming them; they sever the connections that Allah has commanded to be maintained (which can include relationships with Allah, family ties, and bonds within humanity); and they spread corruption or disruption on earth. The verse concludes by emphasizing that the harm from these actions ultimately falls back on themselves, leading to their own loss and detriment.

Verse 28

كَيْفَ تَكْفُرُونَ بِاللَّهِ وَكُنتُمْ أَمْوَاتًا فَأَحْيَاكُمْ ۖ ثُمَّ يُمِيتُكُمْ ثُمَّ يُحْيِيكُمْ ثُمَّ إِلَيْهِ تُرْجَعُونَ
Translation: How can you disbelieve in Allah? You were lifeless, and He gave you life. Then He will cause you to die, then bring you back to life, and then to Him you will be returned.
Reflection: This verse directly questions how anyone can reject faith in Allah when presented with undeniable facts about life itself. It points to the cycle of human existence: we were once without life, then Allah gave us life; He will cause us to die, and then He will bring us back to life again (on the Day of Resurrection). Finally, we will return to Him for accountability. This verse uses the profound and observable reality of life, death, and the promise of resurrection as a powerful sign of Allah's power and a reason to believe in Him and not reject faith.

Verse 29

هُوَ الَّذِي خَلَقَ لَكُم مَّا فِي الْأَرْضِ جَمِيعًا ثُمَّ اسْتَوَىٰ إِلَى السَّمَاءِ فَسَوَّاهُنَّ سَبْعَ سَمَاوَاتٍ ۚ وَهُوَ بِكُلِّ شَيْءٍ عَلِيمٌ
Translation: He is the One who created for you everything that is on the earth. Then He turned to the heaven and fashioned it into seven heavens. And He is fully aware of all things.
Reflection: Building upon the theme of Allah's power in the previous verse, this one further emphasizes His role as Creator. It states that Allah created everything on Earth for our benefit, highlighting His grace and the value He has placed on humanity by making the resources of the world available to us. It then mentions His creation of the heavens, stating that He fashioned them into seven heavens, bringing order and perfection to them. The verse concludes by affirming that Allah has complete knowledge of all things. This verse serves to reinforce Allah's absolute power and knowledge over all creation, both on Earth and in the vastness of the heavens.

Verse 30

وَإِذْ قَالَ رَبُّكَ لِلْمَلَائِكَةِ إِنِّي جَاعِلٌ فِي الْأَرْضِ خَلِيفَةً ۖ قَالُوا أَتَجْعَلُ فِيهَا مَن يُفْسِدُ فِيهَا وَيَسْفِكُ الدِّمَاءَ وَنَحْنُ نُسَبِّحُ بِحَمْدِكَ وَنُقَدِّسُ لَكَ ۖ قَالَ إِنِّي أَعْلَمُ مَا لَا تَعْلَمُونَ
Translation: And (mention, O Muhammad) when your Lord said to the angels, "I am going to place a vicegerent (a successive authority) on earth." They said, "Will You place in it someone who will spread corruption and shed blood, while we glorify Your praises and sanctify You?" He (Allah) replied, "Indeed, I know what you do not know."
Reflection: This verse marks the beginning of the account of humanity’s creation. Allah informed the angels of His decision to appoint a vicegerent on earth—a being who would bear responsibility and authority. This vicegerent refers to humanity, starting with Adam, who was entrusted with caring for and cultivating the earth according to Allah’s guidance. The angels, in humility and sincerity—not in defiance—asked a question to understand the wisdom behind this decision. They wondered if this new creation, capable of corruption and bloodshed, would be suited for such a role, especially when they themselves are fully devoted to glorifying and sanctifying Allah. Allah responded that He knows what they do not, indicating that there is a greater wisdom and hidden potential in humanity—qualities that the angels had not yet seen. This verse sets the foundation for understanding the unique role and trust that humanity has been given on earth.

Verse 31

وَعَلَّمَ آدَمَ الْأَسْمَاءَ كُلَّهَا ثُمَّ عَرَضَهُمْ عَلَى الْمَلَائِكَةِ فَقَالَ أَنبِئُونِي بِأَسْمَاءِ هَـٰؤُلَاءِ إِن كُنتُمْ صَادِقِينَ
Translation: And He taught Adam all the names. Then He presented them to the angels and said, "Tell Me the names of these, if what you say is true."
Reflection: After informing the angels of His intention and hearing their question, Allah revealed a unique quality He had granted to Adam: the knowledge of all the names. This refers to a special capacity for understanding, learning, and expressing knowledge—possibly including the names, attributes, and purposes of created things. Allah then presented what had been named to the angels and invited them to name them, if their earlier understanding of creation had been complete. This moment served to demonstrate Adam’s distinct gift and to show the angels a wisdom they had not yet seen. It affirmed that the creation of humanity carried a purpose known fully only to Allah.

Verse 32

قَالُوا سُبْحَانَكَ لَا عِلْمَ لَنَا إِلَّا مَا عَلَّمْتَنَا ۖ إِنَّكَ أَنتَ الْعَلِيمُ الْحَكِيمُ
Translation: They (the angels) said, "Glory be to You! We have no knowledge except what You have taught us. Indeed, You alone are the All-Knowing, the All-Wise."
Reflection: The angels responded with humility, declaring Allah’s perfection and recognizing their own limitations. They admitted that they had no knowledge beyond what Allah had taught them, and they affirmed that all true knowledge and wisdom belong to Him alone. This moment reflects the angels’ complete submission and reverence, and it reinforces the idea that human knowledge—when granted by Allah—can serve a unique and purposeful role in creation.

Verse 33

قَالَ يَا آدَمُ أَنبِئْهُم بِأَسْمَائِهِمْ ۖ فَلَمَّا أَنبَأَهُم بِأَسْمَائِهِمْ قَالَ أَلَمْ أَقُل لَّكُمْ إِنِّي أَعْلَمُ غَيْبَ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضِ وَأَعْلَمُ مَا تُبْدُونَ وَمَا كُنتُمْ تَكْتُمُونَ
Translation: He (Allah) said, "O Adam, inform them of their names." And when he had informed them, He (Allah) said, "Did I not tell you that I know the unseen of the heavens and the earth, and I know what you reveal and what you conceal?"
Reflection: After the angels acknowledged their limitations, Allah instructed Adam to inform them of the names. Adam did so, demonstrating the unique knowledge that Allah had granted him. This act affirmed the special capacity given to humanity—something the angels had not been given. After Adam named the things, Allah addressed the angels to make clear the depth of His knowledge. He alone knows both the seen and the unseen—realities that go beyond human or angelic perception—including what is made public and what remains hidden. This affirmed the wisdom behind appointing Adam as vicegerent, highlighting qualities and potential the angels had not fully perceived.

Verse 34

وَإِذْ قُلْنَا لِلْمَلَائِكَةِ اسْجُدُوا لِآدَمَ فَسَجَدُوا إِلَّا إِبْلِيسَ أَبَىٰ وَاسْتَكْبَرَ وَكَانَ مِنَ الْكَافِرِينَ
Translation: And (mention) when We said to the angels, "Prostrate before Adam," and they all prostrated—except Iblîs. He refused and acted arrogantly, and so he became one of the disbelievers.
Reflection: To further emphasize the honor Allah had given Adam, He commanded the angels to prostrate before him—a gesture of respect, not worship. All the angels obeyed without hesitation. However, Iblîs, who was present among them, refused. His refusal came not from ignorance, but from arrogance and pride, which led him to reject Allah’s command. This act of defiance marked Iblîs as one who disbelieves. He is introduced here as a figure in opposition to humanity, beginning with his refusal to accept Adam’s honored status. Although mentioned alongside the angels, Iblîs was not one of them. As clarified in the Qur’an (18:50), he was of the jinn—a separate creation made with free will, distinct from the angels (who do not disobey Allah).

Verse 35

وَقُلْنَا يَا آدَمُ اسْكُنْ أَنتَ وَزَوْجُكَ الْجَنَّةَ وَكُلَا مِنْهَا رَغَدًا حَيْثُ شِئْتُمَا وَلَا تَقْرَبَا هَـٰذِهِ الشَّجَرَةَ فَتَكُونَا مِنَ الظَّالِمِينَ
Translation: And We said, “O Adam, dwell—you and your wife—in Paradise and eat freely from wherever you wish. But do not go near this tree, or else you will be among the wrongdoers.”
Reflection: After being honoured and entrusted with responsibility, Adam and his wife were placed in Paradise. They were granted full freedom to enjoy all its blessings, with just one clear restriction: not to approach a particular tree. This single command was a test of their obedience and self-restraint. It symbolizes the moral boundaries Allah places for human beings—limits that define right and wrong and shape the journey of life on Earth.

Verse 36

فَأَزَلَّهُمَا الشَّيْطَانُ عَنْهَا فَأَخْرَجَهُمَا مِمَّا كَانَا فِيهِ ۖ وَقُلْنَا اهْبِطُوا بَعْضُكُمْ لِبَعْضٍ عَدُوٌّ ۖ وَلَكُمْ فِي الْأَرْضِ مُسْتَقَرٌّ وَمَتَاعٌ إِلَىٰ حِينٍ
Translation: But Satan deceived them and led them out of the state they had been in. And We said, “Descend from the heavens—you will be enemies to one another—and you will have on Earth a place to settle and provisions for a time.”
Reflection: Satan (Iblis), who had earlier refused to honor Adam (verse 34), now actively misled Adam and his wife, persuading them to approach the forbidden tree (verse 35). This disobedience led to their removal from Paradise. Allah then declared that they and their descendants would descend to Earth. This marked the beginning of human life on Earth, where humanity was appointed to serve as Allah’s vicegerents (verse 30). The descent also initiated the ongoing struggle between human beings and Satan. Earth would now be their temporary home, a place of trial and provision for a limited time.

Verse 37

فَتَلَقَّىٰ آدَمُ مِن رَّبِّهِ كَلِمَاتٍ فَتَابَ عَلَيْهِ ۚ إِنَّهُ هُوَ التَّوَّابُ الرَّحِيمُ
Translation: Then Adam received words (of guidance) from his Lord, and He accepted his repentance. Indeed, He is the Accepter of Repentance, the Most Merciful.
Reflection: After the mistake, Allah—out of His mercy—taught Adam specific words of repentance. When Adam sincerely turned back using these words, Allah accepted his repentance. This shows that the path to forgiveness was not closed; it was guided by Allah Himself. Although Eve’s repentance is not mentioned in this verse, it appears elsewhere (e.g., 7:23). Her omission here may be because the focus is on Adam’s unique role as the first human and vicegerent on Earth, making his spiritual journey central. This verse highlights a fundamental concept: Allah is always ready to forgive those who return to Him sincerely. It offers deep hope for humanity—showing that repentance, when genuine, can lead to real forgiveness.

Verse 38

قُلْنَا اهْبِطُوا مِنْهَا جَمِيعًا ۖ فَإِمَّا يَأْتِيَنَّكُم مِّنِّي هُدًى فَمَن تَبِعَ هُدَايَ فَلَا خَوْفٌ عَلَيْهِمْ وَلَا هُمْ يَحْزَنُونَ
Translation: We said, "Descend, all of you! Then, when guidance comes to you from Me, whoever follows My guidance will have no fear, nor will they grieve."
Reflection: Confirming humanity's dwelling on Earth, Allah reiterated that guidance would come to them. This verse provides the fundamental promise: whoever follows Allah's guidance will be secure, free from fear and grief. It sets the condition for human success and well-being on Earth, linking it directly to adhering to the divine guidance that would be revealed.

Verse 39

وَالَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا وَكَذَّبُوا بِآيَاتِنَا أُولَـٰئِكَ أَصْحَابُ النَّارِ ۖ هُمْ فِيهَا خَالِدُونَ
Translation: And those who disbelieve and deny Our signs will be residents of the Fire—they will remain there forever.
Reflection: This verse presents the opposite outcome to the promise in the previous verse. While those who follow Allah’s guidance will have no fear or grief, those who reject faith and deny His signs will face a different fate: the Fire, where they will remain forever. It makes clear the final consequence of disbelief and denial, in contrast to the mercy and security found in following divine guidance.

Verse 40

يَا بَنِي إِسْرَائِيلَ اذْكُرُوا نِعْمَتِيَ الَّتِي أَنْعَمْتُ عَلَيْكُمْ وَأَوْفُوا بِعَهْدِي أُوفِ بِعَهْدِكُمْ وَإِيَّايَ فَارْهَبُونِ
Translation: O Children of Israel! Remember the blessings I granted you. Fulfill your covenant with Me, and I will fulfill Mine with you. And stand in reverent awe of Me (alone).
Reflection: This verse begins a significant address in the Qur'an directed at the Children of Israel, recalling the many blessings Allah has historically granted them. It calls upon them to remember these special favors. The verse then emphasizes the importance of fulfilling their covenant with Allah. In return, Allah promises to fulfill His covenant with them. This highlights a reciprocal relationship based on obedience and remembrance. Finally, it instructs them to fear only Allah, emphasizing His ultimate authority and power, setting the stage for the commands and reminders that follow.

Verse 41

وَآمِنُوا بِمَا أَنزَلْتُ مُصَدِّقًا لِّمَا مَعَكُمْ وَلَا تَكُونُوا أَوَّلَ كَافِرٍ بِهِ ۖ وَلَا تَشْتَرُوا بِآيَاتِي ثَمَنًا قَلِيلًا وَإِيَّايَ فَاتَّقُونِ
Translation: Believe in what I have revealed, confirming what you already have, and do not be the first to reject it. Do not trade My signs for a fleeting gain, and be conscious of Me (alone).
Reflection: Building on the call to remember Allah's favors and fulfill the covenant, this verse specifically instructs the Children of Israel to believe in the revelation sent down to Prophet Muhammad. It points out that this new revelation confirms and aligns with the scriptures they already possess (like the Torah). They are urged not to be among the first to disbelieve in it. The verse warns against rejecting Allah's revelations for any worldly gain or trivial benefit, describing such gain as a "small price" when compared to the value of faith and obedience. It reiterates the command to fear only Allah, reinforcing the message of the previous verse.

Verse 42

وَلَا تَلْبِسُوا الْحَقَّ بِالْبَاطِلِ وَتَكْتُمُوا الْحَقَّ وَأَنتُمْ تَعْلَمُونَ
Translation: Do not mix truth with falsehood, nor knowingly hide the truth.
Reflection: Following the command to believe in the revelation sent down to Prophet Muhammad, this verse addresses specific actions. It forbids deliberately mixing truth with falsehood, as this leads to confusion and distorts understanding. It also prohibits concealing the truth, especially when one is fully aware of it. This includes actions such as deliberately misinterpreting earlier scriptures or misleading others about the authenticity of the new revelation from Allah.

Verse 43

وَأَقِيمُوا الصَّلَاةَ وَآتُوا الزَّكَاةَ وَارْكَعُوا مَعَ الرَّاكِعِينَ
Translation: Establish prayer, pay zakah (alms-tax), and bow down with those who bow (in worship and obedience).
Reflection: This verse invites the Children of Israel to engage in fundamental acts of worship and community life in Islam. It calls for establishing regular prayer (Salat) and giving purifying charity (Zakat - a form of obligatory regular charity), which are central pillars of Islamic practice. The command to "bow down with those who bow down" is an invitation to join the community of believers who submit to Allah in worship, encouraging unity and shared practice. This serves as a practical invitation to integrate into the Muslim community and its way of life. This verse introduces the acts of worship that followers of Allah's message undertake.

Verse 44

أَتَأْمُرُونَ النَّاسَ بِالْبِرِّ وَتَنسَوْنَ أَنفُسَكُمْ وَأَنتُمْ تَتْلُونَ الْكِتَابَ ۚ أَفَلَا تَعْقِلُونَ
Translation: Do you call others to righteousness while neglecting it yourselves—though you recite the Scripture? Do you not reflect?
Reflection: This verse transitions to a direct questioning of the Children of Israel's actions. It points out a potential inconsistency: instructing others towards righteous conduct while neglecting to practice it themselves, despite having their own scriptures that teach righteousness. This highlights the importance of aligning one's actions with one's words, particularly for those who have knowledge of religious teachings. It questions their understanding, suggesting that true understanding of scripture should lead to personal practice and consistency. This verse is a reflection on the responsibility that comes with knowledge and preaching.

Verse 45

وَاسْتَعِينُوا بِالصَّبْرِ وَالصَّلَاةِ ۚ وَإِنَّهَا لَكَبِيرَةٌ إِلَّا عَلَى الْخَاشِعِينَ
Translation: And seek (Allah's) help through patience and prayer. Indeed, this is difficult—except for those who are humbly submissive (to Allah).
Reflection: Recognizing the difficulty in aligning words and deeds, and perhaps the challenge of accepting the new message, this verse offers guidance on finding strength. It advises seeking help from Allah through patient perseverance (sabr) and prayer. These two are presented as essential tools for facing challenges and staying firm in faith. The verse acknowledges that this path can be difficult, but notes that it is made easier for those who possess a humble spirit and are truly devoted to Allah. This provides a way forward for those who might find the previous verses' calls challenging.

Verse 46

الَّذِينَ يَظُنُّونَ أَنَّهُم مُّلَاقُو رَبِّهِمْ وَأَنَّهُمْ إِلَيْهِ رَاجِعُونَ
Translation: Those who are certain they will meet their Lord and that they will return to Him.
Reflection: This verse describes the characteristic of those who find strength in patience and prayer (mentioned in the previous verse). They are individuals who live with the certain knowledge that they will ultimately meet their Lord, Allah, and return to Him. This awareness of accountability in the hereafter provides perspective and motivation to endure hardships, remain patient, and turn to Allah in prayer. It connects the acts of worship and patience with the belief in the afterlife and judgment.

Verse 47

يَا بَنِي إِسْرَائِيلَ اذْكُرُوا نِعْمَتِيَ الَّتِي أَنْعَمْتُ عَلَيْكُمْ وَأَنِّي فَضَّلْتُكُمْ عَلَى الْعَالَمِينَ
Translation: O Children of Israel! Remember the favor I bestowed upon you, and that I chose you above others (at a certain time).
Reflection: Returning to a theme introduced in verse 40, this verse again addresses the Children of Israel and reminds them of the special favors Allah bestowed upon them. It explicitly mentions that, at a certain time, they were chosen and preferred by Allah to carry His message to humanity. This serves as a reminder of their historical privilege and responsibility, contrasting it with their attitude towards the new revelation. It sets the stage for recounting specific historical events where these favors were shown, which begins in the next verse.

Verse 48

وَاتَّقُوا يَوْمًا لَّا تَجْزِي نَفْسٌ عَن نَّفْسٍ شَيْئًا وَلَا يُقْبَلُ مِنْهَا شَفَاعَةٌ وَلَا يُؤْخَذُ مِنْهَا عَدْلٌ وَلَا هُمْ يُنصَرُونَ
Translation: And beware of a Day when no soul will be able to benefit another in any way; no intercession will be accepted (without Allah’s permission), no ransom will be taken, and no help (from outside) will be given.
Reflection: Following the reminder of past favors and the responsibility that came with them, this verse issues a solemn warning about the Day of Judgment. It stresses that on that day, no soul will be able to help another, intercession (seeking forgiveness or favor on behalf of another) will not be accepted, no ransom or compensation can be offered, and no external help will be available. This emphasizes individual accountability before Allah and highlights the critical importance of one's own faith and deeds in this life. It links the remembrance of Allah's past dealings with them to the ultimate consequence of their actions in the future.

Verse 49

وَإِذْ نَجَّيْنَاكُم مِّنْ آلِ فِرْعَوْنَ يَسُومُونَكُمْ سُوءَ الْعَذَابِ يُذَبِّحُونَ أَبْنَاءَكُمْ وَيَسْتَحْيُونَ نِسَاءَكُمْ ۚ وَفِي ذَٰلِكُم بَلَاءٌ مِّن رَّبِّكُمْ عَظِيمٌ
Translation: And (remember) how We saved you (your forefathers) from the people of Pharaoh, who subjected you to terrible torment—slaughtering your (newborn) sons and sparing your women. In that was a tremendous trial from your Lord.
Reflection: Beginning the enumeration of specific historical favors, this verse calls upon the Children of Israel to remember their deliverance from the tyrannical rule of Pharaoh and his people in Egypt. It vividly describes the severe suffering they endured under Pharaoh, including forced labor, harsh punishments, and the killing of their male children while sparing the females. The verse concludes by stating that this entire experience was a significant trial from Allah. This specific historical event serves as the first example in a series of reminders of Allah's grace and intervention in their history, setting the context for lessons drawn from their subsequent actions and reactions.

Verse 50

وَإِذْ فَرَقْنَا بِكُمُ الْبَحْرَ فَأَنجَيْنَاكُمْ وَأَغْرَقْنَا آلَ فِرْعَوْنَ وَأَنتُمْ تَنظُرُونَ
Translation: And (remember) when We parted the sea for you, rescuing you, and caused Pharaoh's people to drown before your very eyes.
Reflection: Picking up the narrative of the Israelites' journey, this verse recounts a major event: Allah's intervention to save them from the tyranny of Pharaoh. It reminds the audience of the dramatic moment when Allah caused the sea to part, creating a path for the Israelites to cross to safety, while the pursuing forces of Pharaoh were drowned before their eyes. This act is highlighted as a significant favor from Allah, a clear demonstration of His power and protection, immediately following a period of intense suffering and trial mentioned just before this verse. It emphasizes that their deliverance was a direct act of divine grace.

Verse 51

وَإِذْ وَاعَدْنَا مُوسَىٰ أَرْبَعِينَ لَيْلَةً ثُمَّ اتَّخَذْتُمُ الْعِجْلَ مِن بَعْدِهِ وَأَنتُمْ ظَالِمُونَ
Translation: And (remember) when We appointed forty nights for Moses (on Mount Sinai), then, in his absence, you took the calf (for worship), acting unjustly.
Reflection: Following their miraculous rescue and having received such a great favor, the narrative shifts to illustrate the Israelites' immediate response. It recalls that while Prophet Moses was away for forty nights, having been appointed by Allah to receive divine guidance, his people turned to worship a golden calf. This action is described as a severe transgression considering they had just been saved from a people who were idolaters themselves. This verse highlights a rapid turn from deliverance to disobedience.

Verse 52

ثُمَّ عَفَوْنَا عَنكُم مِّن بَعْدِ ذَٰلِكَ لَعَلَّكُمْ تَشْكُرُونَ
Translation: (Even) then, We forgave you afterward so that you might be grateful.
Reflection: Despite the worship of the calf mentioned in the previous verse, this verse emphasizes Allah's immense mercy. It states that even after such a grave act of polytheism, Allah forgave them. This forgiveness offered an opportunity to show gratitude for His grace, both in saving them from Pharaoh and pardoning their transgression. It illustrates a consistent pattern of divine forgiveness following human error, offering a chance for repentance and thankfulness.

Verse 53

وَإِذْ آتَيْنَا مُوسَى الْكِتَابَ وَالْفُرْقَانَ لَعَلَّكُمْ تَهْتَدُونَ
Translation: And (remember) when We gave Moses the Scripture and the criterion (to distinguish between right and wrong), so that you might be rightly guided.
Reflection: Continuing to recount Allah's favors upon the Israelites after the incident of the calf, this verse reminds them that Allah bestowed upon Prophet Moses the Scripture – the Torah – along with the Criterion, which serves to distinguish between right and wrong, truth and falsehood. This was given as a source of guidance for them. It highlights that receiving this divine law offered them a clear path to be rightly guided, presenting another opportunity for them to correct their ways and live according to Allah's commands following their pardon.

Verse 54

وَإِذْ قَالَ مُوسَىٰ لِقَوْمِهِ يَا قَوْمِ إِنَّكُمْ ظَلَمْتُمْ أَنفُسَكُم بِاتِّخَاذِكُمُ الْعِجْلَ فَتُوبُوا إِلَىٰ بَارِئِكُمْ فَاقْتُلُوا أَنفُسَكُمْ ذَٰلِكُمْ خَيْرٌ لَّكُمْ عِندَ بَارِئِكُمْ فَتَابَ عَلَيْكُمْ ۚ إِنَّهُ هُوَ التَّوَّابُ الرَّحِيمُ
Translation: And (remember) when Moses said to his people, "O my people, you have truly wronged yourselves by worshipping the calf. So turn in repentance to your Creator and execute (the guilty ones among) yourselves. That is better for you before your Creator." Then He accepted your repentance—truly, He is the Accepter of Repentance, the Most Merciful.
Reflection: This verse describes Prophet Moses's address to his people after their calf worship, conveying Allah's command for repentance and purification. Moses tells them they have greatly harmed themselves through this act and instructs them to turn back to their Creator. The severe command to "slay yourselves" is understood as a stringent form of atonement, where those most responsible for the idolatry were to be executed by others within the community as a means of cleansing and purifying the whole group from the sin. This was a heavy responsibility, but necessary to preserve the community's purity. The verse concludes by stating that Allah accepted their repentance after this act, emphasizing that He is indeed the One who accepts repentance and is Most Merciful, reinforcing the theme of forgiveness mentioned earlier (Verse 52).

Verse 55

وَإِذْ قُلْتُمْ يَا مُوسَىٰ لَن نُّؤْمِنَ لَكَ حَتَّىٰ نَرَى اللَّهَ جَهْرَةً فَأَخَذَتْكُمُ الصَّاعِقَةُ وَأَنتُمْ تَنظُرُونَ
Translation: And (remember) when you said, "O Moses, we will never believe you until we see Allah clearly (with our own eyes)." Then the thunderbolt struck you while you were watching.
Reflection: Despite the forgiveness and guidance they received, the narrative highlights another instance of the Israelites' challenging nature. This verse recounts how a group among them made an audacious demand: they declared they would not believe unless they saw Allah directly with their own eyes. Such a demand reflects a deep lack of faith and understanding of Allah's nature. The consequence was that they were struck by a thunderbolt, as a direct response to their inappropriate request. This incident is presented as an example of their stubbornness and is linked to similar demands for impossible proofs made by some people towards Prophet Muhammad.

Verse 56

ثُمَّ بَعَثْنَاكُم مِّن بَعْدِ مَوْتِكُمْ لَعَلَّكُمْ تَشْكُرُونَ
Translation: Then We brought you back to life after your death, so that you might be grateful.
Reflection: Following the severe consequence of their demand to see Allah mentioned in the previous verse, this verse recounts Allah's continued mercy. It states that after being struck down by the thunderbolt, Allah brought them back to life. This act of revival after death was another profound sign of Allah's power and grace, offering them yet another opportunity to recognize His favors and be grateful for the life and chances He provided them.

Verse 57

وَظَلَّلْنَا عَلَيْكُمُ الْغَمَامَ وَأَنزَلْنَا عَلَيْكُمُ الْمَنَّ وَالسَّلْوَىٰ ۖ كُلُوا مِن طَيِّبَاتِ مَا رَزَقْنَاكُمْ ۖ وَمَا ظَلَمُونَا وَلَـٰكِن كَانُوا أَنفُسَهُمْ يَظْلِمُونَ
Translation: And (remember) when We shaded you with clouds and sent down to you manna (a unique provision) and quails, (saying), "Eat from the good things We have provided for you." Yet they did not wrong Us, but they wronged themselves.
Reflection: This verse continues listing Allah’s favors upon the Israelites during their time in the wilderness after leaving Egypt. It recalls how Allah provided them with shade from clouds to protect them from the desert heat and sent down special sustenance: manna (a unique food) and quails. These were given as good things for them to eat—provisions from Allah’s bounty. Yet, despite these continued blessings, they showed no gratitude and acted rebelliously. The verse concludes by emphasizing that their actions did not harm Allah in any way; rather, they only harmed themselves. Their ingratitude and disobedience ultimately deprived them of further blessings and brought negative consequences upon them.

Verse 58

وَإِذْ قُلْنَا ادْخُلُوا هَـٰذِهِ الْقَرْيَةَ فَكُلُوا مِنْهَا حَيْثُ شِئْتُمْ رَغَدًا وَادْخُلُوا الْبَابَ سُجَّدًا وَقُولُوا حِطَّةٌ نَّغْفِرْ لَكُمْ خَطَايَاكُمْ ۚ وَسَنَزِيدُ الْمُحْسِنِينَ
Translation: And (remember) when We said, "Enter this town and eat freely from wherever you wish. But enter the gate in humility—(bowing) in prostration—and say, 'Relieve us of our burdens.' We will then forgive your sins and increase reward for those who do good."
Reflection: The narrative moves forward to another command given to the Israelites. They were instructed to enter a specific town (often understood to be Jerusalem), a place of plenty, and were told they could eat from its abundance as they desired. However, this entry came with specific instructions: they were to enter the gate with humility, showing submission in their physical posture and their spoken words. By doing so, Allah promised to forgive their sins and increase the reward for those among them who performed good deeds. This verse presents another opportunity for the Israelites to show obedience and humility and receive Allah's forgiveness and bounty.

Verse 59

فَبَدَّلَ الَّذِينَ ظَلَمُوا قَوْلًا غَيْرَ الَّذِي قِيلَ لَهُمْ فَأَنزَلْنَا عَلَى الَّذِينَ ظَلَمُوا رِجْزًا مِّنَ السَّمَاءِ بِمَا كَانُوا يَفْسُقُونَ
Translation: But those who wronged themselves (deliberately) altered the words they had been commanded to say. So We sent a punishment from the sky upon the wrongdoers for their repeated disobedience.
Reflection: This verse recounts the consequence of the Israelites' disobedience to the command given in the previous verse (2:58). Instead of following the instructions to enter the town with humility and speaking the words they were commanded, the wrongdoers among them deliberately substituted the words. As a result of this conscious disobedience and persistent defiance of Allah’s guidance, a punishment from the sky was sent upon them. This highlights the serious outcome of willful disobedience and altering Allah's instructions.

Verse 60

وَإِذِ اسْتَسْقَىٰ مُوسَىٰ لِقَوْمِهِ فَقُلْنَا اضْرِب بِّعَصَاكَ الْحَجَرَ ۖ فَانفَجَرَتْ مِنْهُ اثْنَتَا عَشْرَةَ عَيْنًا ۖ قَدْ عَلِمَ كُلُّ أُنَاسٍ مَّشْرَبَهُمْ ۖ كُلُوا وَاشْرَبُوا مِن رِّزْقِ اللَّهِ وَلَا تَعْثَوْا فِي الْأَرْضِ مُفْسِدِينَ
Translation: And (remember) when Moses prayed for water for his people, We said, "Strike the rock with your staff." Then twelve springs burst forth from it, and (every tribe) knew where to drink. "Eat and drink from Allah’s provisions, and do not cause corruption on the earth."
Reflection: This verse recounts a specific event when the Israelites needed water. Prophet Moses prayed to Allah on their behalf, and in response, Allah instructed him to strike a rock with his staff. When he did, twelve springs gushed out—one for each tribe, ensuring everyone had access to water. This is presented as an act of Allah's grace. After this provision, they were instructed to enjoy the sustenance Allah had provided, but were warned against spreading corruption on the earth. This highlights Allah's power to provide and His command for responsible behavior.

Verse 61

وَإِذْ قُلْتُمْ يَا مُوسَىٰ لَن نَّصْبِرَ عَلَىٰ طَعَامٍ وَاحِدٍ فَادْعُ لَنَا رَبَّكَ يُخْرِجْ لَنَا مِمَّا تُنبِتُ الْأَرْضُ مِن بَقْلِهَا وَقِثَّائِهَا وَفُومِهَا وَعَدَسِهَا وَبَصَلِهَا ۖ قَالَ أَتَسْتَبْدِلُونَ الَّذِي هُوَ أَدْنَىٰ بِالَّذِي هُوَ خَيْرٌ ۚ اهْبِطُوا مِصْرًا فَإِنَّ لَكُم مَّا سَأَلْتُمْ ۗ وَضُرِبَتْ عَلَيْهِمُ الذِّلَّةُ وَالْمَسْكَنَةُ وَبَاءُوا بِغَضَبٍ مِّنَ اللَّهِ ۗ ذَٰلِكَ بِأَنَّهُمْ كَانُوا يَكْفُرُونَ بِآيَاتِ اللَّهِ وَيَقْتُلُونَ النَّبِيِّينَ بِغَيْرِ الْحَقِّ ۗ ذَٰلِكَ بِمَا عَصَوا وَّكَانُوا يَعْتَدُونَ
Translation: And (remember) when you said, "O Moses, we can’t bear eating the same food (every day). So call upon your Lord to bring out for us some of what the earth grows—(like) herbs, cucumbers, garlic, lentils, and onions." (Moses) said, "Would you exchange what is better for what is lesser? Then go down to (any) town—you will find what you’re asking for." And they were covered with humiliation and misery, and they drew upon themselves the wrath of Allah. That was because they constantly rejected Allah’s signs and unjustly killed the prophets. All of this because they disobeyed and (willfully) exceeded all bounds.
Reflection: This verse follows the previous account where the people were miraculously given water. Here, it recounts another moment of discontent. Although they were divinely provided with heavenly food—manna and quails—they grew weary of it and asked Prophet Moses to request more variety from Allah, specifically everyday crops like cucumbers, garlic, lentils, and onions. In response, Moses questioned their desire to trade something extraordinary for ordinary food that was easily available in any town. This reflected a deeper attitude of ingratitude and a lack of appreciation for Allah’s unique blessings. Their ongoing defiance—including rejecting Allah’s signs, killing prophets without justification, and repeatedly breaking divine limits—brought upon them humiliation by others and an internal collapse of spirit and dignity. This verse highlights the contrast between Allah’s generous provision and the people's persistent dissatisfaction, emphasizing how ungrateful attitudes can lead to both external disgrace and inner defeat.

Verse 62

إِنَّ الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا وَالَّذِينَ هَادُوا وَالنَّصَارَىٰ وَالصَّابِئِينَ مَنْ آمَنَ بِاللَّهِ وَالْيَوْمِ الْآخِرِ وَعَمِلَ صَالِحًا فَلَهُمْ أَجْرُهُمْ عِندَ رَبِّهِمْ وَلَا خَوْفٌ عَلَيْهِمْ وَلَا هُمْ يَحْزَنُونَ
Translation: Indeed, the believers, the Jews, the Christians, and the Sabians—whoever (among them) believes in Allah and the Last Day and does righteous (deeds)—will have their reward with their Lord. They will have no fear, nor will they grieve.
Reflection: This verse marks a shift from recounting specific historical events to emphasizing a core principle in Islam regarding salvation. It challenges the assumption—held by some among the Children of Israel—that salvation is guaranteed solely by belonging to a particular group. They believed that their identity ensured a special and exclusive relationship with God, regardless of personal belief or conduct. The verse corrects this notion by affirming that what truly matters before Allah is sincere faith and righteous actions, not mere affiliation or labels. Salvation is accessible to all who believe in Allah, the Last Day, and do good deeds—whether they follow the Qur'an or earlier revelations, including the Jews, Christians, or Sabians. It underscores that Allah judges individuals based on merit, not on formal religious identity. Those who meet these conditions will be rewarded and will have no fear or sorrow in the Hereafter. This message stands in contrast to the previous verses, which outlined the consequences of disobedience during the time of Prophet Moses.

Verse 63

وَإِذْ أَخَذْنَا مِيثَاقَكُمْ وَرَفَعْنَا فَوْقَكُمُ الطُّورَ خُذُوا مَا آتَيْنَاكُم بِقُوَّةٍ وَاذْكُرُوا مَا فِيهِ لَعَلَّكُمْ تَتَّقُونَ
Translation: And (remember) when We took a solemn covenant from you (O Children of Israel, to uphold the Torah), and We raised the mountain above you, saying, “Hold firmly to what We have given you and follow its teachings, so that you may become mindful (of Allah).”
Reflection: Returning to historical accounts, this verse reminds the people of Prophet Moses about the solemn covenant Allah made with them. It mentions that Allah raised Mount Sinai above them as this covenant was given, an image emphasizing the seriousness and weight of the agreement. The command given was to hold firmly to what Allah had revealed (the Torah) and to constantly remember its contents, so that they might become God-fearing. This highlights the importance of commitment and remembrance in upholding one's pledge to Allah.

Verse 64

ثُمَّ تَوَلَّيْتُم مِّن بَعْدِ ذَٰلِكَ ۖ فَلَوْلَا فَضْلُ اللَّهِ عَلَيْكُمْ وَرَحْمَتُهُ لَكُنتُم مِّنَ الْخَاسِرِينَ
Translation: But even after that, you turned away. If not for the grace and mercy of Allah upon you, you would surely have been among the losers.
Reflection: Following the reminder of the solemn covenant in the previous verse, this verse states that despite the gravity of the pledge and the command to hold firmly to it, they turned away from it afterwards. Had it not been for Allah's grace and mercy towards them, they would have been among those who are ruined or lost. This emphasizes Allah's immense mercy.

Verse 65

وَلَقَدْ عَلِمْتُمُ الَّذِينَ اعْتَدَوْا مِنكُمْ فِي السَّبْتِ فَقُلْنَا لَهُمْ كُونُوا قِرَدَةً خَاسِئِينَ
Translation: And you surely know about those among you who violated the Sabbath. We said to them, “Be disgraced apes!”
Reflection: This verse recounts another act of disobedience, focusing on the violation of the Sabbath. Allah had designated Saturday as a day of rest for them, during which they were not permitted to work for their livelihood. As a test, fish would appear in abundance specifically on the Sabbath but not on other days. Motivated by greed, some among them devised ways to circumvent the command—setting traps on the Sabbath and collecting the fish afterward. As a consequence of this transgression, Allah described them as being turned into "despicable apes." Some scholars interpret this as a metaphorical transformation, indicating a moral and spiritual degradation due to their lack of self-discipline and defiance, rather than a literal physical change. This illustrates how their behavior led to their own debasement.

Verse 66

فَجَعَلْنَاهَا نَكَالًا لِّمَا بَيْنَ يَدَيْهَا وَمَا خَلْفَهَا وَمَوْعِظَةً لِّلْمُتَّقِينَ
Translation: We made (their fate) a warning for their own generation and those to come, and a lesson for those who are mindful of Allah.
Reflection: This verse explains the purpose behind the consequence described in the previous verse regarding the Sabbath breakers. Allah made this event a clear example for the people living at that time and for future generations. It serves as a warning and a lesson, particularly for those who are God-fearing, about the consequences of disobeying Allah's commands and prioritizing worldly gain over divine directives. It connects their historical experience to a timeless lesson for believers.

Verse 67

وَإِذْ قَالَ مُوسَىٰ لِقَوْمِهِ إِنَّ اللَّهَ يَأْمُرُكُمْ أَن تَذْبَحُوا بَقَرَةً ۖ قَالُوا أَتَتَّخِذُنَا هُزُوًا ۖ قَالَ أَعُوذُ بِاللَّهِ أَنْ أَكُونَ مِنَ الْجَاهِلِينَ
Translation: And (remember) when Moses said to his people, “Indeed, Allah commands you to sacrifice a cow.” They said, “Are you making a mockery of us?” Moses replied, “I seek refuge in Allah from (behaving) like the ignorant.”
Reflection: This verse begins the account of the cow (heifer) incident. Prophet Moses conveyed a simple command from Allah to his people: sacrifice a heifer. Instead of complying directly, their immediate reaction was to ask if he was making fun of them, showing a lack of trust or perhaps reluctance to accept the command. Prophet Moses responded by seeking refuge in Allah from being ignorant, indicating that mocking others, especially in the context of conveying divine commands, is an act of ignorance and unbefitting of someone who knows Allah's position. This initial exchange sets the tone for their subsequent interactions regarding this command.

Verse 68

قَالُوا ادْعُ لَنَا رَبَّكَ يُبَيِّن لَّنَا مَا هِيَ ۚ قَالَ إِنَّهُ يَقُولُ إِنَّهَا بَقَرَةٌ لَّا فَارِضٌ وَلَا بِكْرٌ عَوَانٌ بَيْنَ ذَٰلِكَ ۖ فَافْعَلُوا مَا تُؤْمَرُونَ
Translation: They said, “Ask your Lord to clarify for us what kind of cow it should be.” (Moses) replied, “(Allah) says: It should be neither old nor young, but in between. So do as you are commanded.”
Reflection: Following their initial question in the previous verse, the people of Prophet Moses asked him to pray to Allah to specify what kind of heifer they should sacrifice. This shows they were making the matter more complicated than the initial command required. Prophet Moses conveyed Allah's response: the heifer should be neither too old nor too young, but of an age in between. He then reiterated the command: "Now do what you are commanded!" Despite the clarification, they continued to seek more details rather than simply carrying out the task.

Verse 69

قَالُوا ادْعُ لَنَا رَبَّكَ يُبَيِّن لَّنَا مَا لَوْنُهَا ۚ قَالَ إِنَّهُ يَقُولُ إِنَّهَا بَقَرَةٌ صَفْرَاءُ فَاقِعٌ لَّوْنُهَا تَسُرُّ النَّاظِرِينَ
Translation: They said, “Ask your Lord to specify its color for us.” (Moses) replied, “(Allah) says: It should be a yellow cow—bright in color—pleasing to those who see it.”
Reflection: Continuing the dialogue from the previous verse, the people of Prophet Moses asked for further clarification, specifically about the color of the heifer. Prophet Moses conveyed Allah's response: it should be a yellow heifer, bright and pleasing in appearance. This specification further narrowed down the options and made finding the required animal more difficult. This question about color, following the question about age, illustrates their persistent questioning and tendency to make the simple divine command increasingly complex for themselves.

Verse 70

قَالُوا ادْعُ لَنَا رَبَّكَ يُبَيِّن لَّنَا مَا هِيَ إِنَّ الْبَقَرَ تَشَابَهَ عَلَيْنَا وَإِنَّا إِن شَاءَ اللَّهُ لَمُهْتَدُونَ
Translation: (Once again) they said, "Ask your Lord to make clear to us what she should be like, for to us, all cows seem alike. Then, if Allah wills, we will be rightly guided."
Reflection: This verse continues the account of the Israelites being commanded to sacrifice a cow. After receiving earlier instructions about the cow’s age and color (in the previous verses), they again ask Prophet Moses to seek more details from Allah. They claim that the cows appear too similar to them, making it hard to know which one to choose. They add that, if Allah wills, they will follow the guidance. This reflects their repeated hesitancy and over-questioning of a command that was meant to be simple. Instead of acting with trust and obedience, they sought increasing specificity, which only made the task more burdensome.

Verse 71

قَالَ إِنَّهُ يَقُولُ إِنَّهَا بَقَرَةٌ لَّا ذَلُولٌ تُثِيرُ الْأَرْضَ وَلَا تَسْقِي الْحَرْثَ مُسَلَّمَةٌ لَّا شِيَةَ فِيهَا ۚ قَالُوا الْآنَ جِئْتَ بِالْحَقِّ ۚ فَذَبَحُوهَا وَمَا كَادُوا يَفْعَلُونَ
Translation: He replied, “Allah says, ‘It should be a cow that has not been used to plough the land or to water the fields—one that is free from defects, with no blemish on her.’” They said, “Now you have brought the truth (the needed details).” So they sacrificed her—but they did so hesitantly.
Reflection: In response to their persistent questioning, Prophet Moses conveys the final and most detailed description from Allah: the cow must not have been used for agricultural work, such as ploughing or irrigation, and it must be entirely healthy—without any flaws or marks. Once they hear this, the Israelites acknowledge that the description is now complete and accurate. Only then do they carry out the sacrifice, but even so, they do it reluctantly. Their delay and hesitation, even after all the clarification, reflect a deeper unwillingness to submit to a straightforward command from Allah. This serves as a lesson about the consequences of overcomplicating what is meant to be a test of obedience and sincerity.

Verse 72

وَإِذْ قَتَلْتُمْ نَفْسًا فَادَّارَأْتُمْ فِيهَا ۖ وَاللَّهُ مُخْرِجٌ مَّا كُنتُمْ تَكْتُمُونَ
Translation: And (remember) when you killed a person and began to deflect the accusation among yourselves—but Allah was going to reveal what you were concealing.
Reflection: This verse gives the background to the unusual command to sacrifice a specific cow. It refers to an incident in which a man was murdered among the Israelites, and each group was trying to distance itself from responsibility. The real perpetrator remained hidden. Allah states that He intended to expose what they were concealing. This sets the stage for the next verse, where the sacrificed cow becomes the means through which the truth is revealed. The story shows that divine commands, even when they appear strange or overly detailed, often carry deeper wisdom—such as uncovering the truth and ensuring justice.

Verse 73

فَقُلْنَا اضْرِبُوهُ بِبَعْضِهَا ۚ كَذَٰلِكَ يُحْيِي اللَّهُ الْمَوْتَىٰ وَيُرِيكُمْ آيَاتِهِ لَعَلَّكُمْ تَعْقِلُونَ
Translation: So We said, “Strike him (the corpse of the murdered man) with a part of her (the sacrificed cow).” In this way, Allah brings the dead to life and shows you His signs, so that you may use reason.
Reflection: After the Israelites sacrificed the cow, as commanded, Allah instructed them to strike the corpse of the murdered man with a part of the sacrificed animal. When they did so, the man came back to life by Allah’s will and revealed the identity of his killer. This extraordinary event resolved the dispute mentioned in the previous verses. More importantly, it served as a powerful sign of Allah’s ability to bring the dead back to life. Through this miracle, Allah demonstrated His authority and wisdom, urging people to reflect and use their reason when faced with His signs.

Verse 74

ثُمَّ قَسَتْ قُلُوبُكُم مِّن بَعْدِ ذَٰلِكَ فَهِيَ كَالْحِجَارَةِ أَوْ أَشَدُّ قَسْوَةً ۚ وَإِنَّ مِنَ الْحِجَارَةِ لَمَا يَتَفَجَّرُ مِنْهُ الْأَنْهَارُ ۚ وَإِنَّ مِنْهَا لَمَا يَشَّقَّقُ فَيَخْرُجُ مِنْهُ الْمَاءُ ۚ وَإِنَّ مِنْهَا لَمَا يَهْبِطُ مِنْ خَشْيَةِ اللَّهِ ۗ وَمَا اللَّهُ بِغَافِلٍ عَمَّا تَعْمَلُونَ
Translation: Then (after all this) your hearts became hardened—like rocks, or even harder. For there are rocks from which rivers burst forth, others that crack open and release water, and some that fall down in awe of Allah. And Allah is never unaware of what you do.
Reflection: This verse reflects on the spiritual state of the Israelites after witnessing the miracle of a dead man being brought back to life through Allah’s command. Despite seeing such a clear sign, their hearts remained unmoved. The verse powerfully compares their hearts to rocks—or even harder. It points out that even rocks, though inanimate, can show signs of yielding: some allow rivers to gush from them, others break open and release water, and some fall in submission out of awe of Allah. In contrast, their hearts showed no such softness or humility. The verse ends with a reminder that nothing escapes Allah’s awareness, including the condition of the human heart and what lies within it.

Verse 75

أَفَتَطْمَعُونَ أَن يُؤْمِنُوا لَكُمْ وَقَدْ كَانَ فَرِيقٌ مِّنْهُمْ يَسْمَعُونَ كَلَامَ اللَّهِ ثُمَّ يُحَرِّفُونَهُ مِن بَعْدِ مَا عَقَلُوهُ وَهُمْ يَعْلَمُونَ
Translation: Do you then hope that they will believe (in your message), when a group of them used to hear the words of Allah and then distort them after understanding them—knowingly?
Reflection: This verse addresses the believers, prompting reflection on whether they should expect that some among the Children of Israel will accept their message. It highlights a historical pattern where some individuals from among them, even after hearing and understanding the Divine word, deliberately altered its meaning. This deliberate distortion, despite clear understanding, casts doubt on the likelihood of them embracing the new message with sincerity, suggesting a deep-seated reluctance to accept truth that contradicts their desires.

Verse 76

وَإِذَا لَقُوا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا قَالُوا آمَنَّا وَإِذَا خَلَا بَعْضُهُمْ إِلَىٰ بَعْضٍ قَالُوا أَتُحَدِّثُونَهُم بِمَا فَتَحَ اللَّهُ عَلَيْكُمْ لِيُحَاجُّوكُم بِهِ عِندَ رَبِّكُمْ ۚ أَفَلَا تَعْقِلُونَ
Translation: And when they meet the believers, they say, "We believe." But when they are alone with one another, they say, "Do you inform them of what Allah has disclosed to you, so that they may use it as an argument against you before your Lord?" Do you not understand?
Reflection: Following from the previous verse which mentioned distortion, this verse describes the two-faced behavior of some individuals. When they encounter believers, they outwardly claim to believe. However, in private, they express concern about revealing knowledge from their own scriptures that might support the Muslim claims, fearing it could be used against them in arguments before Allah. This illustrates a level of calculated hypocrisy and a desire to conceal truths that might validate the message brought by Prophet Muhammad. It questions the believers' understanding of this deceptive strategy.

Verse 77

أَوَلَا يَعْلَمُونَ أَنَّ اللَّهَ يَعْلَمُ مَا يُسِرُّونَ وَمَا يُعْلِنُونَ
Translation: Do they not know that Allah is fully aware of (all) that they conceal and (all) that they reveal?
Reflection: This verse directly follows and responds to the scheming described in the previous one. It serves as a rhetorical question directed at those who plot and hide information. It emphasizes that Allah is fully aware of everything, whether it is openly declared or kept secret in their hearts and private gatherings. This reminds everyone that attempts to conceal truth or maintain hypocrisy are ultimately futile before the All-Knowing.

Verse 78

وَمِنْهُمْ أُمِّيُّونَ لَا يَعْلَمُونَ الْكِتَابَ إِلَّا أَمَانِيَّ وَإِنْ هُمْ إِلَّا يَظُنُّونَ
Translation: And among them are unlettered people who know nothing of the Scripture except false hopes, and they do nothing but speculate.
Reflection: Moving from the learned individuals discussed earlier, this verse introduces another group among the Children of Israel: those who are unlettered and do not truly understand their scriptures. Their understanding is based on speculation and personal desires rather than divine knowledge. This highlights that their misconceptions and expectations (as seen in subsequent verses) stem not from scriptural understanding but from wishful thinking and assumptions.

Verse 79

فَوَيْلٌ لِّلَّذِينَ يَكْتُبُونَ الْكِتَابَ بِأَيْدِيهِمْ ثُمَّ يَقُولُونَ هَـٰذَا مِنْ عِندِ اللَّهِ لِيَشْتَرُوا بِهِ ثَمَنًا قَلِيلًا ۖ فَوَيْلٌ لَّهُم مِّمَّا كَتَبَتْ أَيْدِيهِمْ وَوَيْلٌ لَّهُم مِّمَّا يَكْسِبُونَ
Translation: So woe to those who write the Scripture with their own hands and then say, “This is from Allah,” in order to gain a small worldly profit! So woe to them for what their hands have written, and woe to them for what they have earned.
Reflection: This verse turns attention to a more serious offense—deliberate distortion by those who hold positions of knowledge and authority. Unlike the unlettered people mentioned earlier, this group knowingly fabricates content, writing with their own hands and falsely attributing it to Allah. Their motivation is to secure some short-term, worldly benefit. The verse delivers a triple condemnation: for forging the scripture, for falsely claiming it is divine, and for the material gain they pursue through this deception. It is a stern warning against manipulating sacred texts for personal advantage, highlighting both the moral and spiritual consequences.

Verse 80

وَقَالُوا لَن تَمَسَّنَا النَّارُ إِلَّا أَيَّامًا مَّعْدُودَةً ۚ قُلْ أَتَّخَذْتُمْ عِندَ اللَّهِ عَهْدًا فَلَن يُخْلِفَ اللَّهُ عَهْدَهُ ۖ أَمْ تَقُولُونَ عَلَى اللَّهِ مَا لَا تَعْلَمُونَ
Translation: And they say, “The Fire will not touch us except for a (limited) number of days.” Say, (O Muhammad), “Have you taken a promise from Allah? For Allah never breaks His promise. Or are you saying about Allah what you do not know?”
Reflection: This verse addresses a claim made by some among the Children of Israel: that even if they were to be punished in the Hereafter, it would only be for a few days. This belief likely stemmed from a sense of entitlement or a mistaken understanding of their status with Allah. The verse instructs the Prophet Muhammad to challenge this claim by asking whether they have a clear and reliable promise from Allah—something He never breaks—or whether they are simply making baseless assumptions about Him. The verse highlights the danger of speaking about Allah without knowledge and relying on unfounded hope instead of truth.

Verse 81

بَلَىٰ مَن كَسَبَ سَيِّئَةً وَأَحَاطَتْ بِهِ خَطِيئَتُهُ فَأُولَـٰئِكَ أَصْحَابُ النَّارِ ۖ هُمْ فِيهَا خَالِدُونَ
Translation: No! Whoever commits evil and becomes engulfed by their sins—they will be the inhabitants of the Fire, and they will remain there forever.
Reflection: This verse offers a clear and decisive response to the false sense of security mentioned in the previous verse. It establishes that eternal punishment in the Fire is not avoided through identity or heritage, but is the consequence for those who persist in wrongdoing until it defines them. The phrase “engulfed by their sins” implies not occasional error, but a life consumed by rebellion and moral corruption. This verse affirms the universal principle that one’s ultimate fate is tied to their actions, not assumptions or affiliations.

Verse 82

وَالَّذِينَ آمَنُوا وَعَمِلُوا الصَّالِحَاتِ أُولَـٰئِكَ أَصْحَابُ الْجَنَّةِ ۖ هُمْ فِيهَا خَالِدُونَ
Translation: (Whereas) those who believe and do righteous deeds—they will be the inhabitants of Paradise, and they will remain there forever.
Reflection: Following the previous verse's description of the fate of evildoers, this verse presents the contrasting outcome for those who believe and perform good deeds. It states that such individuals are destined for the Garden (Paradise) and will reside there eternally. This highlights the fundamental Islamic principle that salvation and eternal bliss are attained through genuine faith accompanied by righteous actions.

Verse 83

وَإِذْ أَخَذْنَا مِيثَاقَ بَنِي إِسْرَائِيلَ لَا تَعْبُدُونَ إِلَّا اللَّهَ وَبِالْوَالِدَيْنِ إِحْسَانًا وَذِي الْقُرْبَىٰ وَالْيَتَامَىٰ وَالْمَسَاكِينِ وَقُولُوا لِلنَّاسِ حُسْنًا وَأَقِيمُوا الصَّلَاةَ وَآتُوا الزَّكَاةَ ثُمَّ تَوَلَّيْتُمْ إِلَّا قَلِيلًا مِّنكُمْ وَأَنتُم مُّعْرِضُونَ
Translation: And (remember) when We took a covenant from the Children of Israel, (saying), "Worship none but Allah; and be kind to parents, relatives, orphans, and the needy. Speak kindly to people, establish prayer, and give the alms-tax (zakah)." But you turned away—except for a few of you—and paid no attention.
Reflection: This verse shifts focus to the covenant Allah took from the Children of Israel, which included essential moral and spiritual principles: worshipping only Allah, showing kindness to relatives, orphans, and the needy, speaking kindly to all, establishing prayer, and giving charity. These universal values affirm that Allah’s religion has remained consistent throughout human history, with Islam confirming and upholding the core teachings of earlier revelations. However, the verse points out that despite agreeing to this covenant, most turned away, with only a few remaining faithful—illustrating a recurring pattern of breaking their commitment to Allah. This sets the stage for the discussion of further violations to follow.

Verse 84

وَإِذْ أَخَذْنَا مِيثَاقَكُمْ لَا تَسْفِكُونَ دِمَاءَكُمْ وَلَا تُخْرِجُونَ أَنفُسَكُم مِّن دِيَارِكُمْ ثُمَّ أَقْرَرْتُمْ وَأَنتُمْ تَشْهَدُونَ
Translation: And (remember) when We took your covenant, (saying), "Do not shed each other's blood, nor expel one another from your homes." Then you acknowledged it and bore witness to it.
Reflection: Continuing the discussion of the covenant mentioned in the previous verse, this verse highlights specific prohibitions that were part of it: they were forbidden from shedding each other's blood and from expelling their own people from their homes. It emphasizes that they formally agreed to this covenant and could even bear witness to its terms. This sets the stage for the following verses which will contrast this solemn pledge with their later actions where they engaged in violence and expulsion against their own people.

Verse 85

ثُمَّ أَنتُمْ هَـٰؤُلَاءِ تَقْتُلُونَ أَنفُسَكُمْ وَتُخْرِجُونَ فَرِيقًا مِّنكُم مِّن دِيَارِهِمْ تَظَاهَرُونَ عَلَيْهِم بِالْإِثْمِ وَالْعُدْوَانِ وَإِن يَأْتُوكُمْ أُسَارَىٰ تُفَادُوهُمْ وَهُوَ مُحَرَّمٌ عَلَيْكُمْ إِخْرَاجُهُمْ ۚ أَفَتُؤْمِنُونَ بِبَعْضِ الْكِتَابِ وَتَكْفُرُونَ بِبَعْضٍ ۚ فَمَا جَزَاءُ مَن يَفْعَلُ ذَٰلِكَ مِنكُمْ إِلَّا خِزْيٌ فِي الْحَيَاةِ الدُّنْيَا ۖ وَيَوْمَ الْقِيَامَةِ يُرَدُّونَ إِلَىٰ أَشَدِّ الْعَذَابِ ۗ وَمَا اللَّهُ بِغَافِلٍ عَمَّا تَعْمَلُونَ
Translation: But here you are—killing one another and expelling a group of your own people from their homes, cooperating against them in sin and aggression. Yet when they come to you as captives, you ransom them—even though it was forbidden for you to expel them (in the first place). So do you believe in part of the Scripture and reject part? Then what is the reward for those among you who do this, except disgrace in this worldly life? And on the Day of Judgment, they will be subjected to the most severe punishment. And Allah is never unaware of what you do.
Reflection: This verse exposes the contradiction in the actions of some among the Children of Israel. Despite being bound by a divine covenant not to kill each other or expel one another from their homes, they violated these very commands. They not only committed such acts themselves but also cooperated with others in carrying them out. Yet, when their own people were later taken as captives, they followed the command to ransom them—demonstrating selective adherence to the Scripture. The verse challenges this inconsistency: how can one believe in only part of Allah’s revelation while disregarding the rest? It warns that those who engage in such selective belief will face humiliation in this life and the harshest punishment in the next. It closes by affirming that nothing escapes Allah’s knowledge, reinforcing the seriousness of their betrayal.

Verse 86

أُولَـٰئِكَ الَّذِينَ اشْتَرَوُا الْحَيَاةَ الدُّنْيَا بِالْآخِرَةِ ۖ فَلَا يُخَفَّفُ عَنْهُمُ الْعَذَابُ وَلَا هُمْ يُنصَرُونَ
Translation: These are the ones who have exchanged the Hereafter for the (temporary) life of this world. So their punishment will not be lightened, nor will they be helped.
Reflection: Building on the previous verse’s condemnation of selective obedience and covenant-breaking, this verse exposes the root cause of such behavior: the preference for worldly gain over eternal reward. By choosing the fleeting pleasures of this life instead of remaining faithful to Allah’s commands, they have effectively traded away the Hereafter. As a consequence, they are warned of a punishment that will not be reduced, and they will receive no help or support. This verse serves as a sobering reminder of the irreversible cost of sacrificing long-term truth for short-term benefit.

Verse 87

وَلَقَدْ آتَيْنَا مُوسَى الْكِتَابَ وَقَفَّيْنَا مِن بَعْدِهِ بِالرُّسُلِ ۖ وَآتَيْنَا عِيسَى ابْنَ مَرْيَمَ الْبَيِّنَاتِ وَأَيَّدْنَاهُ بِرُوحِ الْقُدُسِ ۗ أَفَكُلَّمَا جَاءَكُمْ رَسُولٌ بِمَا لَا تَهْوَىٰ أَنفُسُكُمُ اسْتَكْبَرْتُمْ فَفَرِيقًا كَذَّبْتُمْ وَفَرِيقًا تَقْتُلُونَ
Translation: Indeed, We gave Moses the Book, and after him We sent successive messengers. And We gave Jesus, son of Mary, clear signs and supported him with the holy spirit. (So) is it that whenever a messenger came to you, (O Children of Israel), with something you did not desire, you grew arrogant—rejecting some and killing others?
Reflection: This verse reminds the Children of Israel of the consistent divine guidance they were given throughout history. It began with Moses, who was entrusted with the Scripture, and continued through a line of messengers. Among them was Jesus, son of Mary, who came with undeniable signs and was strengthened by the holy spirit—understood by many scholars as the Angel Gabriel. Despite this remarkable chain of prophets and revelations, their pattern of response was one of pride and resistance. When a messenger delivered a message they did not wish to hear, they would reject him—some they denied outright, and others they even killed. This verse exposes the deep-rooted tendency to oppose divine truth when it conflicts with personal expectations, setting the stage for how they would later respond to Prophet Muhammad, peace be upon him.

Verse 88

وَقَالُوا قُلُوبُنَا غُلْفٌ ۚ بَل لَّعَنَهُمُ اللَّهُ بِكُفْرِهِمْ فَقَلِيلًا مَّا يُؤْمِنُونَ
Translation: And they said, "Our hearts are sealed (wrapped/unreceptive)." In fact, Allah has cursed (condemned) them for their disbelief—so they have little faith.
Reflection: This verse recounts a common claim made by some among the Children of Israel when confronted with the message of the Qur’an and the prophethood of Muhammad (peace be upon him). They said, “Our hearts are sealed,” suggesting that they were either already content with their beliefs or unable to accept anything new. However, the verse rejects this excuse, stating that their spiritual state was not beyond their control—it was the result of their own disbelief. Because they turned away from the truth, Allah distanced them. The verse concludes by noting that their faith, if any, is minimal. This serves to expose the hollowness of their justification and prepares the ground for further criticism in the next verses.

Verse 89

وَلَمَّا جَاءَهُمْ كِتَابٌ مِّنْ عِندِ اللَّهِ مُصَدِّقٌ لِّمَا مَعَهُمْ وَكَانُوا مِن قَبْلُ يَسْتَفْتِحُونَ عَلَى الَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا فَلَمَّا جَاءَهُم مَّا عَرَفُوا كَفَرُوا بِهِ ۚ فَلَعْنَةُ اللَّهِ عَلَى الْكَافِرِينَ
Translation: And when there came to them a Book from Allah (the Qur’an), confirming what they already had (of the Scripture)— and although before it came, they used to pray (for a prophet to come and help them) to gain victory over the disbelievers— but when there came to them what they recognized, they disbelieved in it. So may Allah’s curse be upon the disbelievers.
Reflection: This verse points out another inconsistency in the behavior of some among the Children of Israel regarding the coming of the Qur'an. They had previously anticipated the arrival of a prophet and a book that would confirm their scriptures, and had even spoken about this with the pagan Arabs, expecting that this would bring them victory over the disbelievers. However, when the Qur'an came through Prophet Muhammad—from the Arabs, not from among them—and fulfilled the description of a book confirming their own scriptures, they refused to believe in it, despite recognizing its truth. The verse condemns this rejection of something they knew to be true, and declares Allah's curse upon those who actively disbelieve. It highlights that their denial was not out of ignorance, but a deliberate rejection of the truth, linking their earlier hope to their later refusal.

Verse 90

بِئْسَمَا اشْتَرَوْا بِهِ أَنفُسَهُمْ أَن يَكْفُرُوا بِمَا أَنزَلَ اللَّهُ بَغْيًا أَن يُنَزِّلَ اللَّهُ مِن فَضْلِهِ عَلَىٰ مَن يَشَاءُ مِنْ عِبَادِهِ ۖ فَبَاءُوا بِغَضَبٍ عَلَىٰ غَضَبٍ ۚ وَلِلْكَافِرِينَ عَذَابٌ مُّهِينٌ
Translation: How wretched is what they sold their souls for: (they did so by) disbelieving in Allah’s revelation (the Qur’an), out of envy that Allah would send down His grace upon whomever He wills from among His servants. So they have earned wrath upon wrath. And (such) disbelievers will suffer a humiliating punishment.
Reflection: This verse highlights the deep spiritual failure of those who rejected the Qur’an and exposes the real motive behind their denial. They traded away faith for disbelief at a terrible price. The verse points to envy as their driving reason: they resented that Allah, in His grace, chose to reveal the message to someone outside their own group—namely, Prophet Muhammad, peace be upon him. This envy led them to deny a truth they recognized. As a result, they brought upon themselves repeated expressions of Allah’s wrath. The verse emphasizes that their rejection wasn’t due to ignorance, but to arrogance and jealousy, and that such a mindset leads to a humiliating punishment in the Hereafter.

Verse 91

وَإِذَا قِيلَ لَهُمْ آمِنُوا بِمَا أَنزَلَ اللَّهُ قَالُوا نُؤْمِنُ بِمَا أُنزِلَ عَلَيْنَا وَيَكْفُرُونَ بِمَا وَرَاءَهُ وَهُوَ الْحَقُّ مُصَدِّقًا لِّمَا مَعَهُمْ ۗ قُلْ فَلِمَ تَقْتُلُونَ أَنبِيَاءَ اللَّهِ مِن قَبْلُ إِن كُنتُم مُّؤْمِنِينَ
Translation: When it is said to them, “Believe in what Allah has revealed,” they say, “We believe in what was sent down to us,” but they reject what came afterwards— even though it is the truth, confirming what is (already) with them! Say (O Muhammad), “Then why did you kill Allah’s prophets before, if you are truly believers?”
Reflection: This verse presents the argument used by some among the Children of Israel when called upon to believe in the Qur'an: "We believe in what was sent down to us," referring to their own scriptures. However, as the verse points out, they rejected everything else, including the Qur'an, even though it is the truth and confirms the revelations they already possess—highlighting the inconsistency in their stance. Prophet Muhammad is instructed to challenge their claim by asking, "Then why did you kill Allah’s prophets before, if you are (truly) believers?" This powerful question, based on their own history, directly contradicts their claim of faith in their scriptures and prophets, preparing the way for the following verses which give specific examples.

Verse 92

وَلَقَدْ جَاءَكُم مُّوسَىٰ بِالْبَيِّنَاتِ ثُمَّ اتَّخَذْتُمُ الْعِجْلَ مِن بَعْدِهِ وَأَنتُمْ ظَالِمُونَ
Translation: And indeed, Moses came to you with clear signs, yet (in his absence) you took the calf in worship and acted wrongfully.
Reflection: Continuing the challenge posed in the previous verse ("Then why did you kill Allah’s prophets, if you were true believers?"), this verse presents a specific historical example that contradicts the claim of sincere faith. It reminds them that Prophet Moses came with clear signs and undeniable proofs of his prophethood and Allah’s message. Nevertheless, during his brief absence, they worshipped the calf. This act—worshipping something other than Allah after receiving clear guidance—directly undermines their claim of believing in the revelations given to them. It serves as further evidence against their assertion that they consistently followed what was revealed to them.

Verse 93

وَإِذْ أَخَذْنَا مِيثَاقَكُمْ وَرَفَعْنَا فَوْقَكُمُ الطُّورَ خُذُوا مَا آتَيْنَاكُم بِقُوَّةٍ وَاسْمَعُوا ۖ قَالُوا سَمِعْنَا وَعَصَيْنَا وَأُشْرِبُوا فِي قُلُوبِهِمُ الْعِجْلَ بِكُفْرِهِمْ ۚ قُلْ بِئْسَمَا يَأْمُرُكُم بِهِ إِيمَانُكُمْ إِن كُنتُم مُّؤْمِنِينَ
Translation: And (remember) when We took your covenant and raised the mountain above you, (saying), “Hold firmly to what We have given you and listen (attentively).” They said, “We hear, (yet) we disobey.” And their hearts were infused with love for the (golden) calf because of their disbelief. Say (O Muhammad), “How wretched is what your (so-called) belief leads you to do, if you are (truly) believers!”
Reflection: This verse presents another historical example—again from the time of Prophet Moses—that challenges the claim of true belief made in Verse 91. It refers to the moment when Allah took a solemn covenant from the Children of Israel at Mount Sinai, raising the mountain above them as a powerful sign to compel seriousness and obedience. They were commanded: “Hold firmly to what We have given you and listen.” Yet their real response, despite outward acceptance, was: “We hear, (yet) we disobey.” This contradiction exposes the insincerity behind their claim that they only rejected the Prophet Muhammad because they follow what was previously revealed to them, such as the Torah. If that were true, why did they also reject the message of Moses and oppose the prophets who came after him—even resorting to violence? The verse further explains the deep emotional attachment they had to the golden calf. The Arabic word translated as “infused” literally means to make something drink, but here it is used metaphorically to express how the love of the calf permeated their hearts—deeply, thoroughly, and obsessively. Their admiration stemmed not only from its form but from their belief in its divinity and benefit. The fact that it was made of gold intensified their attachment, reflecting their excessive love for wealth. Allah states that this happened “because of their disbelief”—meaning their lack of faith allowed this idolatry to settle so deeply within them. A believer becomes more attached to what they believe in, while disbelief opens the heart to false devotion. In the final part of the verse, Prophet Muhammad is instructed to expose the contradiction in their stance: If your faith truly leads you to such disobedience and idol worship, how wretched is that so-called faith! This verse not only highlights the gap between their claim and their actions but also prepares the reader for the next challenge about their view of the hereafter.

Verse 94

قُلْ إِن كَانَتْ لَكُمُ الدَّارُ الْآخِرَةُ عِندَ اللَّهِ خَالِصَةً مِّن دُونِ النَّاسِ فَتَمَنَّوُا الْمَوْتَ إِن كُنتُمْ صَادِقِينَ
Translation: Say (O Muhammad), "If the (eternal) home of the Hereafter with Allah is exclusively for you (O Children of Israel), and not for anyone else, then wish for death—if you are truthful!"
Reflection: This verse responds to a claim made by some among the Children of Israel—that Paradise, the eternal home with Allah, is reserved solely for them. Such a claim was not only an assertion of superiority but also a way to cast doubt on the legitimacy of Islam and discourage others from embracing it. In response, Allah instructs Prophet Muhammad to challenge them directly: “If the Hereafter with Allah is truly yours alone, then wish for death—if you are truthful.” The reasoning is clear: if they were confident in their exclusive right to Paradise, they would not fear death, since it would lead them to their promised reward. Yet their unwillingness to make such a wish reveals the hollowness of their claim. This verse exposes the inconsistency between their self-proclaimed status and their actual behavior. Their history of breaking covenants and rejecting Allah’s messengers casts serious doubt on their assertion of being uniquely favored. The challenge, therefore, highlights a deeper point: true nearness to Allah is not inherited or claimed, but reflected through sincerity, obedience, and faith.

Verse 95

وَلَن يَتَمَنَّوْهُ أَبَدًا بِمَا قَدَّمَتْ أَيْدِيهِمْ ۗ وَاللَّهُ عَلِيمٌ بِالظَّالِمِينَ
Translation: But they will never wish for it, because of what their hands have sent ahead (i.e., their past deeds). And Allah is fully aware of the wrongdoers.
Reflection: This verse follows the challenge issued in the previous verse: if the Children of Israel truly believed that Paradise is reserved solely for them, they should wish for death. Here, the Qur'an reveals why they do not. It explains that they will never wish for death, because deep down they are aware of the wrongs they have committed—their past actions have made them fearful of what awaits them in the Hereafter. The phrase “what their hands have sent ahead” refers to their deeds, especially those that defy the guidance given to them—such as rejecting prophets, breaking covenants, and engaging in idolatry. These actions weigh on their conscience, even if their outward words suggest confidence. The verse ends by affirming that Allah has complete knowledge of all wrongdoers. It serves as a reminder that no action escapes Allah’s awareness, and that people are accountable for what they do, whether they acknowledge it or not.

Verse 96

وَلَتَجِدَنَّهُمْ أَحْرَصَ النَّاسِ عَلَىٰ حَيَاةٍ وَمِنَ الَّذِينَ أَشْرَكُوا ۚ يَوَدُّ أَحَدُهُمْ لَوْ يُعَمَّرُ أَلْفَ سَنَةٍ وَمَا هُوَ بِمُزَحْزِحِهِ مِنَ الْعَذَابِ أَن يُعَمَّرَ ۗ وَاللَّهُ بَصِيرٌ بِمَا يَعْمَلُونَ
Translation: And you will surely find them clinging to life more eagerly than any other people—even more than those who associate others with Allah. Any one of them wishes to live a thousand years, but even that would not save them from the punishment (of the Hereafter). And Allah is All-Seeing of what they do.
Reflection: This verse continues the theme from the previous two verses, which exposed the contradiction between the Children of Israel’s claim to exclusive salvation and their fear of death. Here, their deep attachment to worldly life is highlighted—they are described as being more eager to hold on to life than anyone else, even more than those who openly associate others with Allah. The verse notes that some among them wish for extremely long lives—as long as a thousand years—as if longevity could shield them from divine judgment. But the verse makes it clear: even such an extended lifespan would not protect them from punishment if their deeds warrant it. This serves as a reminder that length of life does not equal righteousness or security in the Hereafter. What matters is one’s faith and conduct. The verse closes with a reaffirmation: Allah is fully aware of all their actions. Nothing is hidden, and their fate is not determined by time, but by truth and accountability.

Verse 97

قُلْ مَن كَانَ عَدُوًّا لِّجِبْرِيلَ فَإِنَّهُ نَزَّلَهُ عَلَىٰ قَلْبِكَ بِإِذْنِ اللَّهِ مُصَدِّقًا لِّمَا بَيْنَ يَدَيْهِ وَهُدًى وَبُشْرَىٰ لِلْمُؤْمِنِينَ
Translation: Say (O Muhammad), “Whoever is an enemy to Gabriel (should know) that he has brought this (Qur’an) down upon your heart by Allah’s permission, confirming what came before it, and as guidance and glad tidings for the believers.”
Reflection: This verse continues the response to the false claims and objections made by some among the Jews of Medina. It begins with the directive "Say", instructing Prophet Muhammad to answer their objection and expose their underlying motives. In earlier verses, their excuses were systematically dismantled: Their claim of honoring only their own messengers was proven false by their history of rejecting, harming, and even killing them. Their claim of deep devotion to previous revelations was exposed as insincere when they refused to wish for death despite claiming exclusive access to Paradise. Their supposed theological objection to accepting Prophet Muhammad was shown to be rooted not in principle, but in envy. This verse now addresses another excuse they raised: hostility toward the Angel Gabriel. Some among the Jews in Medina claimed enmity toward Gabriel, considering him their enemy. This was a deeply irrational stance, especially since they still acknowledged Gabriel as an angel sent by Allah. Their hostility is said to have intensified when Gabriel brought the Qur’an to Prophet Muhammad. Some scholars also report that their enmity had historical roots—because Gabriel was known to bring punishment upon earlier nations that rejected their prophets. But whatever the reason, their reaction reflected confusion and inconsistency in belief. The verse makes it clear that Gabriel acts solely by Allah’s command. He revealed the Qur’an “upon your heart by Allah’s permission”—meaning that he is a faithful messenger, not an independent agent. Therefore, enmity toward him is effectively enmity toward Allah. The verse also affirms that the Qur’an: Confirms the previous scriptures (like the Torah and the Gospel), Is a source of guidance, And brings glad tidings for the believers. The phrase "confirming what came before it" refers to the Qur’an affirming the core truths found in earlier revelations, which were still present and acknowledged among their followers at the time. Though those scriptures had been altered in parts, their divine origin and many of their teachings were still recognized—and the Qur’an came as both a confirmation and a clarification. The phrase "guidance and glad tidings" emphasizes two key roles of the Qur’an: it shows the path to righteousness and reassures the believers of Allah’s pleasure and the promise of success in both this life and the Hereafter. From this verse, five qualities of the Qur’an can be identified: it was revealed by Allah’s permission, it was sent directly to the Prophet’s heart—indicating sincerity and deep internalization, it confirms earlier revelations, it serves as unmatched guidance and it brings joyful news to the believers. Thus, the Qur’an is praised for the nobility of its origin, the honor of its method of revelation, the truth it upholds, and the immense good it brings to those who follow it—with benefits both immediate and eternal.

Verse 98

مَن كَانَ عَدُوًّا لِّلَّهِ وَمَلَائِكَتِهِ وَرُسُلِهِ وَجِبْرِيلَ وَمِيكَالَ فَإِنَّ اللَّهَ عَدُوٌّ لِّلْكَافِرِينَ
Translation: Whoever is an enemy to Allah, His angels, His messengers, Gabriel, and Michael—then indeed, Allah is an enemy to the disbelievers.
Reflection: Following directly from the previous verse, which addressed hostility toward the angel Gabriel, this verse expands the message. It makes it clear that opposition to Gabriel is not an isolated issue but part of a larger pattern of enmity toward the core pillars of faith. The verse lists Allah, His angels, His messengers, Gabriel, and Michael—signifying that enmity toward any of them places a person in opposition to Allah Himself. The explicit mention of Gabriel and Michael highlights their special significance and corrects the false distinctions made by some of the Jews in Medina. They claimed to hate Gabriel, calling him the angel of destruction, while favoring Michael, whom they associated with fertility and peace. But the verse denies any such division, affirming that faith requires equal reverence for all whom Allah has chosen. This verse also clarifies that hostility toward one messenger or angel is effectively hostility toward all, because it shows rejection of the role they collectively fulfill. Gabriel was only delivering revelation, which is a core function of angels; likewise, Prophet Muhammad was fulfilling the same prophetic mission as earlier messengers. To reject either of them is to reject the divine system they represent. By ending with “then indeed, Allah is an enemy to the disbelievers,” the verse states a general rule. The word “disbelievers” is used broadly to include all who show this kind of hostility—making the ruling universal rather than limited to a specific group. The verse asserts that Allah’s enmity is directed toward them not simply because of individual actions, but because their rejection reflects a deeper disbelief in the truth. This serves as both a theological clarification and a firm warning: one cannot claim selective belief or selective loyalty in matters of faith. True belief requires consistency in accepting all that Allah has sent, through all His messengers and angels.

Verse 99

وَلَقَدْ أَنزَلْنَا إِلَيْكَ آيَاتٍ بَيِّنَاتٍ ۖ وَمَا يَكْفُرُ بِهَا إِلَّا الْفَاسِقُونَ
Translation: Indeed, We have sent down to you (O Muhammad) clear revelations. But none reject them except those who defiantly deviate (from the truth).
Reflection: This verse offers reassurance to Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him) in the face of denial and rejection. It reminds him that the revelations he receives are clear—they contain unmistakable evidence, guidance, and signs that should lead to belief. The shift in address toward the Prophet also carries a tone of consolation. It affirms that the rejection he faces is not a reflection of any flaw in the message, but rather a reflection of the character of those who deny it. The truth is evident—but some persist in turning away from it, despite its clarity. The verse describes these deniers using the Arabic word fāsiqūn, which refers to those who have broken away from the bounds of moral or spiritual integrity. The word originally means to split or break away, and in Islamic usage it refers to someone who consistently violates the commands of Allah—someone who has willfully deviated from the path of righteousness. Thus, disbelief in these clear revelations is not portrayed as a matter of confusion or misunderstanding, but as the result of stubborn corruption. It reflects a habitual, ongoing attitude of rebellion against the truth. This behavior is not accidental—it is upheld, entrenched, and actively maintained. The verse sets the tone for what follows by defining the mindset of those who reject revelation: their refusal is not due to any lack of clarity in the message, but to a deep-rooted resistance to the truth. Opposition to it has become part of who they are.

Verse 100

أَوَكُلَّمَا عَاهَدُوا عَهْدًا نَّبَذَهُ فَرِيقٌ مِّنْهُم ۚ بَلْ أَكْثَرُهُمْ لَا يُؤْمِنُونَ
Translation: Why is it that every time they make a covenant, a group of them throws it aside? In fact, most of them do not (truly) believe.
Reflection: Following the previous verse, which described those who reject clear signs, this verse highlights a recurring pattern of behavior among some from the People of the Book—specifically, the Israelites. The verse refers to their repeated breaking of covenants made with their prophets and with Allah. The rhetorical question—Why is it that every time they make a covenant, a group of them throws it aside?—points to a troubling inconsistency in their religious commitment. The verb "throws it aside" is metaphorical, derived from the literal meaning of casting something from the hand. It suggests carelessness or deliberate rejection. Just as "holding on" to a covenant symbolizes loyalty and faithfulness, "throwing it aside" represents betrayal and disregard. The verse concludes by stating a deeper truth: most of them do not (truly) believe. This is not a blanket statement about every individual, but a general characterization of a dominant tendency. Their lack of sincere belief is presented as the root cause of their repeated disloyalty. This prepares the reader for the next verse, which offers a concrete example of this attitude in action.

Verse 101

وَلَمَّا جَاءَهُمْ رَسُولٌ مِّنْ عِندِ اللَّهِ مُصَدِّقٌ لِّمَا مَعَهُمْ نَبَذَ فَرِيقٌ مِّنَ الَّذِينَ أُوتُوا الْكِتَابَ كِتَابَ اللَّهِ وَرَاءَ ظُهُورِهِمْ كَأَنَّهُمْ لَا يَعْلَمُونَ
Translation: And (now) that a messenger from Allah has come to them—confirming what they already have (in their own Scripture)—some of the People of the Book threw the Book of Allah behind their backs, as if they did not know.
Reflection: This verse offers a specific example of the pattern described in the previous verse: breaking covenants and rejecting truth. It refers to the arrival of Prophet Muhammad—sent by Allah—who came confirming the scriptures that the People of the Book already possessed. Rather than recognizing and accepting this confirmation, some among them reject it. The verse uses a striking metaphor: they threw the Book of Allah behind their backs. This powerful image conveys complete disregard and rejection. In Arabic, turning one's back on something is a common way to express denial or disrespect, and the phrase "behind their backs" intensifies that meaning. It implies not only that they turned away from it, but that they deliberately placed it out of sight—no longer something they cared to engage with or uphold. The verse adds: as if they did not know. This expression is meant as a rebuke. It suggests that they do, in fact, know the truth—that the Qur’an is a continuation and confirmation of earlier revelation. Or it may imply that they are acting as though they are unaware of what is already written in the Torah, including the prophecies of a messenger to come from the descendants of Ishmael. In either case, the verse criticizes a willful disregard of both the new revelation and the foundations of their own scripture. Their rejection of the Qur’an is not portrayed as the result of misunderstanding or ignorance, but as a conscious denial—one that goes against the knowledge and guidance they had already received. It highlights how rejecting the new message is, in essence, a rejection of the very principles they claim to follow.

Verse 102

وَاتَّبَعُوا مَا تَتْلُو الشَّيَاطِينُ عَلَىٰ مُلْكِ سُلَيْمَانَ ۖ وَمَا كَفَرَ سُلَيْمَانُ وَلَـٰكِنَّ الشَّيَاطِينَ كَفَرُوا يُعَلِّمُونَ النَّاسَ السِّحْرَ وَمَا أُنزِلَ عَلَى الْمَلَكَيْنِ بِبَابِلَ هَارُوتَ وَمَارُوتَ ۚ وَمَا يُعَلِّمَانِ مِنْ أَحَدٍ حَتَّىٰ يَقُولَا إِنَّمَا نَحْنُ فِتْنَةٌ فَلَا تَكْفُرْ ۖ فَيَتَعَلَّمُونَ مِنْهُمَا مَا يُفَرِّقُونَ بِهِ بَيْنَ الْمَرْءِ وَزَوْجِهِ ۚ وَمَا هُم بِضَارِّينَ بِهِ مِنْ أَحَدٍ إِلَّا بِإِذْنِ اللَّهِ ۚ وَيَتَعَلَّمُونَ مَا يَضُرُّهُمْ وَلَا يَنفَعُهُمْ ۚ وَلَقَدْ عَلِمُوا لَمَنِ اشْتَرَاهُ مَا لَهُ فِي الْآخِرَةِ مِنْ خَلَاقٍ ۚ وَلَبِئْسَ مَا شَرَوْا بِهِ أَنفُسَهُمْ ۚ لَوْ كَانُوا يَعْلَمُونَ
Translation: And they followed what the devils recited during the reign of Solomon. It was not Solomon who disbelieved, but rather the devils disbelieved—teaching people magic and what had been revealed to the two angels, Harut and Marut, in Babylon. These two never taught anyone without saying, "We are only a trial, so do not disbelieve (by practising magic)." Yet they learned from them what could create separation between a man and his wife. But they could not harm anyone with it except by Allah’s permission. They learned what harmed them and brought no benefit. And they certainly knew that whoever buys into it will have no share in the Hereafter. Miserable indeed is what they sold themselves for—if only they knew.
Reflection: This verse continues the theme of rejecting true guidance in favor of falsehood. It recounts how some from among the People of the Book turned away from the revelation brought by the Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him), despite it affirming what they already had. Instead, they followed teachings falsely associated with the time of Prophet Solomon. The devils—rebellious beings from among the jinn—spread tales implying that Solomon ruled through sorcery. The verse makes it explicitly clear: Solomon never engaged in disbelief or magic. It was the devils who were guilty of disbelief, for they taught people harmful magical practices. They twisted historical truth and misrepresented the legacy of a prophet. The verse also refers to a historical event in Babylon involving two angels, Harut and Marut. These angels were sent as a test. They warned people plainly not to misuse what they demonstrated, stating clearly, "We are only a trial, so do not disbelieve." Nevertheless, some ignored the warning and pursued knowledge that brought harm—especially knowledge used to sow division between spouses. Despite the harmful nature of this magic, the verse underscores an important point: no harm can come from it except by Allah’s permission. This affirms the core Islamic belief that all power belongs to Allah alone, and nothing in creation—no matter how seemingly dark or dangerous—acts outside His will. Moreover, those who sought out such knowledge did so at a terrible cost. What they acquired brought no benefit, either in this world or the next. In fact, they already knew that engaging in such practices would strip them of any share in the rewards of the Hereafter. The verse condemns their actions as a disastrous trade-off: selling their souls for a path that brings neither true power nor salvation. By portraying this rejection of divine truth in favor of harmful superstition, the verse warns against the illusion of control through forbidden means. In doing so, it defends the honor of Prophet Solomon, warns of the spiritual danger of magic, and highlights the grave consequences of abandoning revelation for misleading alternatives.

Verse 103

وَلَوْ أَنَّهُمْ آمَنُوا وَاتَّقَوْا لَمَثُوبَةٌ مِّنْ عِندِ اللَّهِ خَيْرٌ ۖ لَّوْ كَانُوا يَعْلَمُونَ
Translation: And if they had believed and been mindful (of Allah), then the reward from Allah would certainly have been far better—if only they knew!
Reflection: After highlighting the harmful consequences of engaging in magic and straying from divine guidance, this verse presents the better path they could have taken. Had they remained committed to faith in Allah and been mindful of Him—by avoiding practices such as magic and falsehood—the reward from Allah would have been far greater than anything they tried to gain through their misguided actions. The phrase "if only they knew!" reflects their lack of awareness about the true value of faith and righteousness. It emphasizes that what they exchanged their souls for, as mentioned in the previous verse, was ultimately worthless compared to what they could have received from Allah. This verse also gently reminds the reader that the way back to Allah remains open. The door of guidance and mercy is never closed for those who turn back with sincerity.

Verse 104

يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا لَا تَقُولُوا رَاعِنَا وَقُولُوا انظُرْنَا وَاسْمَعُوا ۗ وَلِلْكَافِرِينَ عَذَابٌ أَلِيمٌ
Translation: O believers! Do not say “Rāʿinā” (Give us your attention), but say “Unẓurnā” (Look upon us), and listen (attentively). And for the disbelievers is a painful punishment.
Reflection: This verse teaches believers to use clear, respectful language when addressing the Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him), while also revealing how subtle manipulation of words can be used as a form of mockery. Originally, the companions would sometimes say “Rāʿinā”, meaning “Give us your attention,” as a way to ask the Prophet to slow down or repeat part of his recitation so they could better understand and memorize the Qur’an. The word was linguistically sound in Arabic and had a respectful meaning. However, some individuals from the Jewish community—particularly those harboring hostility—misused this word. They exploited its similarity to a Hebrew term (or an Arabic root) that implied insult or foolishness. Under the guise of politeness, they would say “Rāʿinā” with mocking intent, disguising an insult within a word that sounded innocent. To protect the Prophet from such hidden mockery and to close the door to linguistic abuse, Allah instructed the believers to stop using “Rāʿinā” altogether. Instead, He told them to say “Unẓurnā,” which also means “Look upon us” or “Give us your attention.” Though similar in meaning, Unẓurnā had no negative connotations and could not be twisted into disrespect. This switch reflects the Qur’an’s precision and its concern for both meaning and intention in speech. The verse continues: “and listen (attentively).” This instruction calls for more than just hearing—it refers to attentive, thoughtful listening that leads to understanding and acceptance. Some scholars also interpret it as a call to obey the Prophet’s guidance, highlighting the need for both attentiveness and readiness to act upon his words. The final part of the verse warns: “And for the disbelievers is a painful punishment.” This refers specifically to those who engaged in hypocritical and malicious behavior—using innocent-sounding words as a cover for mockery. By using the broader term “disbelievers,” the Qur’an extends this warning to anyone who engages in such covert hostility. This verse also connects conceptually to the previous discussion on magic, where hidden harm is cloaked in outwardly harmless actions. Just as sorcery often relies on manipulation through intention and language, the mockery here relied on deceptive speech—a kind of linguistic “spell” meant to offend without being caught. Thus, the verse serves multiple purposes: safeguarding the dignity of the Prophet, protecting the believers from being unknowingly used in a scheme of mockery, exposing the plots of the hypocrites, and affirming that Allah is fully aware of what people say—and what they intend—no matter how carefully they try to conceal it.

Verse 105

مَّا يَوَدُّ الَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا مِنْ أَهْلِ الْكِتَابِ وَلَا الْمُشْرِكِينَ أَن يُنَزَّلَ عَلَيْكُم مِّنْ خَيْرٍ مِّن رَّبِّكُمْ ۗ وَاللَّهُ يَخْتَصُّ بِرَحْمَتِهِ مَن يَشَاءُ ۚ وَاللَّهُ ذُو الْفَضْلِ الْعَظِيمِ
Translation: Neither the disbelievers from among the People of the Book nor the polytheists wish for any good to be sent down to you from your Lord. But Allah singles out for His mercy whoever He wills. And Allah is the Lord of infinite bounty.
Reflection: This verse follows the previous one, by turning attention to external hostility. It describes the attitude of some individuals outside the Muslim community—specifically, certain disbelievers from among the People of the Book (Jews and Christians) as well as polytheists. These groups harbored resentment toward the revelation received by the Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him) and the favor shown to the Muslim community. Allah deliberately uses the phrase "the disbelievers from among the People of the Book", instead of simply "the People of the Book," to highlight that this rejection and resentment comes only from those who have abandoned the teachings of their own scriptures. Their holy books had, in fact, urged them to follow the truth wherever it appeared and foretold the coming of a prophet after them. By turning away from this guidance—motivated in part by envy—they went against their own revelation. To avoid the impression that this envy was unique to them, the verse adds: "nor the polytheists", making clear that this denial and hostility toward divine favor was shared by other disbelievers as well. The phrasing closes the door to narrow interpretations and affirms that the rejection of truth was not limited to one group. The statement “But Allah singles out for His mercy whoever He wills” emphasizes that divine favor is not controlled by human wishes or prejudice. The blessings of revelation and guidance are given purely according to Allah’s wisdom and mercy. This sentence is also connected to the earlier part of the verse: while others may wish to block this mercy, Allah continues to bestow it upon whomever He wills. The final statement, “And Allah is the Lord of infinite bounty,” is both a reassurance and a reminder. It reassures believers that Allah’s generosity is limitless and not withheld because of others’ resentment. And it reminds all people that those who desire this mercy should turn to Allah with sincerity—by embracing good character, obedience, and humility, while turning away from wrongdoing—so that they may be among those whom Allah chooses to bless.

Verse 106

مَا نَنسَخْ مِنْ آيَةٍ أَوْ نُنسِهَا نَأْتِ بِخَيْرٍ مِّنْهَا أَوْ مِثْلِهَا ۗ أَلَمْ تَعْلَمْ أَنَّ اللَّهَ عَلَىٰ كُلِّ شَيْءٍ قَدِيرٌ
Translation: Whenever We abrogate a verse or cause it to be forgotten, We bring forth one that is better or similar. Do you not know that Allah is Most Capable of everything?
Reflection: This verse addresses doubts raised during the time of the Prophet Muhammad regarding changes in certain divine rulings or revelations. Some critics questioned the legitimacy of Islam, especially when changes occurred—such as the shift in the direction of prayer (qiblah). In response, this verse asserts that Allah has the authority to replace or suspend parts of His revelation, bringing forth something better or equivalent. This emphasizes His perfect wisdom and complete power in guiding humanity throughout changing circumstances. Such changes do not indicate any deficiency in Allah’s knowledge or wisdom. He never abrogates or delays a ruling except to provide what is more beneficial for people at that particular time, or what aligns with their needs in similar ways. If a ruling is postponed and revealed later, what is given in its place during that period sufficiently meets the needs of the people according to their situation. This is a reflection of Allah’s Lordship—He nurtures and guides His creation with care, ensuring their well-being through His mercy and wisdom. Allah’s purpose remains constant across all times and contexts: to preserve order in the world and to guide human conduct in a way that protects their circumstances, tailored to their time, society, and conditions—until the final law was revealed, which represents Allah’s ultimate intention for humankind.

Verse 107

أَلَمْ تَعْلَمْ أَنَّ اللَّهَ لَهُ مُلْكُ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضِ ۗ وَمَا لَكُم مِّن دُونِ اللَّهِ مِن وَلِيٍّ وَلَا نَصِيرٍ
Translation: Do you not know that to Allah belongs the dominion of the heavens and the earth, and that you have no protector or helper besides Allah?
Reflection: This verse continues the theme from the previous one, which addressed changes in revelation. Here, the focus shifts to affirming Allah’s absolute ownership and control over all of creation—everything in the heavens and the earth. It reminds the listener that such authority entails unmatched knowledge and power, and that everything unfolds according to Allah’s will. After establishing that replacing one revelation with another—whether better or equivalent—is not without reason or wisdom, the verse points to Allah’s comprehensive power. Nothing escapes His will. The vastness of His dominion reflects the vastness of His knowledge, and all creation depends on Him entirely. There is no other Lord, protector, or source of support. This verse serves as a conclusive reminder: since Allah alone owns and governs all that exists, it follows that He alone has the right and ability to guide humanity, adjust rulings, and respond to changing circumstances. The phrase "you have no protector or helper besides Allah" reinforces reliance on Him, particularly in moments of uncertainty or when facing outside criticism. The verse may also offer subtle reassurance to some in the Muslim community who, amid social pressure or external doubts, needed to be reminded that true support lies only with Allah. His care, guidance, and protection are constant—and sufficient.

Verse 108

أَمْ تُرِيدُونَ أَن تَسْأَلُوا رَسُولَكُمْ كَمَا سُئِلَ مُوسَىٰ مِن قَبْلُ ۗ وَمَن يَتَبَدَّلِ الْكُفْرَ بِالْإِيمَانِ فَقَدْ ضَلَّ سَوَاءَ السَّبِيلِ
Translation: Or do you (believers) wish to question your Messenger (with demands or objections), as Moses was questioned before? Whoever exchanges belief for disbelief has truly gone far astray from the Straight Path.
Reflection: This verse follows earlier references to how the Children of Israel responded to Prophet Moses (peace be upon him) with excessive questioning and repeated resistance. The verse warns the believers not to adopt a similar attitude toward Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him), by approaching him with inappropriate or defiant questions, especially those that arise not from sincerity but from doubt or external influence. The use of the phrase "do you wish" indicates that such questioning had not yet taken hold among the believers but may have begun to emerge in response to doubts raised by some from among the Jews or others who opposed the message. The comparison to how Moses (peace be upon him) was questioned refers to well-known episodes where the Israelites challenged him with demands that reflected a lack of trust or submission. These include statements such as, “Make for us a god like they have gods,” and “We will not believe in you until we see Allah openly.” Another example is their persistent questioning in the story of the cow, where their inquiries went beyond what was necessary and ultimately brought hardship upon themselves. These instances serve as warnings against a pattern of behavior that can lead to confusion, spiritual burden, or even disbelief. The verse concludes with a firm statement: “Whoever exchanges belief for disbelief has truly gone far astray from the Straight Path.” This final clause reinforces the seriousness of allowing such questioning or doubts to undermine one’s faith. It does not refer only to the act of disbelief itself, but also to any path that moves a person away from trust in the Messenger and in Allah’s guidance. The verse presents a clear caution against undermining the dignity of the Prophet (peace be upon him) and the authority of revelation through skepticism or needless challenges.

Verse 109

وَدَّ كَثِيرٌ مِّنْ أَهْلِ الْكِتَابِ لَوْ يَرُدُّونَكُم مِّن بَعْدِ إِيمَانِكُمْ كُفَّارًا حَسَدًا مِّنْ عِندِ أَنفُسِهِم مِّن بَعْدِ مَا تَبَيَّنَ لَهُمُ الْحَقُّ ۖ فَاعْفُوا وَاصْفَحُوا حَتَّىٰ يَأْتِيَ اللَّهُ بِأَمْرِهِ ۗ إِنَّ اللَّهَ عَلَىٰ كُلِّ شَيْءٍ قَدِيرٌ
Translation: Many among the People of the Book wish they could turn you (believers) back to disbelief, out of envy from within themselves, even after the truth has become clear to them. So pardon and overlook, until Allah brings forth His command. Indeed, Allah is fully capable of all things.
Reflection: This verse addresses a deeply rooted emotional response that some among the People of the Book—particularly certain groups from among the Jews—harbored toward the believers. Even after the truth of Islam had become clear to them, they continued to wish that the Muslims would abandon their faith and return to a state of disbelief. The verse makes clear that this desire did not stem from sincere theological concern or misunderstanding, but rather from envy—something that came from within themselves, not from the teachings of their scriptures. Significantly, the verse does not suggest they wished the Muslims would convert to their own religion, which might have implied a desire for doctrinal alignment. Instead, it speaks of a wish for the believers to revert to disbelief, even to a condition that those same People of the Book recognized as false. This points to the depth of the hostility: it was not about advocating a different religious truth, but about rejecting the success and spiritual clarity of others. The phrase “after the truth has become clear to them” emphasizes that this was not ignorance, but willful opposition. The message of Islam—its call to pure monotheism and the continuity of prophetic guidance—was already consistent with the foundations of earlier revelations. Yet the recognition of this truth stirred resentment rather than acceptance. Despite this hostility, the verse instructs believers not to retaliate or become consumed by bitterness. Instead, they are told to “pardon and overlook.” This guidance promotes a higher moral ground. Pardoning involves releasing the desire for revenge, while overlooking entails not dwelling on the offense at all—demonstrating emotional discipline and inner strength. These qualities were necessary in a time when resentment could easily spark conflict. However, this call to patience is not without boundaries. The verse continues with the phrase, “until Allah brings forth His command,” indicating that ultimate justice rests with Allah and will unfold at the appropriate time. Believers are instructed to show restraint, not because injustice is ignored, but because divine wisdom governs the timing and manner of resolution. The verse closes with the affirmation that “Allah is fully capable of all things.” This statement reassures believers that Allah’s power encompasses all situations. Just as He can expose the truth, He can also protect it and those who uphold it. This divine attribute serves both as a source of comfort and as a model for human conduct: the ability to withhold immediate judgment, not out of weakness, but from a place of control and wisdom.

Verse 110

وَأَقِيمُوا الصَّلَاةَ وَآتُوا الزَّكَاةَ ۚ وَمَا تُقَدِّمُوا لِأَنفُسِكُم مِّنْ خَيْرٍ تَجِدُوهُ عِندَ اللَّهِ ۗ إِنَّ اللَّهَ بِمَا تَعْمَلُونَ بَصِيرٌ
Translation: And establish prayer and give alms-tax (zakah). Whatever good you send forth for yourselves, you will (certainly) find it with Allah. Indeed, Allah is All-Seeing of what you do.
Reflection: After guiding the believers to respond to hostility with patience and forgiveness, this verse shifts focus to the inner foundations of faith—emphasizing consistency in worship and care for others through essential acts of devotion. The command, “Establish prayer and give alms-tax (zakah),” points to the heart of Islamic practice. These two acts—prayer and zakah—serve not only as obligations but as pillars through which one’s connection to Allah and concern for the community are sustained. The directive to uphold these acts serves as an indirect affirmation of steadfastness in Islam. To continue praying and giving zakah is, in effect, to remain firm in one’s faith. These are not mere rituals, but expressions of a life grounded in obedience, self-discipline, and compassion. By anchoring oneself in these deeds, a person invests in a lasting relationship with Allah and prepares for the eternal life to come. The verse then offers reassurance: “Whatever good you send forth for yourselves, you will certainly find it with Allah.” This underscores the idea that every righteous deed, no matter how small or unrecognized by others, is preserved and rewarded by Allah. It also serves as encouragement to continue acting with integrity and patience, even when such efforts are undervalued or misunderstood by others. In the context of the surrounding verses—especially the command to forgive those who harbor envy or hostility—this statement reminds the believers that even if others fail to appreciate their forbearance, Allah sees and preserves every act of goodness. By concluding with the words, “Indeed, Allah is All-Seeing of what you do,” the verse emphasizes divine awareness and justice. Allah is not only aware of each action but is fully capable of responding to it appropriately. His seeing encompasses both the inward intention and the outward act, offering both comfort to the sincere and a warning to those who persist in wrongdoing. It is a subtle promise to the believers—that their efforts will not be wasted—and an implicit warning to others, for nothing escapes His observation.

Verse 111

وَقَالُوا لَن يَدْخُلَ الْجَنَّةَ إِلَّا مَن كَانَ هُودًا أَوْ نَصَارَىٰ ۗ تِلْكَ أَمَانِيُّهُمْ ۗ قُلْ هَاتُوا بُرْهَانَكُمْ إِن كُنتُمْ صَادِقِينَ
Translation: And they (the Jews and Christians) say, “No one will enter Paradise except one who is a Jew or a Christian.” That is merely their wishful thinking. Say, (O Muhammad), “Produce your proof, if you are truthful.”
Reflection: After addressing guidance for the believers, this verse turns to a claim made by some from among the People of the Book—namely, the Jews and the Christians—regarding salvation and access to Paradise. Both groups asserted that eternal reward would be limited exclusively to their own followers, dismissing the possibility of others being admitted. The Qur’an summarizes their claim in the phrase: “No one will enter Paradise except one who is a Jew or a Christian,” thereby capturing the essence of their mutually exclusive beliefs in a single statement. Though each group claimed salvation for itself, the verse combines these perspectives to underscore how both operate on similarly narrow assumptions. These claims are described as “wishful thinking”—a term that reflects the absence of real authority or revelation behind their assertions. Rather than being grounded in clear evidence from Allah, these are personal hopes elevated into absolutes. The verse challenges the presumption that salvation is tied to identity alone, without regard for truth, sincerity, or righteousness. To expose the weakness of these claims, the Prophet (peace be upon him) is instructed to respond: “Produce your proof, if you are truthful.” This is a call to accountability. If such a serious claim is being made—that Paradise is reserved only for one group—then it must be supported by clear evidence from revelation. In doing so, the Qur’an affirms a key principle: belief must rest on evidence, not inherited assumptions or sectarian pride. The verse thus shifts the burden of proof back onto those making the claim and prepares the way for a broader, more inclusive understanding of faith in the verses that follow.

Verse 112

بَلَىٰ مَنْ أَسْلَمَ وَجْهَهُ لِلَّهِ وَهُوَ مُحْسِنٌ فَلَهُ أَجْرُهُ عِندَ رَبِّهِ وَلَا خَوْفٌ عَلَيْهِمْ وَلَا هُمْ يَحْزَنُونَ
Translation: No indeed! Whoever submits their face to Allah and does good will have their reward with their Lord. And there will be no fear for them, nor will they grieve.
Reflection: This verse provides a direct response to the exclusive salvation claims mentioned in the previous verse. Instead of limiting Paradise to any one group, it establishes a universal principle rooted in sincerity and righteousness. The verse begins with a clear rejection—“No indeed!”—which firmly dismisses the idea that salvation belongs solely to those who identify as Jews or Christians. This expression serves as a decisive break from sectarian thinking. The verse then sets forth a comprehensive criterion: “Whoever submits their face to Allah and does good.” The phrase “submits their face” symbolizes a full and conscious surrender to Allah, using the face—the most dignified and visible part of the human being—as a representation of one’s entire self. It denotes complete humility, sincerity, and devotion. This submission is not ritualistic or partial, but total. In addition to this inward submission, the verse requires “doing good.” Righteous actions must accompany sincere belief, indicating that true faith is expressed through both inner commitment and outward deeds. The combination of these two—devotion and action—is what leads to the promised reward. The verse offers reassurance to those who meet these conditions: “They will have their reward with their Lord, and there will be no fear for them, nor will they grieve.” This promise removes uncertainty about the Hereafter. Such individuals will not face fear regarding what lies ahead, nor sorrow for what is left behind. Their path is one of peace and divine acceptance. By shifting the focus from identity or heritage to personal submission and ethical conduct, the verse affirms that access to Allah’s reward is open to all who are sincere in belief and upright in action.

Verse 113

وَقَالَتِ الْيَهُودُ لَيْسَتِ النَّصَارَىٰ عَلَىٰ شَيْءٍ وَقَالَتِ النَّصَارَىٰ لَيْسَتِ الْيَهُودُ عَلَىٰ شَيْءٍ وَهُمْ يَتْلُونَ الْكِتَابَ ۗ كَذَٰلِكَ قَالَ الَّذِينَ لَا يَعْلَمُونَ مِثْلَ قَوْلِهِمْ ۚ فَاللَّهُ يَحْكُمُ بَيْنَهُمْ يَوْمَ الْقِيَامَةِ فِيمَا كَانُوا فِيهِ يَخْتَلِفُونَ
Translation: (Furthermore), the Jews say, “The Christians have no basis (for their beliefs),” and the Christians say, “The Jews have no basis (for their beliefs),” although both recite the Scripture. And those who have no knowledge (the Arab polytheists) say similar words. But it is Allah who will judge between them on the Day of Judgment regarding that over which they used to differ.
Reflection: This verse continues the discussion of sectarian claims to salvation and truth. It highlights the mutual denial between certain members of the Jewish and Christian communities, where each side accuses the other of having no valid foundation for their beliefs. The phrase “although both recite the Scripture” underlines the irony: both groups possess revelations from Allah, yet they dismiss each other, despite having more in common than they acknowledge. This mutual rejection exposes not only their internal divisions, but also a deeper issue—a failure to understand or implement the guidance found in their own scriptures. Their disputes, rather than being rooted in divine revelation, often stem from inherited positions, misinterpretation, or sectarian pride. The verse then shifts to mention those “who have no knowledge,” referring to the Arab polytheists. These individuals, lacking any revealed scripture or prophetic guidance, echo the dismissive language of the People of the Book. By stating that all religious groups are misguided, they reject revelation altogether—including that brought by Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him). Their similarity in speech reflects a shared tendency to make unsupported judgments and to deny the truth out of ignorance. The words “say similar words” link these three groups—Jews, Christians, and idolaters—through a common trait: making baseless claims while ignoring or misusing divine guidance. This parallel highlights the danger of asserting religious truth without sincere engagement with revelation. The verse concludes by affirming that it is Allah who will judge between them on the Day of Judgment. Human assertions, accusations, and divisions will be set aside. What truly matters is not one’s label or group affiliation, but the truth known to and judged by Allah alone. This serves as both a warning and a reassurance: no claim will stand unless it is grounded in truth as judged by Allah.

Verse 114

وَمَنْ أَظْلَمُ مِمَّن مَّنَعَ مَسَاجِدَ اللَّهِ أَن يُذْكَرَ فِيهَا اسْمُهُ وَسَعَىٰ فِي خَرَابِهَا ۚ أُولَـٰئِكَ مَا كَانَ لَهُمْ أَن يَدْخُلُوهَا إِلَّا خَائِفِينَ ۚ لَهُمْ فِي الدُّنْيَا خِزْيٌ وَلَهُمْ فِي الْآخِرَةِ عَذَابٌ عَظِيمٌ
Translation: And who could be more unjust than those who prevent the name of Allah from being mentioned in His places of worship and strive to ruin them? Such people have no right to enter them except in fear. They will face disgrace in this world, and in the Hereafter they will suffer a severe punishment.
Reflection: This verse continues the broader theme of unjust claims and actions concerning religious truth and worship. It identifies a particular form of injustice: obstructing the remembrance of Allah in His places of worship and attempting to damage or dishonor them. The rhetorical question “Who could be more unjust…” emphasizes the gravity of such acts by framing them as among the worst forms of wrongdoing. The verse refers both to general behavior and to specific events, such as when the polytheists of Makkah barred the Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him) and his companions from entering the Sacred Mosque (al-Masjid al-Haram). These actions were not neutral; they represented an active effort to suppress the truth and deny people access to a space that belongs to Allah and is meant for His worship. By preventing the mention of Allah’s name and striving to ruin the sanctity of these spaces, these individuals are described as committing a direct assault on what is sacred. The word “strive” implies deliberate effort, not accidental harm or neglect, but intentional opposition to what is right. The phrase “they have no right to enter them except in fear” reverses the situation. While these individuals once acted with power and arrogance, the verse foresees a time when, if they enter such spaces at all, it will be under threat, anxiety, or divine judgment. This foretells a future marked by accountability. The verse concludes by describing the two levels of consequences for their actions. In this world, they will face humiliation and disgrace—whether through social rejection, political downfall, or moral exposure. In the Hereafter, their punishment will be far greater: a severe, unmistakable consequence for opposing Allah’s command and desecrating what is meant for His remembrance. This verse reaffirms the sacred nature of all places of worship dedicated to Allah and warns against any attempt to hinder or corrupt their purpose. It offers reassurance to believers that such transgressions will not go unnoticed and that ultimate justice belongs to Allah.

Verse 115

وَلِلَّهِ الْمَشْرِقُ وَالْمَغْرِبُ ۚ فَأَيْنَمَا تُوَلُّوا فَثَمَّ وَجْهُ اللَّهِ ۚ إِنَّ اللَّهَ وَاسِعٌ عَلِيمٌ
Translation: To Allah belong the east and the west, so wherever you turn (in sincere devotion), there is the countenance of Allah. Truly, Allah is All-Encompassing, All-Knowing.
Reflection: This verse affirms that all directions—east, west, and everything in between—belong entirely to Allah. It emphasizes that no part of the earth is beyond His ownership or beyond the reach of His mercy and knowledge. The phrase “To Allah belong the east and the west” is not merely geographical; it underscores the totality of Allah’s dominion and the futility of restricting worship to one physical direction as a necessary condition for divine acceptance. The central message of the verse, “so wherever you turn (in sincere devotion), there is the countenance of Allah,” offers deep reassurance to believers. It refers to the historical moment when Muslims were instructed to change the direction of prayer (qiblah). The verse assures that such a shift does not diminish the validity of worship, for Allah is not confined to a single direction. What He commands is always guided by wisdom, and He is fully aware of those who turn to Him with sincerity. This verse also teaches that what matters most in acts of worship is the sincerity of the heart, not the orientation of the body. Wherever one may be—traveling, facing obstacles, or uncertain about the prayer direction—what matters is the intention to turn toward Allah in devotion. This guidance is especially comforting for those who experience difficulty or displacement. The “countenance of Allah” is a Qur’anic expression that conveys His nearness, favor, and acceptance. Muslims accept such descriptions as they are revealed, without comparing Allah to His creation or trying to define the unseen. His attributes are unique to Him and are understood in a manner befitting His majesty. The verse ends by affirming two divine attributes: “Truly, Allah is All-Encompassing, All-Knowing.” This reminds believers that Allah’s mercy and authority reach all places and that He knows every intention and action. Nothing escapes His knowledge, and no sincere act goes unnoticed.

Verse 116

وَقَالُوا اتَّخَذَ اللَّهُ وَلَدًا ۗ سُبْحَانَهُ ۖ بَل لَّهُ مَا فِي السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضِ ۖ كُلٌّ لَّهُ قَانِتُونَ
Translation: They say, “Allah has taken a son.” Exalted is He! Rather, to Him belongs whatever is in the heavens and the earth. All are devoutly obedient to Him.
Reflection: This verse addresses a false claim made by certain groups—that Allah has taken a son. This belief existed among Christians, who said Jesus was the son of Allah; some Jews, who attributed divine sonship to Prophet Ezra; and some Arab idolaters, who regarded angels as Allah’s daughters. The verse firmly rejects this notion. The phrase "has taken a son" is especially significant—it points to an act that suggests need, acquisition, or dependence. Yet Allah, by His nature, is entirely self-sufficient, eternal, and free of all needs. The idea of sonship implies deficiency, limitation, or mortality, all of which are impossible for the One who created everything and needs nothing. The response is immediate: “Exalted is He!” This is a declaration of Allah’s complete transcendence and freedom from all imperfections. It expresses that such claims are not only false, but fundamentally dishonor the majesty of Allah. To attribute human qualities such as parenthood to Him is to misunderstand what it means for Him to be the Creator and Sustainer of all things. The verse then affirms that “to Him belongs whatever is in the heavens and the earth.” Everything in existence—seen and unseen—belongs to Allah. Ownership in this absolute sense leaves no room for the possibility of offspring or partners. A son, in human terms, is often associated with inheritance, continuation, or shared authority. But Allah’s dominion is already perfect and complete. He does not share it, nor does He need to extend it. The closing statement, “All are devoutly obedient to Him,” further highlights this truth. Every part of creation, from the smallest particle to the greatest galaxy, is subject to Allah’s will and authority. Their obedience is not partial or voluntary—it is inherent and constant. This absolute servitude leaves no room for the kind of relationship implied by sonship. A son shares closeness or even equality in some respects, while creation remains utterly dependent and submissive before the Creator. This verse thus corrects a deep theological error, reasserts Allah’s unmatched majesty, and reminds all that the proper relationship between creation and Creator is one of obedience, not kinship.

Verse 117

بَدِيعُ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضِ ۖ وَإِذَا قَضَىٰ أَمْرًا فَإِنَّمَا يَقُولُ لَهُ كُن فَيَكُونُ
Translation: (He is the) Originator of the heavens and the earth. When He decrees a matter, He only says to it, “Be!”—and it is.
Reflection: Continuing the theme of Allah’s unmatched power and uniqueness, this verse presents Him as the Originator—the one who brings into existence the heavens and the earth without any precedent or model. The term "Originator" implies creation from absolute nothingness, without imitation or inherited design. This is a concept beyond human ability and imagination—only Allah possesses such creative power. The verse also describes how this act of creation takes place: not through labor, time, or process, but by a single, irresistible command. When Allah wills something to exist, He simply says to it, “Be,” and it is—immediately and completely. This truth has deep implications, particularly in response to misunderstandings about divine power or status. Some may view unusual events—like the miraculous birth of Jesus—as evidence of a divine lineage. However, this verse clarifies that such miraculous creation does not point to sonship, but to Allah’s limitless will and authority. The birth of Jesus without a father is no more difficult for Allah than the creation of Adam with no parents at all. All are examples of His will being enacted by His command. The verse thus reaffirms Allah’s complete independence, creative power, and transcendence. Everything that exists—no matter how ordinary or extraordinary—exists only because Allah willed it into being. He is free from partners, intermediaries, or needs, and His act of creating reflects His supreme mastery over all things.

Verse 118

وَقَالَ الَّذِينَ لَا يَعْلَمُونَ لَوْلَا يُكَلِّمُنَا اللَّهُ أَوْ تَأْتِينَا آيَةٌ ۗ كَذَٰلِكَ قَالَ الَّذِينَ مِن قَبْلِهِم مِّثْلَ قَوْلِهِمْ ۘ تَشَابَهَتْ قُلُوبُهُمْ ۗ قَدْ بَيَّنَّا الْآيَاتِ لِقَوْمٍ يُوقِنُونَ
Translation: Those who do not know say, “Why does Allah not speak to us, or why does no sign come to us?” Those before them said the same. Their hearts are alike. Indeed, We have made the signs clear for people who are certain (in faith).
Reflection: This verse addresses a recurring human attitude: the demand for direct communication from Allah or the appearance of miraculous signs tailored to personal expectations. Those “who do not know” refers primarily to the polytheists of the Prophet’s (peace be upon him) time, who voiced such objections frequently. Their statement is given priority here because it was widespread and prominent during the early revelations of the Qur’an. However, the verse also draws a parallel to earlier communities—such as some among the Jews and Christians—who made similar demands of their prophets. This shows that the pattern is not new. Human beings, across generations, often respond to divine guidance with skepticism, demanding extraordinary proofs while ignoring the clear signs already provided. For example, some of the Children of Israel demanded to see Allah directly, even after witnessing clear miracles through Prophet Moses (peace be upon him). The phrase “their hearts are alike” highlights the continuity of human resistance rooted in similar dispositions—doubt, pride, or defiance—rather than sincere inquiry. The problem is not the lack of signs, but a shared attitude of rejection that blinds people to them. Rather than meeting such demands with miracles on their terms, the verse redirects the focus: the signs have already been made clear for those who seek truth with certainty. The Qur’an itself, along with the life and character of the Prophet (peace be upon him), contains sufficient guidance for those willing to reflect. The verse implies that no sign is sufficient for one whose heart is closed, while for those grounded in faith, the existing signs are more than enough.

Verse 119

إِنَّا أَرْسَلْنَاكَ بِالْحَقِّ بَشِيرًا وَنَذِيرًا ۖ وَلَا تُسْأَلُ عَنْ أَصْحَابِ الْجَحِيمِ
Translation: Indeed, We have sent you (O Muhammad) with the truth, as a bearer of glad tidings and a warner. And you will not be held accountable for the companions of the Hellfire.
Reflection: This verse provides the Prophet (peace be upon him) with reassurance and clarity regarding the scope of his mission. It affirms that he was sent with the truth—meaning the Qur’an, the divine message, and the guidance it brings. Turning away from the revealed truth in order to appease others would sever the bond with Allah’s support, leaving a person without His protection or aid. This warning is not a reflection of doubt in the Prophet’s commitment, but a universal reminder of the consequences of compromising divine guidance. The closing words—about losing a “protector or helper”—highlight the severity of such a loss. A protector implies care and nearness; a helper implies assistance in times of hardship. The verse uses strong, absolute language to emphasize this reality: turning from Allah’s revealed knowledge toward human desires results in complete abandonment by the divine. It also implicitly addresses those among the People of the Book who believe themselves to hold a special status with Allah, reminding them that true favor is not inherited but earned through adherence to His actual guidance.

Verse 125

Within the bounds of the Ka'bah, fighting and injustice were forbidden, establishing it as a symbol of divine peace and mercy. The verse also gives a command: “Take the standing place of Abraham as a place of prayer.” This refers to the spot near the Ka'bah where Abraham is believed to have stood while constructing it. Muslims are instructed to perform prayer at this place, linking their worship to the legacy of Abraham’s monotheism. Furthermore, Allah entrusted both Abraham and his son Ishmael with the responsibility of purifying the Ka'bah. The command to purify the House has both physical and spiritual dimensions. Physically, it refers to keeping the area clean and suitable for worship. Spiritually, it refers to removing any forms of idolatry, corruption, or inappropriate behavior, including the pre-Islamic practices that later emerged, such as idol-worship and circumambulating the Ka'bah unclothed. The verse specifically mentions four types of worshippers: those who perform Tawaf (circling the Ka'bah), those who stay for spiritual retreat (i’tikaf), and those who bow and prostrate in prayer. This verse affirms that from the very beginning, the Ka'bah was established as a place of monotheistic worship, purified and prepared by two prophets, and intended for those who turn to Allah with humility and reverence.

However, spiritual leadership and nearness to Allah are reserved for those who are upright in faith. The verse thus reminds us of the difference between temporary provision and ultimate accountability. This verse ties closely to the preceding ones, showing Abraham’s deep concern for the future of Mecca and the legacy of worship centered around the Ka'bah. This verse is seen by Muslims as a prophetic prayer for the coming of Prophet Muhammad—who was, in fact, from the lineage of Ishmael, and who fulfilled all the roles mentioned: reciting the revelation, teaching the Book and wisdom, and guiding people toward spiritual purification.

In essence, this verse upholds Abraham’s example as a timeless model of faith and submission to Allah, one that transcends lineage or sectarian claims.

Verse 131

إِذْ قَالَ لَهُ رَبُّهُ أَسْلِمْ ۖ قَالَ أَسْلَمْتُ لِرَبِّ الْعَالَمِينَ

Reflection: After highlighting Abraham’s elevated status and faith in the previous verse, this verse gives a direct example of that faith in action. It captures a defining moment that reflects the very core of Abraham’s relationship with Allah: complete and willing submission. When Allah said to him, “Submit,” Abraham responded without hesitation: “I have submitted to the Lord of the worlds.” This brief exchange expresses a deep and unwavering commitment. It is not just a verbal agreement, but a reflection of a heart already aligned with the truth. Abraham’s response affirms his readiness and the clarity of his recognition that the entire universe belongs to and is governed by Allah. Notably, Abraham replies with “to the Lord of the worlds” rather than simply “to You.” This phrasing emphasizes his conscious awareness of Allah’s supreme authority over all creation—it demonstrates both his submission and his understanding. It also reflects the universality of his faith: it was not bound to a particular tribe or group but directed toward the Lord of all existence. This moment perfectly encapsulates what it means to be a Muslim—one who submits fully and sincerely to Allah.

Verse 132

وَوَصَّىٰ بِهَا إِبْرَاهِيمُ بَنِيهِ وَيَعْقُوبُ يَا بَنِيَّ إِنَّ اللَّهَ اصْطَفَىٰ لَكُمُ الدِّينَ فَلَا تَمُوتُنَّ إِلَّا وَأَنتُم مُّسْلِمُونَ
Translation: And Abraham advised his sons—and so did Jacob—(saying), "O my sons, indeed Allah has chosen for you this (pure) faith, so do not die except in submission (as Muslims, in a state of full surrender to Allah)."
Reflection: This verse shows how the faith of submission to Allah—Islam—was passed from one generation to the next. It tells us that Prophet Abraham left this way of life as a legacy to his sons, and his grandson, Prophet Jacob (also known as Israel), gave the same advice to his own children. A key message in this verse is the importance of guiding future generations by passing down essential truths and values. Those who have deeply recognized and followed a divinely guided path naturally want others—especially their descendants—to remain connected to it. This concern is not limited to personal belief; it reflects a broader responsibility to protect and preserve what is believed to be the truth. The act of giving such heartfelt advice—especially during important moments like the end of life—is shown here as an expression of deep care. It reflects an awareness that truth can be forgotten, neglected, or replaced. When a parent urges their children to remain in submission to Allah, it shows a desire to safeguard them from spiritual loss or misguidance. Such advice is meaningful because it comes from love, concern, and a sense of duty. The verse mentions both Abraham and Jacob to highlight their shared devotion to the same faith. By recalling their final words, the verse links the message of Islam to the broader family line of the Children of Israel. For those who identify with Jacob, this serves as a reminder of the original message that united the prophets—a message rooted in submission to Allah. Islam is presented here not as something new, but as the continuation and fulfillment of that message. At the heart of their advice is the statement: “Allah has chosen for you this faith, so do not die except in submission.” This is more than a reminder about the moment of death—it is a call to live a life defined by submission to Allah. The idea is to remain committed to this path at every stage of life, so that when death eventually comes, one is already in a state of surrender. By warning against dying in any other condition, the verse encourages lifelong commitment. It is as if to say: live with the kind of sincerity and consistency that ensures your life ends in the right state. It is not about predicting the moment of death, but about maintaining faith and obedience so that whenever that moment comes, a person is ready. Finally, the verse shows that the essence of the religion followed by Abraham, Jacob, and their descendants was Islam—submission to the Lord of all worlds. Although different prophets brought different laws suited to their times, the foundation remained the same. This reinforces the view of Islam as the original, complete, and chosen way of life given by Allah for all humanity.

Verse 133

أَمْ كُنتُمْ شُهَدَاءَ إِذْ حَضَرَ يَعْقُوبَ الْمَوْتُ إِذْ قَالَ لِبَنِيهِ مَا تَعْبُدُونَ مِن بَعْدِي قَالُوا نَعْبُدُ إِلَـٰهَكَ وَإِلَـٰهَ آبَائِكَ إِبْرَاهِيمَ وَإِسْمَاعِيلَ وَإِسْحَاقَ إِلَـٰهًا وَاحِدًا وَنَحْنُ لَهُ مُسْلِمُونَ
Translation: Or were you present when death approached Jacob? He asked his children, “What will you worship after me (after my passing)?” They replied, “We will worship your God and the God of your forefathers—Abraham, Ishmael, and Isaac—the one (true) God. And to Him we will submit.”
Reflection: This verse presents a deeply meaningful moment from the life of Prophet Jacob (also known as Israel), as he nears the end of his life. In this intimate and solemn setting, his primary concern is not with material inheritance or worldly affairs, but with the spiritual direction his children will follow after his passing. His question—“What will you worship after me?”—goes to the heart of their identity and future. His children’s response is firm and unambiguous. They declare that they will remain devoted to the same God worshipped by their forefathers: Abraham, Ishmael, and Isaac. This highlights a continuous line of monotheism that stretches back generations. Their words—“the one (true) God”—affirm the absolute oneness of Allah and reject any association of partners with Him. By ending with the phrase, “And to Him we will submit,” they not only express belief but also a commitment to live in submission to Allah, which is the essence of Islam. This verse serves more than just a historical or narrative purpose. It challenges claims—particularly from among the Jews at the time of the Qur’an’s revelation—that Jacob died adhering to a later religious identity, such as Judaism. The opening phrase, “Or were you present…” is a rhetorical question. It is not asking for information but rather exposing the groundlessness of such claims. It points to the fact that these were private moments, and no one among those making such assertions had witnessed what Jacob actually believed or advised. The verse gently but clearly refutes the idea that Abraham, Jacob, or other early prophets were followers of systems that had not yet come into existence. It emphasizes that their religion was submission to Allah alone—Islam in its original, unaltered form. Religious labels that developed later do not reflect the foundational message they lived and passed on. The children’s reply confirms this continuity: worshipping the same God as their noble ancestors and pledging to submit to Him. This testifies to the unbroken chain of monotheistic belief and practice. It also links directly to the previous verse (2:132), in which both Abraham and Jacob advised their children not to die except in a state of submission. Here, we see that Jacob’s children internalized that message and responded with conviction. This moment, captured in the verse, shows how faith was not just taught but lived and transferred with purpose—from heart to heart, generation to generation. It reminds the reader that true faith is rooted in sincere devotion to Allah and a commitment to uphold that path with clarity, certainty, and continuity.

Verse 134

تِلْكَ أُمَّةٌ قَدْ خَلَتْ ۖ لَهَا مَا كَسَبَتْ وَلَكُم مَّا كَسَبْتُمْ ۖ وَلَا تُسْأَلُونَ عَمَّا كَانُوا يَعْمَلُونَ
Translation: That was a nation who has (already) passed away. What they earned (of deeds and their results) is theirs (alone), and what you earn (of deeds and their results) is yours (alone). And you will not be held accountable for what they did.
Reflection: This verse follows the account of Prophet Jacob and his children affirming their commitment to Allah. It shifts the focus from the faith of the past to the responsibility of the present. The “nation” mentioned here refers to Prophet Abraham and his descendants, described collectively as an ummah (community). They have “passed away,” meaning their time on earth has ended, and their deeds are complete. This is not merely a statement of historical fact—it serves as a reminder that no one can claim the reward of another’s actions once that person’s life is over. The message is clear: the righteousness of past generations, however great, cannot guarantee the salvation of those who come after them. “What they earned (of deeds and their results) is theirs (alone)” means that the good deeds of those before belong solely to them; “what you earn (of deeds and their results) is yours (alone)” stresses that each generation—and each person—is rewarded or held accountable only for their own actions. The verse directly challenges the belief that ancestral merit could cover or erase present wrongdoing. By adding, “And you will not be held accountable for what they did,” the Qur’an makes it clear that no generation will be burdened with the sins of those who came before. At the same time, it affirms that all deeds—whether righteous or sinful—are recorded only for the person who performed them, and their results do not transfer to others. The principle here is foundational in Islam: accountability is individual. Lineage, heritage, or family history do not determine a person’s standing with Allah. Instead, each person’s outcome is tied to their own faith and deeds. This teaching also prepares the reader for the verses that follow, which will further emphasize that true guidance is not inherited but lived, and that each soul must maintain its own connection to Allah through sincere belief and righteous action.

Verse 135

وَقَالُوا كُونُوا هُودًا أَوْ نَصَارَىٰ تَهْتَدُوا ۗ قُلْ بَلْ مِلَّةَ إِبْرَاهِيمَ حَنِيفًا ۖ وَمَا كَانَ مِنَ الْمُشْرِكِينَ
Translation: And they say, “Be Jews or Christians (and then) you will be guided.” Say, (O Muhammad), “Rather, (we follow) the way of Abraham, who was inclined toward truth, and was not among those who associate partners with Allah.”
Reflection: After describing the error of turning away from the way of Abraham, Allah shows how this error appears in their speech and beliefs. They narrow the path of guidance to either Judaism or Christianity, with each group considering only its own religion as the true guidance. For the Jews, anyone who is not Jewish is not considered guided; for the Christians, anyone who is not Christian is not considered guided. This attitude amounts to rejecting those who truly follow the way of Abraham, even though his faith existed before these divisions. It reflects the height of their arrogance. The verse responds with a clear and principled statement: true guidance is not tied to belonging to a later group, but to following “the way of Abraham.” Islam is presented as the direct continuation of Abraham’s pure monotheism. Abraham is described as “inclined toward truth” (hanif)—turning away from the falsehoods of his time and remaining steadfast upon what is right. This is high praise, because Abraham’s devotion to Allah stood in stark contrast to the widespread errors of his people. His way was defined by sincere worship of Allah alone, without any association of partners. By affirming that Abraham “was not among those who associate partners with Allah,” the verse removes any possibility of misinterpreting his example to justify polytheistic or corrupted practices. It makes clear that the claim to follow Abraham can only be valid if it aligns with the original, pure, and uncompromising monotheism that he lived by. This establishes a direct link between the faith of Muslims and the faith of Abraham, showing that the essence of true guidance throughout history has been the worship of Allah alone.

Verse 136

قُولُوا آمَنَّا بِاللَّهِ وَمَا أُنزِلَ إِلَيْنَا وَمَا أُنزِلَ إِلَىٰ إِبْرَاهِيمَ وَإِسْمَاعِيلَ وَإِسْحَاقَ وَيَعْقُوبَ وَالْأَسْبَاطِ وَمَا أُوتِيَ مُوسَىٰ وَعِيسَىٰ وَمَا أُوتِيَ النَّبِيُّونَ مِن رَّبِّهِمْ لَا نُفَرِّقُ بَيْنَ أَحَدٍ مِّنْهُمْ وَنَحْنُ لَهُ مُسْلِمُونَ
Translation: Say, (O believers), “We believe in Allah, in what has been revealed to us, in what was revealed to Abraham, Ishmael, Isaac, Jacob, and his descendants, and in what was given to Moses, Jesus, and (all) the prophets from their Lord. We make no distinction between any of them, and to Him (Allah) we submit.”
Reflection: This verse is a call not only to believe in Allah but also to embrace the entire span of His divine guidance as it has been revealed throughout history. By stating, “We believe in Allah and what has been revealed to us”, it affirms the Qur’an as the most recent and final link in a continuous chain of revelations from Allah. Yet this belief is not limited to the final revelation. It is rooted in acknowledging and honoring all previous scriptures and prophets mentioned by name here—Abraham, Ishmael, Isaac, Jacob, Moses, and Jesus—as well as the descendants of Jacob and all other prophets sent by Allah. This reflects a central tenet of Islam: that all true prophets were bearers of the same divine truth, sent to guide humanity in different times and places. The statement, “We make no distinction between any of them”, directly challenges the tendency—often driven by religious identity—to value certain prophets or revelations while disregarding others. In Islam, true faith means accepting the divine origin of every prophet’s mission, even if their laws and practices varied according to the needs of their communities. This approach places Islam not as a break from the past but as its continuation and completion, affirming the unity of Allah’s message across history. The verse concludes with the declaration, “And to Him (Allah) we submit”, encapsulating the heart of Islamic belief: full surrender to Allah alone. This submission unites all prophets and revelations into a single purpose—calling humanity to worship Allah without partners. By affirming belief in all prophets and scriptures, this verse demonstrates the fairness and openness of the Islamic message. It invites unity among those who sincerely seek truth, while contrasting this inclusive vision with the narrower approaches of those who accept some prophets but reject others.

Verse 137

فَإِنْ آمَنُوا بِمِثْلِ مَا آمَنتُم بِهِ فَقَدِ اهْتَدَوا ۖ وَّإِن تَوَلَّوْا فَإِنَّمَا هُمْ فِي شِقَاقٍ ۖ فَسَيَكْفِيكَهُمُ اللَّهُ ۚ وَهُوَ السَّمِيعُ الْعَلِيمُ
Translation: So if they believe in what you believe, then they have indeed been rightly guided. But if they turn away, they are only in opposition, and Allah will be sufficient for you against them. And He (Allah) is the All-Hearing, All-Knowing.
Reflection: After affirming belief in all prophets and revelations, this verse explains the consequence of accepting or rejecting this unified understanding of faith. If others embrace this belief in Allah and in all His messengers—without discrimination—then they are on the correct path. But if they turn away, their stance becomes one of opposition, placing themselves in conflict with the truth that has been clearly presented. This is not merely a difference in opinion; it is described as an active resistance, a refusal that leads to separation from the central message of divine guidance. The words “and Allah will be sufficient for you against them” serve as reassurance. Opposition can seem daunting, particularly when it comes from groups with influence and support. This statement promises the Prophet and the believers that they are under Allah’s protection. His sufficiency means safety from harm and certainty that the truth will prevail despite resistance. The verse closes with the reminder that Allah is “the All-Hearing, the All-Knowing.” He hears every statement made, whether in public or in private, and He knows the intentions and inner realities behind people’s words and actions. This assures the believers that Allah’s awareness is complete, and that no act of opposition, however subtle, escapes His knowledge. With His protection and His perfect awareness, the believers can remain steadfast, trusting that the ultimate outcome rests with Him alone.

Verse 138

صِبْغَةَ اللَّهِ ۖ وَمَنْ أَحْسَنُ مِنَ اللَّهِ صِبْغَةً ۖ وَنَحْنُ لَهُ عَابِدُونَ
Translation: (We take our) color from Allah — and who can give a better color (to life) than Allah? And we worship (none but) Him.
Reflection: In this verse, faith is compared to a distinctive "color" or "hue," symbolizing the spiritual identity and purity granted by Allah. Just as color shapes how something is seen, this divine "hue" shapes the believer’s entire life and outlook. It represents the religion that comes directly from Allah — unmatched in purity, beauty, and truth. The rhetorical question, “Who can give a better color than Allah?” underscores that no source can provide a more perfect way of life, spiritual purification, or inner transformation than Him. The verse closes with the believers’ declaration: “And we worship Him alone.” This unites the metaphor of divine color with the reality of devotion, showing that this spiritual identity is not just a label but a lived commitment to worshiping and serving Allah exclusively.

Verse 139

قُلْ أَتُحَاجُّونَنَا فِي اللَّهِ وَهُوَ رَبُّنَا وَرَبُّكُمْ وَلَنَا أَعْمَالُنَا وَلَكُمْ أَعْمَالُكُمْ وَنَحْنُ لَهُ مُخْلِصُونَ
Translation: Say, (O Muhammad), “Do you argue with us about Allah, while He is our Lord and your Lord? We have our deeds, and you have your deeds. And to Him (Allah alone) we are devoted.”
Reflection: This verse challenges the pointlessness of disputing about Allah when His Lordship over all people is an undeniable fact. The argument here is not about Allah’s existence or essence but about His decisions—specifically, the sending of Prophet Muhammad, the abrogation of earlier Jewish and Christian laws, and the special favor Allah has shown to the Prophet and his community. Such disputes, the verse suggests, often arise from envy and a sense of exclusive entitlement to Allah’s favor. The statement “while He is our Lord and your Lord” exposes the contradiction in their argument. If Allah is equally the Lord of both parties, there should be no grounds for claiming monopoly over His favor. The phrase “We have our deeds, and you have your deeds” shifts the focus from mere argumentation to actual conduct. It implicitly invites the other party to consider which set of deeds is closer to righteousness and therefore more worthy of divine pleasure. Finally, “And to Him (Allah alone) we are devoted” underscores the believers’ complete sincerity in worship. While others may mix their devotion with practices contrary to pure monotheism, the believers dedicate their worship entirely to Allah. This purity of devotion, combined with righteous action, is presented as the true basis for Allah’s favor. By highlighting shared beliefs (Allah as the universal Lord), personal accountability for deeds, and the principle of pure devotion, the verse addresses the roots of religious contention and reaffirms Islam’s uncompromising monotheism.

Verse 140

أَمْ تَقُولُونَ إِنَّ إِبْرَاهِيمَ وَإِسْمَاعِيلَ وَإِسْحَاقَ وَيَعْقُوبَ وَالْأَسْبَاطَ كَانُوا هُودًا أَوْ نَصَارَىٰ ۗ قُلْ أَأَنتُمْ أَعْلَمُ أَمِ اللَّهُ ۗ وَمَنْ أَظْلَمُ مِمَّن كَتَمَ شَهَادَةً عِندَهُ مِنَ اللَّهِ ۗ وَمَا اللَّهُ بِغَافِلٍ عَمَّا تَعْمَلُونَ
Translation: Or do you claim that Abraham, Ishmael, Isaac, Jacob, and his descendants were Jews or Christians? Say, (O Muhammad), “Are you more knowledgeable, or is Allah?” And who could be more unjust than those who conceal the testimony they have received from Allah? And Allah is never unaware of what you do.
Reflection: This verse directly challenges the claim that early prophets—Abraham, Ishmael, Isaac, Jacob, and their descendants—were followers of Judaism or Christianity. Such a claim ignores the historical reality that these prophets lived long before either faith emerged as a distinct religion. By attributing later religious identities to them, the argument becomes not only inaccurate but also misleading. The rhetorical question, “Are you more knowledgeable, or is Allah?” confronts those making the claim with the reality of divine knowledge. Allah knows the truth of history and revelation, and His account is definitive. Abraham and his descendants were devoted monotheists who submitted entirely to Allah, free from the divisions and labels that came centuries later. The verse then warns against a deeper wrongdoing: “And who could be more unjust than those who conceal the testimony they have received from Allah?” This refers to religious leaders or scholars who possess knowledge of the truth but intentionally hide it. Such concealment may be motivated by pride, fear of losing influence, or a desire to preserve established traditions, even when those traditions are based on falsehood. Finally, “Allah is never unaware of what you do” serves as a solemn reminder that nothing is hidden from Allah. Whether openly declared or quietly suppressed, every action is known to Him, and deliberate distortion of divine truth is among the gravest injustices. This verse reinforces the message of Verse 135, calling people back to the pure monotheism of Abraham—free from later religious divisions and firmly rooted in worship of Allah alone.

Verse 141

تِلْكَ أُمَّةٌ قَدْ خَلَتْ ۖ لَهَا مَا كَسَبَتْ وَلَكُم مَّا كَسَبْتُمْ ۖ وَلَا تُسْأَلُونَ عَمَّا كَانُوا يَعْمَلُونَ
Translation: That was a nation who has (already) passed away. Whatever they earned (of deeds and their results) is theirs alone, and whatever you earn (of deeds and their results) is yours alone. And you will not be held accountable for what they did.
Reflection: This verse, repeating the statement from Verse 134, serves as a powerful closing to the preceding discussion about prophets and later religious claims. By echoing the earlier message, it underscores that salvation and honor before Allah are not inherited through lineage, historical association, or belonging to a particular community. The deeds of those past generations belong solely to them; their merits or failings are not transferable. Likewise, each person will face the outcome of their own actions. This principle shifts the focus from pride in ancestry to personal responsibility in faith and conduct. The repetition here is intentional, leaving no doubt that, before Allah, what matters is a person’s own sincerity, worship, and righteousness—not the achievements or legacy of others.

Verse 142

سَيَقُولُ السُّفَهَاءُ مِنَ النَّاسِ مَا وَلَّاهُمْ عَن قِبْلَتِهِمُ الَّتِي كَانُوا عَلَيْهَا ۚ قُل لِّلَّهِ الْمَشْرِقُ وَالْمَغْرِبُ ۚ يَهْدِي مَن يَشَاءُ إِلَىٰ صِرَاطٍ مُّسْتَقِيمٍ
Translation: The foolish among the people will say, “What has turned them away from their (former) direction of prayer (qiblah), which they used to face?” Say, (O Muhammad), “To Allah belong the east and the west. He guides whom He wills to the straight path.”
Reflection: This verse anticipates the reaction of certain people to the change in the direction of prayer. Before this change, Muslims in Madinah prayed facing Jerusalem, a direction also recognized in Jewish and Christian practice. The verse foresees that some would question, criticize, or even mock the shift to facing the Kaaba in Makkah. The phrase “will say” (in the future tense) indicates that, at the time this verse was revealed, the objection had not yet been voiced. It served as advance notice to the believers that the change in qiblah would be a significant event, provoking doubts and providing an opportunity for those inclined to question the Prophet’s leadership. Allah instructs Prophet Muhammad to respond by affirming that both east and west—indeed all directions—belong to Him. This emphasizes that no place or direction has inherent sanctity apart from what Allah designates. The choice of a qiblah is therefore an expression of divine wisdom, not human preference, and it can change whenever Allah commands. The concluding statement, “He guides whom He wills to the straight path,” shifts the focus from geography to guidance. Accepting the new qiblah is presented as a sign of being on the straight path granted by Allah. Those who follow the Prophet’s direction in this matter demonstrate alignment with divine guidance, while those who persist in questioning it reveal a deeper spiritual misalignment.

Verse 143

وَكَذَٰلِكَ جَعَلْنَاكُمْ أُمَّةً وَسَطًا لِّتَكُونُوا شُهَدَاءَ عَلَى النَّاسِ وَيَكُونَ الرَّسُولُ عَلَيْكُمْ شَهِيدًا ۗ وَمَا جَعَلْنَا الْقِبْلَةَ الَّتِي كُنتَ عَلَيْهَا إِلَّا لِنَعْلَمَ مَن يَتَّبِعُ الرَّسُولَ مِمَّن يَنقَلِبُ عَلَىٰ عَقِبَيْهِ ۚ وَإِن كَانَتْ لَكَبِيرَةً إِلَّا عَلَى الَّذِينَ هَدَى اللَّهُ ۗ وَمَا كَانَ اللَّهُ لِيُضِيعَ إِيمَانَكُمْ ۚ إِنَّ اللَّهَ بِالنَّاسِ لَرَءُوفٌ رَّحِيمٌ
Translation: And thus We have made you (believers) a balanced community so that you may be witnesses over the people, and the Messenger may be a witness over you. We only appointed your former direction of prayer (qiblah) to clearly distinguish those who would follow the Messenger from those who would turn back on their heels. And indeed, it is difficult—except for those whom Allah has guided. And Allah would never discount your (previous acts of) faith. Indeed, Allah is Ever Gracious and Most Merciful to the people.
Reflection: This verse links the change in the qiblah (direction of prayer) to the identity and mission of the Muslim community—referred to in the Qur’an as the Ummah. In the previous verse, believers were described as being guided to the straight path, a divine favor. Here, Allah mentions another favor: making them a “balanced community.” This balance refers to moderation in belief, practice, and character—avoiding both excess and neglect. For example, courage lies between cowardice and recklessness; generosity lies between stinginess and extravagance; justice lies between harshness and misplaced leniency. The Muslim community is thus placed in the middle ground, free from the distortions of those who exaggerated their religion and those who fell short in upholding it. This “balanced” nature is not just an inner quality; it equips believers for a great responsibility—being “witnesses over the people.” This role applies both in this life and the Hereafter. In this world, it means serving as a living testimony to truth, justice, and the divine message, becoming a reference point for all nations. On the Day of Judgment, the Muslim community will testify that the messengers of Allah delivered their message, and the Messenger Muhammad will, in turn, testify about his community’s adherence to the truth. The verse explains that the earlier qiblah (towards Jerusalem) was appointed as a means to test and distinguish the sincere followers of the Messenger from those whose commitment was weak. When the direction was changed to the Kaaba in Makkah, it became a clear dividing line between those who accepted the change without hesitation and those who resisted it. This shift revealed the sincerity of faith, to expose those whose commitment may have been superficial, causing them to “turn back on their heels”—a metaphor for rejecting the new command and reverting to their previous state. The verse acknowledges that this change was a difficult test, a significant spiritual challenge requiring submission and steadfastness even when the wisdom behind it was not immediately clear. However, it was “difficult except for those whom Allah has guided,” emphasizing that true guidance is a divine gift that enables one to face such trials with confidence and conviction. It then reassures believers about the effect of these changes on their past deeds: “Allah would never discount your (previous acts of) faith.” This is a profound comfort, especially for those who feared that following the earlier qiblah might nullify their prayers or devotion. The verse makes it clear that Allah’s guidance does not invalidate past obedience; His mercy ensures that even rulings later replaced remain valid for their time, and the devotion shown under them is fully rewarded. The verse concludes with a reminder of Allah’s attributes: “Ever Gracious and Most Merciful to the people.” This underscores His constant kindness, which protects believers from harm, and His encompassing mercy, which ensures that their efforts are rewarded and that their trials are guided with compassion.

Verse 144

قَدْ نَرَىٰ تَقَلُّبَ وَجْهِكَ فِي السَّمَاءِ ۖ فَلَنُوَلِّيَنَّكَ قِبْلَةً تَرْضَاهَا ۚ فَوَلِّ وَجْهَكَ شَطْرَ الْمَسْجِدِ الْحَرَامِ ۚ وَحَيْثُ مَا كُنتُمْ فَوَلُّوا وُجُوهَكُمْ شَطْرَهُ ۗ وَإِنَّ الَّذِينَ أُوتُوا الْكِتَابَ لَيَعْلَمُونَ أَنَّهُ الْحَقُّ مِن رَّبِّهِمْ ۗ وَمَا اللَّهُ بِغَافِلٍ عَمَّا يَعْمَلُونَ
Translation: Indeed, We have seen you, (O Muhammad), turning your face toward the heaven (seeking guidance). Now We will direct you toward a direction of prayer (qiblah) that will please you. So turn your face toward the Sacred Mosque (al-Masjid al-Haram). And wherever you (believers) may be, turn your faces toward it (in prayer). Indeed, those who were given the Scripture certainly know that this (command) is the truth from their Lord. And Allah is never unaware of what they do.
Reflection: This verse describes Prophet Muhammad’s quiet longing for divine instruction regarding the qiblah—the direction Muslims face in prayer. His repeated glances toward the sky reflected the depth of his hope and anticipation for guidance on this matter. Allah acknowledges this yearning, reassuring the Prophet that he will now be directed to a qiblah that will bring him satisfaction. The shift to the Sacred Mosque (al-Masjid al-Haram in Mecca) was not an arbitrary change but a deliberate return to the House established by Prophet Ibrahim as a pure centre of monotheistic worship. The command to “turn your face toward the Sacred Mosque” is immediately extended to all believers, wherever they may be, making this a universal and unifying practice for the Muslim community. Some scholars understand “al-Masjid al-Haram” here as referring specifically to the Ka‘bah itself, the central structure within the sacred precinct. The verse also addresses the People of the Book, noting that those with knowledge among them recognize the truth and divine origin of this command. Despite this awareness, some disregard it. The closing statement—“And Allah is never unaware of what they do”—serves both as a warning to those who conceal the truth and as reassurance to believers that Allah’s knowledge encompasses all actions. This verse establishes the Ka‘bah as the permanent and definitive qiblah for Muslims worldwide, strengthening unity in worship and reaffirming the community’s link to the Abrahamic legacy.

Verse 145

وَلَئِنْ أَتَيْتَ الَّذِينَ أُوتُوا الْكِتَابَ بِكُلِّ آيَةٍ مَّا تَبِعُوا قِبْلَتَكَ ۚ وَمَا أَنتَ بِتَابِعٍ قِبْلَتَهُمْ ۚ وَمَا بَعْضُهُم بِتَابِعٍ قِبْلَةَ بَعْضٍ ۚ وَلَئِنِ اتَّبَعْتَ أَهْوَاءَهُم مِّن بَعْدِ مَا جَاءَكَ مِنَ الْعِلْمِ ۙ إِنَّكَ إِذًا لَّمِنَ الظَّالِمِينَ
Translation: And (even) if you were to present every possible proof to the People of the Book, they would still not follow your direction of prayer (qiblah). And neither will you follow their qiblah. Nor will they follow one another’s qiblah. And if you were to follow their desires after (all) the knowledge that has come to you, you would certainly be among the wrongdoers.
Reflection: This verse describes the firm resistance shown by some among the People of the Book toward divine guidance, even when it is supported by clear and undeniable proof. It had already been established that their learned scholars recognized the truth, yet they did not act upon it. This deliberate refusal—despite knowledge—shows that their rejection of the qiblah was not due to confusion or lack of evidence. Rather, it was an entrenched opposition, making it necessary to clearly state that they would not follow the Muslim qiblah. This prevents any misunderstanding that they might accept it if only the truth were made clearer. The phrase "the People of the Book" here refers to those mentioned in the previous verse: "Indeed, those who were given the Scripture certainly know that this is the truth from their Lord." Since the conduct of their scholars influences the general public, their refusal to follow the qiblah made it even less likely for ordinary people to do so. The verse also stresses that the Prophet Muhammad would not follow their qiblah, thereby affirming the independence of the Muslim community’s worship and removing any hope that believers would return to the former direction toward Jerusalem. The statement "Nor will they follow one another’s qiblah" points out that even within the People of the Book, there were divisions—such as between Jews and Christians—over their own direction of prayer. Finally, the verse issues a strong warning: if one were to follow the desires of others after receiving complete and certain knowledge from Allah, it would be a grave wrongdoing. The phrase "after (all) the knowledge that has come to you" magnifies the weight of the revealed guidance, portraying it as comprehensive and leaving no excuse for deviation. This underscores the distinct identity of the Muslim faith and the necessity of unwavering adherence to the guidance given by Allah.

Verse 146

الَّذِينَ آتَيْنَاهُمُ الْكِتَابَ يَعْرِفُونَهُ كَمَا يَعْرِفُونَ أَبْنَاءَهُمْ ۖ وَإِنَّ فَرِيقًا مِّنْهُمْ لَيَكْتُمُونَ الْحَقَّ وَهُمْ يَعْلَمُونَ
Translation: Those to whom We gave the Scripture recognize it (as clearly) as they recognize their own children. Yet, a group among them knowingly conceals the truth.
Reflection: This verse affirms that those who received earlier revelations—particularly the scholars of the Torah and the Gospel—possessed unmistakable knowledge of the truth of Prophet Muhammad’s message. Their recognition was not based on vague impressions or uncertain reasoning; it was as clear and instinctive as the way a parent recognizes their own child. The Arabic expression used here—“to know something as one knows one’s own children”—is an idiom signifying absolute certainty, leaving no room for doubt. In this context, it underscores that the People of the Book were fully aware of the Prophet’s foretold qualities and the truth of the revelation he brought, as described in their own scriptures. However, the verse points out a serious moral failing: despite this deep and personal recognition, a group among them deliberately concealed the truth. This concealment was intentional, not due to ignorance or misunderstanding. Such suppression reflects a conscious rejection of what they knew to be from Allah, making their action especially blameworthy. This also explains the attitude described in the previous verse—why they refused to acknowledge or follow the Muslim qiblah despite recognizing its authenticity. Their opposition was not caused by a lack of evidence, but by a willful decision to hide and resist the truth they knew.

Verse 147

الْحَقُّ مِن رَّبِّكَ ۖ فَلَا تَكُونَنَّ مِنَ الْمُمْتَرِينَ
Translation: (This is) the truth from your Lord, so never be among the doubters.
Reflection: This verse directly addresses Prophet Muhammad, affirming that the revelation given to him is the ultimate truth, coming only from Allah. The instruction “never be among the doubters” does not suggest that the Prophet himself had doubts. Rather, it serves to emphasize the absolute certainty of revelation and to reassure the believers. By addressing the Prophet, the verse delivers a powerful message to the entire community: if even the Messenger, who is closest to Allah, is reminded in such strong terms, then everyone else must be even more cautious not to let doubt enter their hearts. This rhetorical method is common in the Qur’an: when the Prophet is addressed, it often carries a broader lesson for all Muslims. The phrase “from your Lord” underscores the personal and direct nature of this guidance, reminding both the Prophet and the community that the source of truth is none other than Allah, whose word admits no uncertainty. In this way, the verse highlights both the certainty of revelation and the need for steadfast confidence in it, regardless of external challenges or objections.

Verse 148

وَلِكُلٍّ وِجْهَةٌ هُوَ مُوَلِّيهَا ۖ فَاسْتَبِقُوا الْخَيْرَاتِ ۚ أَيْنَ مَا تَكُونُوا يَأْتِ بِكُمُ اللَّهُ جَمِيعًا ۚ إِنَّ اللَّهَ عَلَىٰ كُلِّ شَيْءٍ قَدِيرٌ
Translation: And for each (nation or community) there is a direction to which they turn. So compete with one another in (all that is) good. Wherever you may be, Allah will bring you all together (for judgement). Surely Allah is Most Capable of everything.
Reflection: This verse shifts the focus from the earlier debate about the qiblah to a broader perspective. After explaining the truth of the Muslim qiblah and making clear that opposition from others would not diminish its validity, this verse redirects the believers’ attention to what truly matters: striving in goodness. The opening statement, “And for each (nation or community) there is a direction to which they turn,” acknowledges that different groups have their own practices and orientations. For Muslims, this serves as guidance not to be preoccupied with disputes over such matters. Opposition from others does not weaken the truth established by Allah. Instead, believers are instructed to channel their efforts into what is meaningful: “So compete with one another in (all that is) good.” This encourages Muslims to prioritize righteous action and moral excellence, strengthening their society and drawing closer to Allah through service, justice, and good deeds. The verse continues, “Wherever you may be, Allah will bring you all together (for judgement).” This reassures the believers that their deeds are never lost or forgotten. Whether near or far, in different lands or circumstances, all will ultimately be gathered before Allah. This emphasizes both accountability and the unity of the community under Allah’s will. The verse concludes with, “Surely Allah is Most Capable of everything,” affirming that nothing lies beyond Allah’s power. This ending reinforces the message that just as Allah unites all people for judgement, He is fully capable of bringing about every consequence and fulfilling every promise.

Verse 149

وَمِنْ حَيْثُ خَرَجْتَ فَوَلِّ وَجْهَكَ شَطْرَ الْمَسْجِدِ الْحَرَامِ ۖ وَإِنَّهُ لَلْحَقُّ مِن رَّبِّكَ ۗ وَمَا اللَّهُ بِغَافِلٍ عَمَّا تَعْمَلُونَ
Translation: And from wherever you set out (O Muhammad), turn your face toward the Sacred Mosque (for prayer). Indeed, this is the truth from your Lord. And Allah is never unaware of what you do.
Reflection: This verse reaffirms the command first given in Verse 144 concerning the direction of prayer. The words “from wherever you set out” emphasize that no matter the location or circumstances, the Prophet—and by extension, all Muslims—must turn toward the Sacred Mosque (al-Masjid al-Haram) during prayer. The statement “Indeed, this is the truth from your Lord” highlights the authority and certainty of this instruction. It is not a matter of human choice or cultural preference, but a divinely established truth that defines the unity and distinct identity of the Muslim community. The verse continues with a reminder: “And Allah is never unaware of what you do.” This serves as both reassurance and caution. Allah’s complete awareness ensures that sincere obedience will never go unnoticed, while negligence or disregard for His command will not escape His knowledge. Through repetition and emphasis, the verse reinforces the central importance of the qiblah in worship and calls believers to observe it with both outward consistency and inward sincerity.

Verse 150

وَمِنْ حَيْثُ خَرَجْتَ فَوَلِّ وَجْهَكَ شَطْرَ الْمَسْجِدِ الْحَرَامِ ۚ وَحَيْثُ مَا كُنتُمْ فَوَلُّوا وُجُوهَكُمْ شَطْرَهُ لِئَلَّا يَكُونَ لِلنَّاسِ عَلَيْكُمْ حُجَّةٌ إِلَّا الَّذِينَ ظَلَمُوا مِنْهُمْ فَلَا تَخْشَوْهُمْ وَاخْشَوْنِي وَلِأُتِمَّ نِعْمَتِي عَلَيْكُمْ وَلَعَلَّكُمْ تَهْتَدُونَ
Translation: And from wherever you set out, turn your face toward the Sacred Mosque (for prayer). And wherever you (believers) may be, turn your faces toward it (when you pray), so that people will have no argument against you—except those who persist in wrongdoing. So do not fear them, but fear Me, so that I may complete My favor upon you and so that you may be (rightly) guided.
Reflection: This verse repeats for the third time the command to face the Sacred Mosque (al-Masjid al-Haram) in prayer, showing its central importance. Here the instruction is extended universally: not only the Prophet Muhammad but all Muslims, in every place and circumstance, must turn toward the Kaaba when they pray. The repeated phrasing—“And from wherever you set out” and “And wherever you may be”—removes any doubt that the ruling applies only to the Prophet. It confirms that this direction of prayer is a binding and enduring command for the entire Muslim community. Repetition in the Qur’an often serves to reinforce certainty, ensuring that the instruction becomes firmly established in the hearts of believers. The verse provides a key wisdom behind this command: “so that people will have no argument against you.” By establishing a clear and divinely ordained qiblah linked to the legacy of Prophet Abraham, Muslims are given a solid foundation that cannot be undermined by opponents. Any criticism that remains after such clarity comes only from those who persist in wrongdoing. The command also carries a spiritual dimension: “So do not fear them, but fear Me.” Believers are reminded that their focus must not be on the objections or hostility of others, but on awe and reverence for Allah alone. The phrase “so that I may complete My favor upon you” shows that facing the Kaaba is not only about prayer direction, but also about the perfection of divine guidance. This qiblah unites the community, marks their distinct identity, and represents Allah’s continued favor. The verse concludes by linking this practice to the goal of guidance. Observing the qiblah with sincerity demonstrates readiness to submit fully to Allah’s will, and it ensures that His favor and guidance continue to shape the path of the believers. In this way, the command affirms both the unity and the spiritual direction of the Muslim community.

Verse 151

كَمَا أَرْسَلْنَا فِيكُمْ رَسُولًا مِّنكُمْ يَتْلُو عَلَيْكُمْ آيَاتِنَا وَيُزَكِّيكُمْ وَيُعَلِّمُكُمُ الْكِتَابَ وَالْحِكْمَةَ وَيُعَلِّمُكُم مَّا لَمْ تَكُونُوا تَعْلَمُونَ
Translation: Just as We have sent among you a messenger from yourselves, reciting to you Our revelations, purifying you, teaching you the Book and wisdom, and teaching you what you did not know.
Reflection: This verse continues the theme of Allah’s favor mentioned in the previous verse, while broadening the perspective. It recalls a great blessing already bestowed on the Muslim community: Allah sent a Messenger, Prophet Muhammad, from among themselves. This detail emphasizes closeness and familiarity, making the blessing even more profound, since the Messenger was not a distant or foreign figure. The verse deliberately echoes the prayer of Prophet Abraham in Verse 129, creating a strong link between the two prophets and their missions. This connection highlights that Islam is not a new invention but part of Abraham’s legacy, a continuation of his supplication that Allah would raise a messenger among his descendants. In the same way, the establishment of the qiblah (direction of prayer) mentioned earlier is not arbitrary. The mission of Prophet Muhammad is described here in terms of four central roles. He recites Allah’s revelations, the Qur’an, as a source of guidance. He purifies people, cleansing them from false beliefs and harmful practices, which also elevates and strengthens the soul’s natural capacity for good. He teaches them the Book, meaning the Qur’an as a source of divine legislation. Alongside this, he teaches wisdom, which refers to the insight and guidance needed to apply the divine law correctly and avoid error or corruption. Finally, the phrase “and teaching you what you did not know” expands the scope of his mission even further. It points to knowledge beyond legislation and wisdom, including insights into human life, history, the reality of the hereafter, and the ways communities and nations function. The repetition of the phrase “and teaching you” reinforces that this is not a restatement but an additional gift: knowledge that the community did not previously possess. In this way, the verse portrays the transformative impact of Prophet Muhammad’s mission. Through revelation, purification, and teaching, he elevated the early Muslims from ignorance and harmful customs to knowledge, moral refinement, and wisdom—preparing them for their role as a community guided by Allah and entrusted with guidance for others.

Verse 152

فَاذْكُرُونِي أَذْكُرْكُمْ وَاشْكُرُوا لِي وَلَا تَكْفُرُونِ
Translation: So remember Me; I will remember you. And be grateful to Me, and do not deny Me.
Reflection: After mentioning Allah’s favors—such as sending a Messenger, Prophet Muhammad, and guiding the believers—this verse turns to the heart of the relationship between Allah and His servants. It calls believers to live in constant remembrance and gratitude, drawing them into a direct and intimate connection with their Creator. The opening statement, “So remember Me; I will remember you,” is built on the same word root, highlighting a reciprocal relationship. Believers are urged to remember Allah, not only with their tongues through praise and glorification but also with their hearts and actions by recalling His greatness, attributes, commands, and prohibitions. Such remembrance is not an empty ritual; it uplifts the soul, brings inner peace, and opens the way to Allah’s special mercy. In response comes Allah’s promise: “I will remember you.” This remembrance signifies His care and favor—support, guidance, and blessings in this life, and honor and reward in the hereafter. It reassures the believer that no act of remembrance is unnoticed or forgotten. The command “And be grateful to Me” extends this connection further. Gratitude to Allah means recognizing His countless favors and responding with humility, obedience, and service. It is not merely a feeling but a way of life that strengthens the bond with the One who provides every blessing. The verse then concludes with a serious caution: “and do not deny Me.” This warns against ingratitude, which can appear in different forms. At its most severe, it is outright denial of Allah’s favors; at a lesser level, it is concealing them or neglecting to acknowledge them. Even heedless silence, though the weakest form, still falls short of true gratitude. In a world filled with distractions, this balance of remembrance and gratitude acts as a shield. It preserves faith, identity, and the believer’s living connection with Allah, the ultimate source of strength and guidance.

Verse 153

يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا اسْتَعِينُوا بِالصَّبْرِ وَالصَّلَاةِ ۚ إِنَّ اللَّهَ مَعَ الصَّابِرِينَ
Translation: O believers! Seek help through patience and prayer. Indeed, Allah is with the patient.
Reflection: This verse addresses the believers directly, guiding them to draw strength and support through two essential means: patience and prayer. These qualities are not presented as optional virtues but as vital tools for facing life’s trials and remaining steadfast in faith. The command to seek help through patience and prayer prepares the Muslim community for the challenges ahead—whether difficulties in daily life or greater tests such as hardship, loss, or even moments of collective struggle. It emphasizes that perseverance and turning to Allah in prayer form the foundation of resilience. Patience here encompasses steadfastness in obeying Allah, restraint from wrongdoing, and endurance in times of suffering. Prayer serves as the believer’s lifeline, a constant reminder of Allah’s presence and a source of renewed strength. The assurance, “Indeed, Allah is with the patient,” is deeply comforting. It means that those who remain patient are not left to face struggles alone but are supported by Allah’s special care, guidance, and companionship. This transforms patience from a passive act into an active form of worship that brings the believer closer to Allah. Placed after the verses about community identity and divine favor, this verse provides practical instruction on how to uphold that identity—with inner discipline, steady faith, and a living connection to Allah.

Verse 154

وَلَا تَقُولُوا لِمَن يُقْتَلُ فِي سَبِيلِ اللَّهِ أَمْوَاتٌ ۚ بَلْ أَحْيَاءٌ وَلَـٰكِن لَّا تَشْعُرُونَ
Translation: Never say about those who are killed in the cause of Allah, “They are dead.” Rather, they are alive—but you do not perceive it.
Reflection: This verse deals with one of the most difficult aspects of striving in the cause of Allah: the sacrifice of life. Believers are instructed not to label those who are killed in this path as merely “dead.” The verse makes it clear that, in reality, they are alive, though their life is not accessible to our ordinary perception. This does not deny physical death in the worldly sense. Instead, it corrects a limited human understanding by pointing to a greater truth: that their sacrifice does not lead to nonexistence, but to a different, elevated form of life. The prohibition against calling them “dead” is a way of rejecting the idea that their end is like any other end. The statement, “Rather, they are alive—but you do not perceive it,” affirms their continued existence in a realm hidden from us. This life is of a higher quality, filled with blessings and honor, beyond what our senses can grasp. In this way, the verse presents sacrifice in the cause of Allah not as loss, but as triumph. Their life after death is a testimony to the enduring victory of faith. Linked to the previous verse about patience and prayer, this verse highlights that the path of faith may demand the greatest of sacrifices. Yet even in that sacrifice, there is reassurance: those who fall are not abandoned to nothingness but are raised to a living reality far greater than what we can comprehend.

Verse 155

وَلَنَبْلُوَنَّكُم بِشَيْءٍ مِّنَ الْخَوْفِ وَالْجُوعِ وَنَقْصٍ مِّنَ الْأَمْوَالِ وَالْأَنفُسِ وَالثَّمَرَاتِ ۗ وَبَشِّرِ الصَّابِرِينَ
Translation: And We will certainly test you with a bit of fear and hunger, and with loss of wealth, lives, and fruits (worldly goods). But give good news to those who are patient (who remain steadfast in these trials).
Reflection: Building on the earlier themes of patience (Verse 153) and sacrifice (Verse 154), this verse makes it explicit that believers will inevitably face trials. The tests mentioned include fear, hunger, and the loss of wealth, loved ones, and worldly goods. These are not described as punishments, but as part of the journey of faith—occasions that reveal sincerity, strengthen the heart, and nurture spiritual growth. The verse also implies that being recipients of Allah’s favor and guidance does not exempt believers from such hardships. On the contrary, enduring these trials is itself a sign of steadfastness, devotion, and trust in Allah’s decree. Through patience, believers not only purify their souls but also demonstrate that their commitment to faith is not tied to worldly benefit, but to love of Allah and obedience to Him. The expression “a bit” softens the news, suggesting that these trials, while difficult, are limited and not like the overwhelming punishments that befell past nations. Finally, the verse shifts from warning to comfort: “But give good news to those who are patient.” This promise introduces hope, assuring believers that those who endure with patience are not forgotten but are honored by Allah. It also prepares the ground for the next verse, which describes their dignified response to such trials.

Verse 156

الَّذِينَ إِذَا أَصَابَتْهُم مُّصِيبَةٌ قَالُوا إِنَّا لِلَّهِ وَإِنَّا إِلَيْهِ رَاجِعُونَ

Reflection: This verse portrays the response of those who are patient when struck by calamity, as mentioned in the previous verse. Their words express a fundamental truth: that they belong to Allah, and their final return is to Him. This declaration reflects humility, recognition of Allah’s sovereignty, and acceptance of His decree. It is not merely a statement, but a mindset that embodies trust, submission, and steadfastness in the face of hardship. By saying “Indeed, we belong to Allah and to Him we will return,” believers affirm two essential realities: first, that their lives and possessions are entrusted to Allah, who has full authority over them; and second, that their journey ultimately ends with Him, where reward and justice await. This perspective prevents despair and brings hope, even in the midst of loss. The verse shows that true patience is not passive endurance but an active response shaped by faith and understanding. Uttering these words reinforces belief, strengthens the heart, and reminds others of this truth. In this way, the phrase becomes both a personal comfort and a teaching for the community.

Verse 157

أُولَـٰئِكَ عَلَيْهِمْ صَلَوَاتٌ مِّن رَّبِّهِمْ وَرَحْمَةٌ ۖ وَأُولَـٰئِكَ هُمُ الْمُهْتَدُونَ
Translation: It is they who will receive blessings from their Lord and mercy. And it is they who are the (rightly) guided.
Reflection: This verse concludes the sequence of verses on patience and trials by declaring the special status of those who endure hardship with steadfast faith and say, “Indeed, to Allah we belong, and to Him we will return.” For such people, Allah promises two profound gifts: blessings and mercy. These are not temporary comforts but lasting signs of His favor and compassion. In addition, they are described as being the rightly guided, meaning they possess clarity of heart and direction that allows them to navigate life’s hardships without despair. This guidance shields them from falling into objection or doubt when calamities strike. Instead, they maintain patience, find strength in submission to Allah, and remain firm in their devotion. Their reward, therefore, is not only resilience in this life but also divine acknowledgment and support, marking them as recipients of Allah’s mercy and guidance.

Verse 158

إِنَّ الصَّفَا وَالْمَرْوَةَ مِن شَعَائِرِ اللَّهِ ۖ فَمَنْ حَجَّ الْبَيْتَ أَوِ اعْتَمَرَ فَلَا جُنَاحَ عَلَيْهِ أَن يَطَّوَّفَ بِهِمَا ۚ وَمَن تَطَوَّعَ خَيْرًا فَإِنَّ اللَّهَ شَاكِرٌ عَلِيمٌ
Translation: Indeed, (the hills of) Ṣafā and Marwah are among the symbols of Allah. So whoever makes the major pilgrimage (Hajj) or the minor pilgrimage (ʿUmrah), there is no blame upon them for walking between them (the two hills). And whoever does good voluntarily, Allah is truly Appreciative, All-Knowing.
Reflection: This verse addresses a specific rite of pilgrimage connected to the Kaʿbah in Mecca. Ṣafā and Marwah are two small hills near the Sacred House, and walking between them—known is part of both Hajj and ʿUmrah. Before Islam, the pagan Arabs had placed idols on these two hills, which made some of the early Muslims hesitant about this practice, fearing it might resemble those pre-Islamic rituals. This verse resolves that concern by affirming that Ṣafā and Marwah are among the symbols established by Allah Himself. With the coming of Islam, the idols were removed, and their association with paganism ended. Their significance lies not in those past customs but in their designation as markers of worship. Thus, walking between them during pilgrimage is not only free of blame but is an act of devotion to Allah. The verse also clarifies the meaning of Hajj and ʿUmrah. Hajj is the greater pilgrimage, performed in the prescribed season, involving rites such as standing at ʿArafah and circumambulating the Kaʿbah. ʿUmrah is a lesser pilgrimage that can be performed at other times of the year and involves fewer rites. Both are acts of worship directed solely to Allah, distinguished from earlier practices that had been corrupted by idolatry. The verse then expands beyond the ritual itself: “And whoever does good voluntarily, Allah is truly Appreciative, All-Knowing.” This reassures believers that any voluntary act of goodness—whether within pilgrimage or in other aspects of life—is valued by Allah. His name “Appreciative” signifies that no act of obedience or kindness, however small, is overlooked, while His name “All-Knowing” reminds us that He is fully aware of the sincerity and intention behind each deed. In this way, the verse shows how Islam both purified existing practices and encouraged believers to go beyond the minimum requirements, assuring them that every act of goodness is cherished and rewarded by Allah.

Verse 159

إِنَّ الَّذِينَ يَكْتُمُونَ مَا أَنزَلْنَا مِنَ الْبَيِّنَاتِ وَالْهُدَىٰ مِن بَعْدِ مَا بَيَّنَّاهُ لِلنَّاسِ فِي الْكِتَابِ ۙ أُولَـٰئِكَ يَلْعَنُهُمُ اللَّهُ وَيَلْعَنُهُمُ اللَّاعِنُونَ
Translation: Indeed, those who conceal the (clear) proofs and guidance that We have revealed—after We have made them clear for the people in the Book—are cursed by Allah and (also) by all who curse.
Reflection: This verse issues a strong warning against those who deliberately hide the truths and guidance that Allah revealed in His scriptures, even though these truths were clearly explained for people. The wording “after We have made them clear for the people in the Book” intensifies the seriousness of the act. The guidance was not hidden or vague—it was openly clarified in the Torah and other scriptures. Concealing it, therefore, was not due to confusion or lack of understanding, but a deliberate suppression of truth despite its clarity. The term “concealment” here means more than silent withholding; it refers to actively hiding, suppressing, or covering up information that should be made known. Whether through words, actions, or deliberate silence, it is an ongoing act of injustice that deprives people of what Allah intended for them. By adding the phrase “for the people,” the verse shows the purpose behind making the truth clear: Allah revealed it for the benefit of humanity, so it would be shared widely and spread openly. To withhold it, then, is not just hiding knowledge but denying people their right to divine guidance. This makes the act even more blameworthy, as it combines injustice, misguidance, and betrayal of trust. The “(clear) proofs and guidance” in this verse refer especially to what was revealed in earlier scriptures, like the Torah, including the descriptions of prophets and other essential teachings. Those who persist in this concealment are condemned by Allah. The “curse” means being cast out from Allah’s mercy and favor, subjected instead to His displeasure and ultimate punishment. Moreover, this curse is reinforced by “all who curse”—meaning the righteous and the angels who denounce wrongdoing, as well as all who pray against injustice in general. Even without knowing names, people may condemn such behavior collectively. The warning is also directed at Muslims: to conceal or distort what Allah has revealed is a grave sin. This verse stresses the importance of honesty and responsibility in preserving and sharing religious knowledge, ensuring that guidance is passed on as Allah intended.

Verse 160

إِلَّا الَّذِينَ تَابُوا وَأَصْلَحُوا وَبَيَّنُوا فَأُولَـٰئِكَ أَتُوبُ عَلَيْهِمْ ۚ وَأَنَا التَّوَّابُ الرَّحِيمُ
Translation: Except those who repent, correct themselves, and openly declare (the truth which they had concealed). (For) those, I will accept their repentance, for I am the Accepter of Repentance, the Most Merciful.
Reflection: This verse provides an important exception to the stern warning given previously. It opens a path to redemption. The phrase “Except those who repent” shows that the curse mentioned earlier does not apply to them. However, repentance here is not limited to regret alone. It requires actively correcting the harm done by concealment—by making clear to people what was previously hidden.True repentance, therefore, involves three elements: sincere regret before Allah, amending one’s behavior, and openly declaring the truth that was concealed. By doing so, they undo the wrong and restore what was corrupted. For them, Allah gives reassurance: “(For) those, I will accept their repentance, for I am the Accepter of Repentance, the Most Merciful.” This promise highlights both Allah’s justice—requiring that the harm be corrected—and His immense mercy in welcoming those who sincerely turn back. Allah is described as being ever-ready to accept repentance and being Most Merciful, emphasizing His boundless grace and willingness to forgive those who sincerely seek His forgiveness and strive to do good.

Verse 161

إِنَّ الَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا وَمَاتُوا وَهُمْ كُفَّارٌ أُولَـٰئِكَ عَلَيْهِمْ لَعْنَةُ اللَّهِ وَالْمَلَائِكَةِ وَالنَّاسِ أَجْمَعِينَ

Reflection: This verse introduces another category of people who, like those previously mentioned, fall under Allah’s curse. After describing the curse upon the People of the Scripture who concealed what was revealed, the verse continues by speaking of the polytheists—those who entirely rejected faith and persisted in their denial until death. It is declared that such individuals will be cursed by Allah, the angels, and all of humanity. The mention of “all mankind” here is significant: it refers to the believers in monotheism, including the People of the Scripture, who also condemn polytheism. By contrast, those who concealed revelation were cursed by Allah, the angels, and the righteous among their own people, but not universally by all of humanity. This underlines the gravity of knowingly rejecting the truth and guidance once it has been made clear.

Verse 162

خَالِدِينَ فِيهَا ۖ لَا يُخَفَّفُ عَنْهُمُ الْعَذَابُ وَلَا هُمْ يُنظَرُونَ
Translation: They will remain in it forever. Their punishment will not be reduced, nor will they be (granted) any respite.
Reflection: This verse continues from the previous one, describing the fate of those who died in a state of disbelief. It declares that they will remain forever in a state of punishment. This punishment will never be eased, nor will they be granted even a moment of respite. The phrase “They will remain in it forever” emphasizes the lasting nature of this consequence. The additional statement that their punishment will neither be reduced nor interrupted underscores the severity and permanence of this condition. The verse conveys a powerful warning about the reality of ultimate accountability and the seriousness of rejecting the truth once it has been made clear.

Verse 163

وَإِلَـٰهُكُمْ إِلَـٰهٌ وَاحِدٌ ۖ لَّا إِلَـٰهَ إِلَّا هُوَ الرَّحْمَـٰنُ الرَّحِيمُ
Translation: And your Ilah (deity) is only one Ilah. There is no Ilah (deity worthy of worship) except Him — the Most Compassionate, the Most Merciful.
Reflection: This verse follows the description of those who denied faith and faced eternal punishment. In contrast, it reaffirms the central foundation of Islam: the absolute oneness of Allah. By saying “your Ilah (deity)”, the verse addresses all people—past, present, and future. Its immediate relevance, however, is to those who worship false deities, such as idols. The verse affirms that the true Ilah, the one who alone deserves worship, is Allah. The word Ilah in Arabic refers to anything that people take as an object of worship. Throughout history, humans have called idols, natural forces, animals, or even people their ilahs. Here, Allah makes clear that the true Ilah—the one genuinely deserving of worship—is only one. The name Allah is different from Ilah. While Ilah is a generic term for “a god” or “deity,” Allah is the unique, proper name of the One True God, who alone has the right to be worshipped. This distinction is crucial: many things may be falsely called ilahs (plural of ilah), but in reality, only Allah is the true Ilah. The statement “There is no Ilah (deity worthy of worship) except Him” is a complete declaration of monotheism. It negates all false objects of worship and affirms that only Allah is worthy of worship. This directly addresses those who associated partners with Allah, making clear that divinity cannot be shared or divided. The concluding attributes “the Most Compassionate, the Most Merciful” highlight that Allah’s oneness is not merely about His exclusive right to be worshipped, but also about His nature as the One who provides, sustains, and shows mercy. His compassion itself testifies to His divinity, for no false deity can bestow blessings or mercy as He does. Thus, the verse conveys a timeless truth: Allah is the sole divine being, the only one worthy of worship. Positioned after verses about disbelief and associating partners with Allah, this declaration corrects false beliefs—especially polytheism—by affirming that the true Ilah is one and unique.

Verse 164

إِنَّ فِي خَلْقِ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضِ وَاخْتِلَافِ اللَّيْلِ وَالنَّهَارِ وَالْفُلْكِ الَّتِي تَجْرِي فِي الْبَحْرِ بِمَا يَنفَعُ النَّاسَ وَمَا أَنزَلَ اللَّهُ مِنَ السَّمَاءِ مِن مَّاءٍ فَأَحْيَا بِهِ الْأَرْضَ بَعْدَ مَوْتِهَا وَبَثَّ فِيهَا مِن كُلِّ دَابَّةٍ وَتَصْرِيفِ الرِّيَاحِ وَالسَّحَابِ الْمُسَخَّرِ بَيْنَ السَّمَاءِ وَالْأَرْضِ لَآيَاتٍ لِّقَوْمٍ يَعْقِلُونَ
Translation: Indeed, in the creation of the heavens and the earth, in the alternation (succession) of night and day, in the ships that sail across the sea carrying what benefits people, in the water (rain) which Allah sends down from the sky—reviving the earth after it was lifeless and spreading upon it every kind of moving creature—in the directing of the winds, and in the clouds held between the heavens and the earth—(in all of this) are surely signs for people who use reason.
Reflection: After declaring the oneness of Allah, this verse turns to the natural world as a living testimony to His existence, power, and wisdom. It lists a variety of phenomena that human beings can observe: the vastness of the heavens and the earth, the regular alternation of night and day, the ships that navigate the seas for human benefit, the rain that revives dead land, the creatures that spread across the earth, the movement of the winds, and the drifting of clouds. Each of these is presented as a sign pointing to Allah. The alternation of night and day forms a continuous cycle of benefit: daylight enabling activity, and night granting rest and renewal. What may seem like a simple natural rhythm is, in truth, a profound sign of divine order. This alternation also carries a sign for those with deeper knowledge, as they recognize that the cycle of night and day results from the Earth’s rotation in relation to the sun. The seas, vast and life-sustaining, also testify to Allah’s power. By the natural laws Allah has established, ships are able to traverse their depths. This enables humanity to connect, trade, and explore, bringing immense benefit. Likewise, the winds serve many roles: they drive clouds to deliver rain, assist in navigation, and fulfill countless other needs. Rain, sent from the sky, restores barren earth to life, producing vegetation and sustaining countless creatures. This recurring cycle of life springing from lifelessness is a striking proof of Allah’s creative power and His continuing care for His creation. These signs are not simply to be seen but to be reflected upon. They are intended for people who truly use their intellect, for it is through thoughtful contemplation that one can recognize the Creator behind the design. Those who remain blind to these signs, despite their clarity, are shown to lack depth in their reasoning.

Verse 165

وَمِنَ النَّاسِ مَن يَتَّخِذُ مِن دُونِ اللَّهِ أَندَادًا يُحِبُّونَهُمْ كَحُبِّ اللَّهِ ۖ وَالَّذِينَ آمَنُوا أَشَدُّ حُبًّا لِّلَّهِ ۗ وَلَوْ يَرَى الَّذِينَ ظَلَمُوا إِذْ يَرَوْنَ الْعَذَابَ أَنَّ الْقُوَّةَ لِلَّهِ جَمِيعًا وَأَنَّ اللَّهَ شَدِيدُ الْعَذَابِ
Translation: And (yet, despite such clear signs of the oneness of Allah), among the people are some who take others as equals to Allah. They love them as (only) Allah should be loved. But the (true) believers love Allah far more. If only the wrongdoers could see—as they will one day—the (terrible) punishment (awaiting them on the Day of Resurrection), they would realize that all power belongs to Allah (alone) and that Allah is severe in punishment.
Reflection: After inviting people to reflect on the heavens, the earth, and all signs of Allah’s power, this verse highlights a striking contrast: despite such evidence of His oneness, some still associate others with Him. It explains that there are people who give their devotion to false deities, idols, or intermediaries, loving them with the kind of reverence and attachment that should belong only to Allah. This misplaced love is expressed in the words: “They love them as (only) Allah should be loved.” It describes a devotion that wrongly elevates others to a level of affection and reverence equal to, or even greater than, what should be directed to Allah alone. Believers, however, are distinct in that their love for Allah surpasses all other loves. The verse clarifies that their bond with Allah is stronger and deeper than the attachment idolaters feel toward their false gods. The believers’ love arises from recognizing Allah’s essence, His unique worthiness of devotion, and from seeking nearness to Him through purification of the heart and spirit. The verse then presents a solemn vision: “And if only the wrongdoers could see—as they will—the (terrible) punishment (awaiting them on the Day of Resurrection).” This phrase suggests that if they could witness their fate beforehand, they would immediately recognize the futility of their misplaced devotion. Calling them “wrongdoers” emphasizes the severity of associating others with Allah. At that time, all illusions of worldly strength will collapse, and they will grasp the ultimate truth: “that all power belongs to Allah (alone) and that Allah is severe in punishment.” Every form of power they imagined—whether from idols, leaders, or their own might—will prove meaningless. This verse serves as a stark reminder that true power belongs only to Allah, whose justice is absolute and whose retribution is severe. This prepares the reader for the verses that follow, which illustrate scenes from that Day.

Verse 166

إِذْ تَبَرَّأَ الَّذِينَ اتُّبِعُوا مِنَ الَّذِينَ اتَّبَعُوا وَرَأَوُا الْعَذَابَ وَتَقَطَّعَتْ بِهِمُ الْأَسْبَابُ
Translation: (On that Day) those who were followed will disown those who followed them. When they see the punishment (before them), the bonds that once united them will be cut off.
Reflection: This verse continues the scene of the Day of Judgment, building upon the previous description. It portrays the moment when leaders, idols, or figures of authority—those once followed in matters of worship or obedience—will turn away from their followers. On that Day, both groups will witness the punishment that awaits them, and every tie of loyalty, alliance, or shared devotion that bound them together in worldly life will be severed. What once appeared to be strong bonds will be shown to have no weight in the Hereafter. Those who were followed—the proponents of false worship, the ones who invited others to misguided beliefs—will completely renounce those who once trusted and revered them. This act of disowning occurs precisely when both parties are confronted with the reality of the punishment, leaving the followers abandoned. The cutting of ties is total and irreversible. It can be imagined like a climber depending on a rope to reach a treasured fruit, only for the rope to snap suddenly, causing a fatal fall. Just as the climber’s trust in the rope ends in disaster, so too will misplaced trust in false leaders collapse into ruin. Their disavowal means that any promises once made in this world—whether of benefit, protection, or intercession in the Hereafter—will be denied. Those who are followed will refuse to grant refuge, leaving their followers with nothing to rely on in that decisive moment.

Verse 167

It presents the desperate words of the followers, who, upon seeing themselves abandoned by those they once trusted, express bitter regret. They will wish for another chance in worldly life, so that they could renounce those whom they once revered and obeyed. But this wish will remain unfulfilled. Their opportunity to act differently existed only in their earthly life, and once it is gone, it will not return. Allah will expose before them the true consequences of their past actions—how their misplaced devotion and false attachments led only to loss. This unveiling will not correct their fate but will instead intensify their remorse. The verse closes with a stark reminder: there will be no escape from the Fire for those who persist in such false devotion. Their regret, no matter how strong, cannot undo what has already been sealed. This is not simply a warning about punishment but a powerful call to recognize the reality of accountability: that a genuine relationship with Allah must be established in this life, before the Hereafter makes all opportunities vanish.

Verse 168

يَا أَيُّهَا النَّاسُ كُلُوا مِمَّا فِي الْأَرْضِ حَلَالًا طَيِّبًا وَلَا تَتَّبِعُوا خُطُوَاتِ الشَّيْطَانِ ۚ إِنَّهُ لَكُمْ عَدُوٌّ مُّبِينٌ

Reflection: After affirming Allah’s oneness and recalling His blessings, this verse turns to correct practices that had strayed from His guidance, particularly regarding what is considered permissible and forbidden in provisions. It speaks not just to believers but to all of humanity, granting wide permission to enjoy what Allah has placed on Earth, provided it is both lawful and wholesome. This is especially relevant in contrast to the practices of the polytheists, who forbade themselves certain foods and drinks that Allah had in fact made available. By reminding humanity that Allah created these resources for their benefit, the verse establishes a foundational principle: the things Allah has made lawful are inherently good and meant for human well-being. The phrase “lawful and wholesome” carries two layers of meaning. “Lawful” points to what Allah permits through His guidance, distinguishing it from what is forbidden. “Wholesome” refers to that which healthy human nature finds pleasing and beneficial, free from harm to body or soul. Together, these words expose the futility of superstitious taboos and self-imposed restrictions, which often claimed to be religious but were, in truth, distortions. Alongside this permission comes a warning: do not follow the footsteps of Satan. The imagery of “footsteps” suggests a gradual process, where one step leads to another until one is far from safety. Just as travelers might follow footprints, assuming they lead to the right destination, so too do people risk blindly imitating practices without real guidance. But Satan’s path leads only to harm, never to truth. The verse closes with an emphatic reminder: “Indeed, he is your clear enemy.” The enmity of Satan toward humanity is well-known and firmly established. Following him means yielding to the evil thoughts he whispers, thoughts that arise like unseen influences—persistent and subtle. If a person accepts these whispers without resistance, they manifest as harmful actions. But if one restrains them with intellect, determination, and the support of divine guidance, they can overcome them. Allah has endowed human beings with the faculties of reason and strength, and He has perfected this with revelation, so that Satan’s whispers cannot confuse them into seeing evil as good. This verse, therefore, combines permission with caution: humanity is invited to enjoy what Allah has provided, but only by holding fast to His guidance and not being misled by their most ancient enemy.

Verse 169

Reflection: This verse continues from the previous one, clarifying the reality of Satan’s hostility. The words “he only commands you to evil and indecency” expand on the earlier warning, “Indeed, he is your clear enemy.” Satan’s enmity is shown in his constant attempt to lead people into what harms them, both outwardly and inwardly. The verse then adds a separate, more serious warning: Satan urges people “to say about Allah what you do not know.” This points to a specific danger—fabricating claims about religion without knowledge. In the time of the polytheists, this took the form of inventing rituals or declaring certain foods forbidden when Allah had not forbidden them. By including this phrase separately, the verse emphasizes that making false statements about Allah is among the gravest of sins. It includes associating partners with Him and inventing rules in His name without revelation. Thus, the verse exposes the essence of Satan’s enmity: his influence is not only about leading into sin, but also about corrupting the very relationship between humanity and Allah by planting lies in the place of divine truth. He does not issue commands in a voice people hear directly, but rather whispers into hearts and minds, making wrongdoing seem appealing and falsehood sound convincing. If left unchecked, this influence can lead people to claim things about Allah that have no basis in truth. This misrepresentation of Allah’s guidance is portrayed as especially dangerous, because it undermines the foundation of faith itself.

Verse 170

وَإِذَا قِيلَ لَهُمُ اتَّبِعُوا مَا أَنزَلَ اللَّهُ قَالُوا بَلْ نَتَّبِعُ مَا أَلْفَيْنَا عَلَيْهِ آبَاءَنَا ۗ أَوَلَوْ كَانَ آبَاؤُهُمْ لَا يَعْقِلُونَ شَيْئًا وَلَا يَهْتَدُونَ
Translation: And when they are told, “Follow what Allah has revealed,” they reply, “Instead, we follow (only) what we found our forefathers practicing.” Even if their forefathers had no understanding at all and were without guidance?
Reflection: This verse illustrates a familiar human tendency: clinging to inherited traditions while resisting divine guidance. When called to follow what Allah has revealed through His Messenger, some people respond by insisting that they will continue with the customs of their ancestors. Their loyalty to inherited practices outweighs their willingness to consider the truth revealed by Allah. The phrase, “Follow what Allah has revealed,” refers to the commands and guidance delivered through revelation. The answer, “Instead, we follow (only) what we found our forefathers practicing,” exposes a mindset of blind imitation—accepting customs simply because they are passed down, regardless of whether they are true or false. The verse then challenges this position with a piercing rhetorical question: would they still follow their ancestors’ ways if those ancestors had no real understanding and lacked true guidance? This exposes the weakness of their argument. Tradition alone is not a sufficient foundation for faith or practice; what matters is whether it rests upon knowledge and divine guidance. The message is clear: faith requires reflection, sincerity, and reliance on Allah’s revealed truth, not unquestioned adherence to inherited customs.
